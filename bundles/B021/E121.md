E121｜災害支援の配分：原則に例外を加えると主張はどう変わるか

P3 / LT02 / D10 exception→qualification / RQ10 / TF13



今日の狙い

原則を否定せず、例外・条件によって適用範囲を限定する論理を読む。unless / except / only when / does not mean などが、主張の強さをどう調整するかを捉える。



時間配分

Qualification Guide 6分 / Reading 19分 / Questions 13分 / Qualification Map 8分 / Short Output 6分 / Self-check 3分 / Post-solve 5分 ＝ Core 60分



Qualification Guide

1. まず一般原則を取る

2. 例外が原則を「否定」するのか「範囲を限定」するのか判断する

3. always / never に言い換えない

4. exceptionが入った後の最終主張を一文で言い直す



Reading

[1] Emergency relief systems need rules that allow limited resources to be distributed quickly. A simple rule such as “send more supplies to the area with more people” can be attractive because it is easy to explain and apply. When information is incomplete, transparent rules can also reduce the risk that decisions depend only on political influence or public attention.



[2] Population, however, cannot be the only factor. Two communities of similar size may face very different levels of isolation, damage, or access to local supplies. A coastal town with an operating road network may be able to receive food from nearby areas, while a mountain community of the same size may be cut off completely.



[3] This does not mean that population is irrelevant. It means that a population-based rule needs qualification. Larger populations generally require more total resources, unless another factor substantially changes the severity of need or the ability of residents to obtain help elsewhere.



[4] The same issue appears in decisions about delivery speed. It can be reasonable to prioritize the place where delay will create the greatest immediate harm. Yet this principle should not be interpreted as “always send everything to the most urgent location.” If doing so would leave several other communities without minimum supplies, a mixed allocation may reduce overall harm more effectively.



[5] Exceptions also need limits. A rule that allows any decision maker to declare a special case without evidence can become less transparent than having no rule at all. For an exception to be useful, the reason should be observable—for example, loss of transport access, failure of local storage, or a sudden rise in medically vulnerable residents—and the exception should be reviewed as conditions change.



[6] Good emergency allocation therefore combines general rules with justified qualifications. The rule creates consistency; the qualification allows the system to respond to conditions the rule cannot represent fully. Fairness does not require treating every case as unique, but neither does it require pretending that every relevant difference fits one formula.



Questions

Q3｜D10

Short Output

解答・解説・自己修正



Reading answers

Q1｜解答例

The general rule is that larger populations generally need more resources. It is limited when another factor significantly changes need or access to alternative help.



Q2｜解答例

Because urgency is one criterion, but concentrating everything in one place could leave other communities below a minimum level of support.



Q3｜解答例

Because unstructured exceptions allow decisions to become arbitrary and reduce the transparency that rules are supposed to provide.



Q4｜解答例

Emergency allocation is strongest when transparent general rules are combined with evidence-based qualifications for conditions the rule cannot capture.



D10｜exception→qualification

exceptionは原則を壊すものとは限らない。

general rule → exception condition → qualified rule

へ組み替える。

“X is useful unless Y” は “X is useless because Y exists” ではない。



Self-correction

例外を見つけた瞬間に筆者が原則を撤回したと読んでいないか確認する。最終段落のqualified ruleを書き直す。



到達目安

A：原則・例外・限定後の主張を三段階で保持できる。

B：例外は読めるが、原則との関係が曖昧になる。

C：例外＝反例＝原則の否定と処理する。
