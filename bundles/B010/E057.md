E057｜多言語の公共案内：英語を毎文訳さず日本語に切り替える
P2 / LT02 / A24 日英出力切替 / TF16


今日の狙い
英語を読みながら毎文日本語化するのではなく、まず英語で意味関係を保持し、必要なcontent unitsを確定してから日本語へ切り替える。


時間配分
Switching Guide 7分 / Reading 14分 / Content-unit Check 12分 / Questions 10分 / Output Switch 7分 / Self-check 4分 / Post-solve 6分 ＝ Core 60分


Switching Guide
Step 1：英文のsurface formではなく意味を取る
Step 2：誰が／何が／どうなる／条件は何か、のcontent unitsを保持する
Step 3：必要になった時点で日本語として自然な順序に再構成する
英語の語順をそのまま日本語へ移すことを目的にしない。


Reading
[1] A city wants emergency information, health notices, and public-service guides to be understandable to residents who use different languages. One possible response is to translate every message word for word. That approach can be useful, but it does not solve every problem. A sentence that is clear in one language may become long or unnatural in another, and technical terms may require explanation rather than direct substitution. The first task is therefore to understand what information must be preserved.


[2] Consider a notice about a temporary water-supply problem. The essential content might include where the problem is occurring, who is affected, what people should do, and when the next update will be given. These are content units. A translator who keeps these units in mind does not need to copy the English sentence structure. The order can change if the target language becomes clearer, as long as the relationships among the ideas remain accurate.


[3] This is especially important when a reader has to move between English input and Japanese output. Translating every sentence while reading can overload working memory and make it harder to see the whole message. A more efficient process is to understand a section in English, hold its key content units, and then reconstruct them in Japanese. The Japanese wording may be different from the English surface form, but the meaning and logical relations should stay the same.


[4] The goal is not to avoid translation completely. Some tasks require precise Japanese wording. The point is to delay that wording decision until the reader knows what the English means as a whole. This reduces the risk of producing a grammatically neat Japanese sentence that misrepresents the original message. Good output switching depends on stable comprehension first and language-specific expression second.
