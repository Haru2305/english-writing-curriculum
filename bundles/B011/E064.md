E064｜食品ロス対策：old→newで段落をつなぐ
P2 / LT03 / A24 Transfer / D08 情報構造 / TF10

E063のA13を使い、文ごとの情報配置が段落全体の流れをどう作るかを見る。そのうえで英語の意味関係を保ったまま日本語へ切り替える。


時間配分

Flow Guide
段落では、前文のnew informationが次文のold informationになることで流れができる。
A → B
B → C
C → D
この「受け渡し」が切れると、各文は正しくても段落が読みにくくなる。


Reading
[1] Many campaigns against food waste begin by telling consumers to buy less. That advice can help, but household decisions are only one part of the system. Waste can also occur when stores order more than they can sell, when appearance standards exclude edible products, or when package sizes do not match household needs. A useful policy therefore has to identify where waste is created before deciding who should change behavior.


[2] One common source of waste is uncertainty about demand. Retailers do not know exactly how much customers will buy on a given day, so they often keep extra stock to avoid empty shelves. That extra stock reduces the risk of lost sales, but it also increases the amount that may remain unsold. The same inventory that protects availability can therefore create a disposal problem.


[3] This trade-off can be reduced in several ways. Better demand forecasting can narrow the gap between expected and actual sales. Smaller delivery batches can then allow stores to adjust more frequently. More frequent adjustment, however, may raise transport or labor costs. Those costs must be compared with the value of reducing unsold food.


[4] The broader point is that food waste is not a single bad choice made by one actor. It can emerge from linked decisions across households, retailers, suppliers, and logistics systems. Policies become more precise when they follow those links and ask how a change in one part of the chain shifts costs and risks elsewhere.


[5] The same principle applies to household behavior. A consumer may be willing to waste less food but still face package sizes that are too large or date labels that are difficult to interpret. Those design features shape the choices available at home. Changes aimed only at consumer motivation can therefore miss part of the problem. Once the chain is viewed as a sequence of linked decisions, a policy can ask whether information, packaging, ordering, or distribution should change first and which change would simply move costs to another part of the system.
Information Flow
Paragraph 3の各文について、「前文から受け取るold information」と「新しく加えるnew information」を短く書きなさい。

Information Flow｜例
better forecasting → narrows the gap
smaller batches → become possible as a response
more frequent adjustment → introduces a new cost
those costs → becomes old information and is evaluated against waste reduction


Reading answers
Q1
“Those costs” refers back to transport or labor costs introduced immediately before it. The sentence begins with known information and then adds the new comparison with the value of reducing waste.


Q2｜A
Paragraph 2はdemand uncertainty→extra stock→unsold foodというmechanismを説明する。


Q3｜A
availabilityを守る在庫が同時にwaste riskを上げるというtrade-off。


D08｜情報構造
D08では一文だけでなく、段落内で情報がどう受け渡されるかを見る。
new information → next sentence’s old information
この連鎖がcohesionを作る。


A24 Transfer
E057で学んだ「英語理解→content units→日本語表現」を別題材で自力使用する。語順対応ではなく、trade-offの関係を保つ。


Japanese Output｜例
商品を切らさないための余分な在庫は、売れ残りを増やす原因にもなりうる。この両立しにくい問題は、需要予測や配送方法を工夫することで軽減できる。


Self-correction
自分の日本語に、①availabilityを守る ②disposal problemが生じる ③trade-offは軽減可能、の関係が残っているか確認する。
