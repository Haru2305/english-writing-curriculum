Passage
A fictional regional bus system uses a “service recovery margin.” The margin is the extra time built into selected parts of a timetable so that a small delay does not automatically spread through the rest of the route.
The idea sounds inefficient because scheduled travel time becomes slightly longer. However, a timetable with no spare time can be fragile. If traffic adds five minutes early in the route, every later stop may remain five minutes behind schedule. [A] A small margin at a transfer point can allow the bus to recover some of that delay before the next tightly coordinated segment begins.
The useful margin depends on conditions. Too little spare time provides almost no protection. Too much creates unnecessarily slow schedules and may reduce the number of trips that can be operated with the same vehicles. The goal is therefore not to maximize spare time but to place enough of it where delays are most likely to propagate.
Dense Sentence
What makes recovery time operationally valuable is not its mere presence in a schedule but its placement at points where a modest delay can otherwise interfere with later transfers, driver changes, or vehicle assignments whose disruption would affect several subsequent services.
Candidate Sentence
This buffer converts a small amount of scheduled inefficiency into protection against delay propagation.
FAQ
Q: Is recovery time added at every stop?
A: No. It is concentrated at selected locations.
Q: Does a bus always wait through the full recovery period?
A: No. If it arrives on time, departure rules may allow only the scheduled dwell time.
Q: Can extra margin eliminate major disruptions?
A: No. It is mainly intended to absorb small or moderate delays.

解答・解説・自己修正
Q1｜解答例
A service recovery margin is extra scheduled time placed at selected route points so that small or moderate delays can be absorbed before they spread to later services and connections.
Q2｜解答
A. The previous sentences explain how delay propagates when there is no spare time, and the Candidate Sentence names the protective role of a buffer before the next sentence gives a transfer-point example.
Q3｜解答例
Recovery time is useful because of where it is placed, not simply because it exists. Some locations connect later transfers, driver changes, or vehicle assignments. A delay at those points can disrupt several later services, so spare time there has greater value.
Q4｜解答例
Segment C, because it has the largest average early delay, affects the most later connections, and has only one minute of recovery margin.
Q5｜解答例
Segment D may have more recovery time than its observed delay and connection exposure appear to require, but the table alone does not show whether the extra time serves other reliability needs.
Model summary
A service recovery margin is extra timetable time used to prevent small delays from spreading through later bus services. Its value depends on placement, especially near important connections, while excessive margin can slow schedules and reduce operating efficiency. Segment C appears vulnerable because it combines the largest average delay and the most affected connections with only one minute of recovery time. The margin cannot eliminate major disruptions.
Model writing
The operator should place recovery time where small delays most often threaten multiple later connections. It should track average delay together with the number of transfers, driver changes, or vehicle assignments affected, rather than adding equal spare time everywhere. Segment C would be a priority for review, while Segment D may warrant testing whether some margin can be reduced. However, the table covers only average delay and connection counts, not severe-event frequency or passenger volumes. Any change should therefore be tested for both reliability improvement and added schedule time. That test should include both normal and disrupted days.
