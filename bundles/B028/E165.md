Passage A
A university considers automatically recording and publishing every lecture. Supporters argue that universal recording gives students a reliable backup when illness, timetable conflicts, or difficult material prevents full understanding in class. It also avoids uncertainty about which courses will provide recordings.
The challenge is that automatic publication may change behavior. Some students may postpone difficult material because they know a recording exists. Teachers may also feel pressure to review every recording before release, especially when discussions include incomplete ideas, student questions, or examples intended only for the classroom context.
Passage B
A second proposal allows instructors to publish recordings selectively. Courses with large lectures, complex demonstrations, or known accessibility needs can provide recordings, while discussion-heavy classes can choose summaries or short review videos instead.
This approach gives instructors more control and may fit different teaching formats better. However, availability becomes less predictable for students, and similar courses may adopt inconsistent rules. Selective systems can also create more administrative work if every course must justify its recording policy.

Q2｜解答例

Model English summary
Both proposals aim to improve access to course content, but they differ in consistency and adaptability. Universal recording gives students predictable access and a reliable backup, though it may affect study habits and create review burdens for instructors. Selective recording can better match different course formats, especially discussion-based classes, but availability becomes less predictable and policy decisions may create administrative work. Overall, the trade-off is standardized access versus course-specific flexibility, and neither option removes every cost.
Model evaluation
The university should guarantee some form of review access in every course but allow the format to vary. Lecture-based classes could publish full recordings automatically, while discussion-heavy classes could provide shorter review materials or edited recordings. This would support accessibility without forcing one format onto every teaching style. Instructors could also receive short guidance on turning discussions into edited review materials. However, the university should publish clear course-level rules before registration so students know what to expect. The policy should therefore balance accessibility, instructional fit, and administrative simplicity rather than treating recording itself as the only measure of access.
## 解説の考え方

### この問題で考えること
「録画をする / しない」ではなく、**accessibilityを保証しつつ、course typeに合うreview accessをどう設計するか**を判断する。

### 考え方
universal recording はpredictabilityに強いが、discussion context・teacher review burden・student behaviorへの影響がある。selective recording はinstructional fitに強いが、availabilityが不統一になる。many discussion-based classes という条件から、**review accessは全courseで保証し、formatはcourse typeで変える**というhybrid designが自然に出る。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× 「accessibilityのため全授業を録画」  
× 「教員裁量のため完全選択制」  
○ 「目的はrecording自体でなくreview accessなので、full recording / edited recording / summaryを授業形式で使い分ける」

### 模範解答の読み方
Model evaluation は Position（review accessの保証＋format可変）→ lecture/discussionでの実装 → accessibilityとinstructional fitのmechanism → advance ruleというQualification → 3基準へのReturn、の順。二案の中間を取るのではなく、**制度の目的を保って手段を分解**している。

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
制度比較でA/Bの欠点が補完的なら、**共通目的を固定し、手段だけ条件別に分ける**設計を考える。
