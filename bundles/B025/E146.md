E146｜夜間照明と生態系：高密度科学文と表を統合する
P3 / LT03 / A29 Review / RQ13 / RF04 / RF03 / SS03 / TF08
今日の狙い
高密度な科学説明を構文処理し、本文と表の対応を使って自然な日本語説明へ変換する。
時間配分
Structure Plan 4分 / Reading 16分 / Dense Sentence 12分 / Table Questions 10分 / Japanese Output 10分 / Self-check 3分 / Post-solve 5分 ＝ Core 60分
Reading
[1] Outdoor lighting allows people to use roads and public spaces after dark, but the ecological effect of light depends on more than brightness alone. Different wavelengths can influence animal behavior differently, and the timing, direction, and duration of exposure can matter as much as the measured intensity at a single point.
[2] Researchers studying a fictional riverside park compared three lighting designs. Design A used bright broad-spectrum lamps throughout the night. Design B used lower-intensity lamps with shielding that directed light toward paths. Design C used similar shielding but reduced output after midnight when pedestrian use was low.
Dense Sentence
What complicates attempts to identify a single “safe” lighting level is that an intensity which produces little measurable change in one species under one set of conditions may alter feeding, movement, or attraction behavior in another species whose sensitivity differs in wavelength, time of activity, or dependence on darkness.
[3] The researchers therefore measured several outcomes instead of searching for one universal threshold. They counted insects near lamps, recorded bat activity along the river, and measured how much light reached vegetation outside the path area. The goal was not to prove that one lamp was harmless, but to compare how different designs changed exposure.
Table
Design | Insects near lamps | Bat activity | Spill light beyond path
A | High | Low | High
B | Medium | Medium | Low
C | Low after midnight | Higher after midnight | Low
[4] The table does not show that Design C is always preferable. A park with heavy late-night use may require more light for human activity, while another location may contain species with different responses. Still, the comparison suggests that direction and timing can reduce ecological exposure without simply turning all lighting off.
[5] This matters for policy because “less light” is an incomplete instruction. A lower-intensity lamp aimed poorly may illuminate more habitat than a somewhat brighter lamp that is carefully shielded. Likewise, reducing output during low-use hours may change exposure more than a small reduction maintained all night.
Questions
Q1｜A29
Break the Dense Sentence into 3–4 simpler English statements.
Q2｜RF04

Q3｜RQ13

Q4｜RQ09

解答・解説・自己修正
Q1｜解答例
It is difficult to identify one universally safe lighting level. One intensity may produce little change in one species. The same intensity may alter another species’ behavior. Species differ in wavelength sensitivity, active time, and dependence on darkness.
Q2｜和訳例
単一の「安全な」照明レベルを特定しにくいのは、ある条件下では一つの種にほとんど変化を生じさせない明るさでも、波長への感受性や活動時間、暗闇への依存度が異なる別の種では、採餌・移動・光への誘引行動を変える可能性があるためである。
Q3｜解答例
Design C used reduced output after midnight and showed fewer insects near lamps and higher bat activity after midnight than designs with stronger continuous lighting.
Q4｜解答例
Brightness alone is insufficient because exposure also depends on wavelength, direction, timing, duration, and the sensitivity of the organisms present.
A29 × RQ13
高密度文をほどいた後、表の列を「本文のどの主張を検証する情報か」に戻す。
Model Japanese output
夜間照明の影響は、明るさだけでなく波長、照射方向、時間帯、生物種ごとの感受性で変わる。表でも、光を遮へいして深夜に出力を下げた設計では、ランプ周辺の昆虫やコウモリの活動が他の設計と異なっていた。単一の明るさだけでは生態系への曝露を十分に評価できない。
## 解説の考え方

### この問題で考えること
単一の brightness を「安全性そのもの」と扱わず、**exposure を決める複数条件と生物側の感受性**を分ける。

### 考え方
本文は wavelength / direction / timing / duration / species sensitivity を挙げ、Table はそのうち direction と timing を変えた設計で反応がどう違うかを示す。したがって表は「Design C が普遍的に最善」と証明するものではなく、**brightness alone では説明不足**という本文の主張を具体化する evidence である。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× 「Cでbat activityが高い → Cが常に安全」  
○ 「Cの比較は timing と shielding が exposure を変え得ることを示すが、late-night human use や species differences で結論は変わる」  
改善点は、**specific comparison から universal policy へ飛ばない**こと。

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
環境・安全の指標では、一つの強度だけでなく、**when / where / who is exposed / for how long** に分解する。
