Passage A
A company is considering a “core-hours” system. Employees may choose when to begin and end work as long as they are available between 11:00 and 15:00. Supporters argue that this gives workers flexibility for commuting, family responsibilities, or periods of higher concentration while preserving a predictable period for meetings.
The system, however, can create hidden differences between teams. Employees whose tasks require customer contact may have less freedom than workers doing independent analysis. Managers may also schedule most meetings inside the core period, turning four nominally shared hours into a tightly packed block.
Passage B
Another company is testing “compressed scheduling.” Employees work the same total weekly hours over four longer days instead of five standard days. Supporters value the predictable full day away from work and the reduction in commuting days.
The trade-off is daily intensity. Longer workdays may be difficult for employees with caregiving duties or jobs requiring sustained interaction. The system can also reduce overlap with clients or partner organizations that operate on a five-day schedule. Unlike core hours, compressed scheduling offers less day-to-day flexibility but a larger block of predictable personal time.

Q1｜解答例
Both systems try to increase employee control over working time without eliminating coordination needs.
Q2｜解答例
Core hours provide more flexibility within each day but require a shared meeting window. Compressed scheduling offers less daily flexibility but gives workers a predictable full day away from work.
Q3｜解答例
Job roles differ in dependence on customers, team coordination, and continuous coverage, so the same schedule can create different practical constraints.
Model English summary
Both systems aim to give employees more control over time while maintaining organizational coordination. Core hours allow workers to vary daily start and end times, though customer-facing roles may have less real flexibility and meetings can crowd the shared period. Compressed scheduling provides a predictable day off and fewer commuting days, but longer workdays may be demanding and can reduce overlap with outside organizations.
Model evaluation
A core-hours system would probably suit this company better because frequent team meetings require reliable overlap, while limited customer-facing work means many employees could still adjust their schedules around the shared period. It also preserves normal-length workdays, which may be easier to sustain than compressed scheduling. However, managers should limit unnecessary meetings during core hours; otherwise the nominal flexibility could disappear. The policy should therefore be judged by both actual schedule control and the quality of team coordination.
## 解説の考え方

### この問題で考えること
制度名で選ぶのでなく、task condition で指定された会社に対して **coordination と flexibility のどちらをどの程度必要とするか**を判断する。

### 考え方
比較は同じ軸を両方へ当てる。core hours は daily flexibility が高く shared overlap を確保しやすい。compressed scheduling は full-day predictability が高いが daily intensity と external overlap に弱点がある。頻繁な team meetings・limited customer-facing work という条件では、shared overlap が決定基準になりやすい。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× 各制度の長所短所を別々に列挙して最後に好みで選ぶ  
○ **同じ criterion → A/B比較 → task condition → qualification** の順で選ぶ

### 答案の組み立て
- **Position**: core-hours system
- **Reason**: team meetings に必要な overlap を保ちやすい
- **Mechanism**: shared period が coordination point になる一方、その外側は調整可能
- **Qualification**: meeting が core period を埋め尽くすと実質的flexibilityが消える
- **Return**: actual schedule control と coordination quality の両方で評価する

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
比較問題では「どちらが良いか」より、**誰にとって・どの目的で・どの条件なら**を先に決める。
