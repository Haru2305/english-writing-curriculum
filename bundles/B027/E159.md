E159｜大学の学習支援：一律支援と対象支援を比較して評価する

Passage A
A university is considering offering a short academic-skills workshop to all first-year students. The workshop would cover planning assignments, using feedback, and seeking help early. Supporters argue that universal access avoids the problem of deciding in advance who needs support. Students who appear confident at the start of term may still encounter difficulties later, and some students may avoid asking for help because they do not want to be identified as struggling.
The weakness is resource use. Many students may already possess the relevant skills, so staff time is spent on learners who receive little additional benefit. A universal workshop can also become too general if it must fit a wide range of needs.
Passage B
A second proposal is targeted support. Students are invited to additional sessions if early coursework, attendance, or self-reported difficulty suggests that they may benefit. This approach concentrates staff time where need appears greatest and allows sessions to address more specific problems.
However, targeted systems depend on the quality of the indicators used to identify students. Some learners who would benefit may not be selected, especially if they hide difficulty or perform adequately on early tasks before problems become visible. Being singled out can also discourage participation if the program feels remedial rather than developmental.

Q1｜解答例
Both proposals aim to help students develop effective academic habits and receive support before difficulties become severe.
Q2｜解答例
Universal support reaches everyone but spreads staff resources broadly. Targeted support concentrates resources on selected students but may miss people whose needs are not visible early.
Q3｜解答例
Because some students may perform adequately at first, hide difficulty, or develop problems only later in the term.
Q4｜解答例
Because universal access can reduce labeling and missed cases, but it may also use limited staff time on students who gain little from the program.
Model English summary
Both proposals aim to prevent academic difficulties, but they distribute support differently. A universal workshop reaches all first-year students and avoids missing hidden needs, though it may use staff time on students who need little help. Targeted support allows more intensive assistance for selected students, but its success depends on accurate identification and it may miss learners whose difficulties appear later. Each design therefore trades reach against precision: the trade-off is broad access versus concentrated support.
Model evaluation
The university should combine a brief universal workshop with targeted follow-up rather than choose only one model. A short common session would ensure that every student receives basic strategies and information about help, while limited staff time could then be concentrated on students showing clearer need. The university should also allow self-referral later in the term so that students whose difficulties emerge gradually are not excluded. That combination keeps staff costs manageable while limiting missed cases. However, this mixed model would still require careful monitoring to prevent targeted sessions from being seen as punishment or labeling.
## 解説の考え方

### この問題で考えること
universal / targeted のどちらが「公平」かをラベルで決めず、**reach / intensity / identification error / stigma / staff capacity** を共通軸で比較する。

### 考え方
universal support は hidden need を拾いやすいが、resourceを薄く広げる。targeted support はneedの高い人へ集中できるが、indicatorが不完全なら false negative が生じる。task condition は limited staff と late-emerging difficulty の両方を含むため、**brief universal + targeted follow-up + later self-referral** がそれぞれの弱点を補う。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× 「全員に同じ支援だから公平」  
× 「必要な人だけだから効率的」  
○ 「参加機会と支援強度を分け、取りこぼしとresource concentrationのtrade-offを処理する」

### 模範解答の読み方
Model evaluation は、混合制度をPositionに置き、universal sessionのreach、targeted follow-upのresource concentration、self-referralのmissed-case対策を順に配置している。最後のlabeling riskは制度を否定する反論ではなく、運用時に処理すべきQualificationである。
