Passage
A fictional city evaluates public cooling centers during periods of extreme heat. Officials distinguish between “nominal coverage” and “effective coverage.” Nominal coverage asks how many residents live within a stated distance of a center. Effective coverage asks how many can realistically use one when heat risk is highest.
The distinction matters because distance alone can overstate access. A center may be geographically close but close early, require a difficult uphill walk, or lack enough indoor capacity during peak demand. [A] In that case, the map says a resident is covered even though the service may not be practically usable.
Officials therefore began combining location data with opening hours, indoor capacity, and observed queues. They also examined whether bus routes remained frequent during the hottest part of the day. The purpose was not to replace geographic measures but to identify where nominal access and practical access diverged.
A large gap between the two measures does not always imply poor management. A small rural center may have low nominal coverage but serve nearly everyone who can realistically reach it. A dense urban center may have excellent nominal coverage but still face crowding. The useful question is why the gap exists and whether the limiting factor can be changed.
Candidate Sentence
This difference creates what planners call a coverage gap.
FAQ
Q: Does living within one kilometer guarantee admission?
A: No. Capacity limits and operating hours still apply.
Q: Are all centers open for the same period?
A: No. Hours depend on staffing and building availability.
Q: Is bus access included in nominal coverage?
A: No. Nominal coverage is based only on residential distance.

解答・解説・自己修正
Q1｜解答例
Effective coverage is the share of residents who can realistically use a cooling center when heat risk is highest, considering practical conditions such as hours, capacity, queues, and transport.
Q2｜解答
A. The previous sentences contrast geographic proximity with practical usability. The Candidate Sentence names that difference, and the following sentence gives an example of how the gap appears.
Q3｜解答例
Center C, because it has the largest nearby population but the smallest capacity, the longest queue, and the earliest closing time.
Q4｜解答例
Center B may provide more usable access than its smaller nearby population suggests because it has greater capacity, shorter queues, and later hours. The evidence does not establish access for every resident.
Q5｜解答例
Because geographic distance does not capture capacity, hours, queues, route difficulty, or transport, all of which can determine whether residents can actually use the service.
Model summary
The city distinguishes nominal coverage, based on distance, from effective coverage, which reflects whether residents can realistically use cooling centers. A coverage gap can appear when nearby sites have limited capacity, long queues, short hours, or weak transport access. Center C illustrates this problem because many residents live nearby despite severe practical constraints. However, the available data do not show individual travel ability or actual health outcomes.
Model writing
The city should evaluate cooling-center access with both geographic and practical measures. It can continue tracking the number of residents within one kilometer, but it should also monitor peak queue time, capacity use, and operating hours during the hottest periods. This would identify places where nominal coverage looks strong but actual access is limited, as Center C suggests. The analysis should also include public-transport frequency where possible. However, the current table does not show who actually attempted to use each center or whether vulnerable residents were served, so access conclusions remain incomplete.
