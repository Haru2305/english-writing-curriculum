Passage A

Machine translation can reduce routine language barriers. For travelers, customers, and workers handling predictable messages, speed often matters more than perfect wording. A system that produces an understandable draft in seconds may therefore be useful even when a human could create a more elegant sentence.



[A1]



This advantage is especially strong when the alternative is no translation at all. Small organizations cannot always hire specialists for every language. Automatic tools can help them publish basic notices, identify the topic of incoming messages, or prepare a first draft for review.



[A2]



However, the value of speed depends on the cost of error. A mistranslated restaurant menu may be inconvenient; a mistranslated medical instruction can be dangerous. The same system can therefore be acceptable in one setting and inappropriate in another.



Passage B

Debates about translation technology sometimes begin with the wrong question. Asking whether machines can “replace translators” treats translation as one uniform task. In reality, tasks differ in purpose, risk, audience, and need for cultural judgment.



[B1]



A professional translator may spend time choosing between two expressions that are both grammatically correct because one fits the relationship between writer and reader better. That decision is not simply about recovering dictionary meaning.



[B2]



This does not imply that every sentence requires human attention. A sensible workflow can automate low-risk, repetitive material while reserving human review for texts where ambiguity, tone, or consequences make errors costly. The important issue is task allocation rather than a single verdict about the technology.



Candidate Sentence X

The practical question is therefore not whether the tool is perfect, but whether its level of accuracy is sufficient for the purpose.



Candidate Sentence Y

Such choices become more important when the text must do more than transfer factual content.

Q1｜正解

A2



Sentence X summarizes the usefulness described before it and prepares the contrast with higher-risk settings. At A1, “therefore” arrives before the passage has developed enough evidence for the practical criterion.



Q2｜正解

B2



“Such choices” refers to the translator’s choice between acceptable expressions described immediately before. The sentence then generalizes why those choices matter.



Q3｜解答例

Both passages argue that translation technology should be judged by task and context rather than by a simple perfect/imperfect or human/machine contrast.



Q4｜解答例

Passage A emphasizes acceptable accuracy and risk, while Passage B emphasizes task diversity, tone, and the allocation of human judgment.



RF07 × RQ12

二本文では「この文が正しいか」だけでなく、「どちらの本文のどの議論段階に属するか」を判定する。



Model comparison

Sentence X belongs in Passage A because “therefore” summarizes the earlier case for speed and usefulness before the text turns to risk. Sentence Y belongs in Passage B because “Such choices” directly refers to a translator’s wording decisions. Both passages support task-sensitive use, but the local reference chains differ.
