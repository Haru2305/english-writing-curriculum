Passage A

A public library introduced an online reservation system for study rooms. The change was intended to reduce long lines at the service desk and make room availability visible before visitors traveled to the library.



[1] During the first month, total reservations increased, but unused reservations also rose. Some users booked several possible times and later attended only one. [Blank 1], a system designed to improve access sometimes made rooms appear unavailable even when nobody was using them.



[2] Staff first considered limiting every user to one reservation per week. [A] Interviews suggested, however, that some students genuinely needed several sessions during examination periods, while occasional users rarely booked more than once. [B] The library therefore tested a different rule: users could hold multiple reservations, but they had to confirm each booking two hours before use. [C] Unconfirmed bookings were automatically released. [D]



[3] The new rule reduced unused bookings without sharply reducing legitimate use. It also changed the information users saw: a room that became available after a cancellation returned to the system quickly instead of remaining blocked.



[4] The trial illustrates a broader design issue. Digital systems do not merely display demand; their rules can shape demand. A convenient booking button may encourage people to reserve “just in case,” while a small confirmation step can make users reconsider whether they still need the resource.



Passage B

Another library used a waiting list instead of confirmation. When all rooms were booked, users could join a queue and receive a message if a room opened. This approach worked well when cancellations were common, but it required users to respond quickly. People who could not check their phones frequently were less likely to benefit.



Candidate Sentence

This evidence made a uniform weekly limit look unnecessarily strict.

解答・解説・自己修正



Q1｜正解

b. As a result



Multiple speculative reservations caused rooms to appear unavailable. The second clause is a consequence, not an example or contrast.



Q2｜正解

B



The preceding interviews show that a uniform limit would punish some legitimate heavy users. “This evidence” refers to those findings, and the next sentence introduces the alternative rule adopted because of them.



Q3｜解答

The rules built into digital reservation systems.



Q4｜解答例

It reports the outcome of the revised rule and shows how it improved both actual room use and the information displayed to users.



Q5｜解答例

The confirmation system requires users to reconfirm bookings but quickly releases unused rooms; the waiting list reallocates cancellations efficiently but favors users who can respond quickly to messages.
