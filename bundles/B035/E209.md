Passage

For many years, scientists studying animal behavior focused on what individuals did in carefully controlled experiments. Such experiments were valuable because researchers could change one condition at a time. Yet they sometimes produced a narrow picture of behavior.

An animal that solves a problem in a laboratory may behave differently in a social group, where competitors, allies, and previous experience influence every choice. Researchers therefore began combining laboratory tasks with observations in more natural settings.

This change created a new difficulty. Natural behavior is richer but harder to interpret. If an animal changes its feeding location, the cause might be food quality, the presence of another animal, temperature, or memory of an earlier event. More realistic observation does not automatically produce simpler explanation.

Researchers responded by using several forms of evidence. A field observation can identify a pattern worth testing. A controlled experiment can then isolate one possible mechanism. Tracking data can show whether the pattern continues over longer periods.

The important shift is not from “bad laboratory research” to “good field research.” It is from treating one method as sufficient to asking which method can answer which part of a larger question.

This approach also changes the meaning of disagreement. If a laboratory study and a field study produce different results, researchers should not immediately decide that one must be wrong. The difference may reveal that a mechanism operates only under certain social or environmental conditions.

Modern animal-behavior research therefore often depends on moving back and forth between control and realism. The laboratory helps identify causes; the field shows whether those causes remain important in complex life.

Questions

1. 30字以内
なぜ自然環境での観察は行動を豊かに捉えられても説明を容易にするとは限らないのか。

2. 50字以内
研究者が複数の研究方法を組み合わせる理由を説明しなさい。

3. 60字以内
“one method as sufficient” という考え方から何が変化したのか説明しなさい。

4. 90字以内
実験室研究と野外研究の結果が異なる場合、その不一致をどのように解釈すべきだと本文は述べているか。
