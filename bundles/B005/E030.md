Text A｜Medication safety notice
A clinic learns that one batch of a common medication may have been stored incorrectly. No serious harm has been confirmed, but the clinic decides that patients using the affected batch should receive clear guidance. Staff notify patients whose records show the relevant batch number and inform them about how to check the package. The message conveys that the problem is being investigated and alerts patients to specific symptoms that would require urgent medical advice. It does not claim that every patient is in danger. The clinic also explains what remains uncertain and promises an update when testing is complete. It gives patients a telephone number for questions and explains that receiving the notice does not mean harm is expected. This separates formal notification from risk interpretation and from the action that should follow. The clinic also prepares translated versions and asks local pharmacies to display the same core instructions so that patients receive consistent information through more than one channel.


Text B｜Museum renovation
A city museum occupies a historic building that needs major structural repairs. One proposal would preserve the exterior while adapting several interior spaces for accessibility and climate control. Some residents argue that any major alteration would reduce the building's historical value, while others argue that the museum cannot remain open safely without change. The city allocates part of the budget to structural work and part to documenting features that will be modified. The final plan does not keep every detail unchanged, but it tries to preserve the elements considered most historically significant while allowing continued public use. Before approving the renovation, the city consults accessibility groups, historians, and local residents. Their priorities differ, so officials publish the criteria used to decide which features must remain and which can be altered. The consultation does not remove the trade-off, but it makes the reasoning behind the final design more visible.


Text C｜Research invitation
A university research team identifies adults who meet the eligibility criteria for a study of a new rehabilitation program. Researchers select potential participants from clinic records, but selection does not enroll anyone automatically. Each person receives information about the study, its possible benefits, burdens, alternatives, and the right to withdraw. Staff ask questions to check understanding rather than assuming that reading the form is enough. Eligible people can choose whether to join, and some may opt for standard rehabilitation instead. The researchers record consent only after the person has had an opportunity to decide voluntarily. They also ask participants to describe the main procedures in their own words so that misunderstanding can be corrected. If important new information emerges later, participants are informed again and can reconsider their decision without penalty. The team avoids asking the treating clinician to pressure hesitant patients, because a voluntary choice can be affected by authority even when no explicit threat is made.

## Writing解説

Short Writing
モデル答案
A clinic can notify patients about a possible medication problem, but simply sending a message does not guarantee a safe response. The notice should communicate what is known and convey which symptoms require action without exaggerating uncertain risk. Patients also need information they can understand so that they can choose what to do next with appropriate medical guidance.


別解
Researchers may select people who meet eligibility criteria, but those individuals must still choose whether to participate. Staff should inform them about risks, burdens, and alternatives in understandable language. A signed form alone does not show that the person understood the study or made a voluntary choice.
