Reading
As a population ages, demand for health and social care often rises. This seems straightforward, but the relationship is more complex than simply counting the number of older people. People of the same age can differ greatly in health, independence, family support, and the type of care they need.


One important factor is the number of people living with chronic conditions. If more people need regular assistance over a long period, the total amount of care required may rise even if the population grows slowly. At the same time, better prevention, safer housing, and support for independent living may reduce the intensity of care that some people need. The type of demand matters too. A community may need more home visits, rehabilitation, transport assistance, or help with daily activities rather than simply more hospital beds. These services require different kinds of workers and different schedules. As a result, two areas with similar numbers of older residents can face very different planning problems even before total demand changes.


The supply of care can also change. Family members provide a large share of support in many communities, but smaller households or longer working hours may reduce the time available for unpaid care. Formal services then face greater pressure. Raising wages may help organizations recruit workers, but it can also raise the cost of providing care. Training more workers increases capacity only after enough people complete the training and remain in the sector. Technology may change this balance, but it does not remove the need for workers. Digital monitoring can make some follow-up more efficient, while home modifications can help some older adults remain independent for longer. These measures may reduce certain kinds of demand, yet they can also create new needs for technical support, coordination, and staff training. The effect of an aging population therefore depends partly on how the care system changes in response.


Regional differences matter as well. A densely populated city may differ from a rural area in travel time, workforce availability, and the location of services. Two regions with a similar proportion of older residents may therefore differ in how easily care can be delivered. Planning based only on age statistics may therefore miss these important differences.


For policy makers, the key question is not simply whether demand will rise. They also need to ask what kind of demand will rise, how quickly it may change, and whether families, communities, and formal services can respond. Population aging can increase pressure on care systems, but the size of that pressure depends on health, living arrangements, workforce conditions, and service design.

### この問題で考えること
「高齢者が増えるからcareが増える」という一段の説明ではなく、**人口構成の変化が、どの種類・強度の需要を生み、供給側がそれに対応できるか**まで分けて考えます。

### 考え方
population aging  
→ chronic conditions / independence / household supportの構成が変わる  
→ home care, rehabilitation, transport supportなど必要なservice mixが変わる  
→ 必要なworker・schedule・capacityも変わる

したがって、同じ高齢者数でも
family support
workforce availability
travel time
service location
が違えば、同じpolicyにはなりません。

ここでのポイントは、**需要量だけでなく需要の種類と供給能力を同時に見ること**です。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× More older people mean more hospital beds are needed.  
○ Aging may increase demand, but the required response depends on the type of care needed and the local capacity to provide it.

後者は「人数 → 一つの設備」と短絡せず、demand compositionとcapacityを挟んでいます。

### 答案の組み立て
- **Answer**: similar age structures can require different care policies
- **Reason**: local conditions differ
- **Mechanism**: those conditions change how care can be delivered
- **Support**: travel time / workforce / family support
- **Return**: the same demographic statistic does not imply the same policy priority

### 模範解答の読み方
1文目で具体的な相違点を置き、2文目でそれがcare systemへのpressureの違いにつながると示しています。  
つまり differ from / differ in は語法練習であると同時に、**比較対象と判断に効く相違点を分ける道具**になっています。

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
人口統計をpolicyへつなぐときは、  
**population statistic → demand type/intensity → supply/capacity → local delivery constraints**  
の順に考えると、数字だけから結論へ飛びにくくなります。
