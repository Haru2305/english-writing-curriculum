Reading
Online education can widen learning opportunities because it changes when and where students can study. A learner who lives far from a school may be able to attend a class without traveling, and a student with work or family responsibilities may benefit from flexible scheduling. Recorded lessons can also allow students to review difficult material more than once.


However, simply putting lessons online does not guarantee equal participation. Students need a suitable device, a stable connection, and enough digital literacy to use the platform. They may also need a quiet place to study. If these conditions are missing, online courses can be technically available while remaining difficult to use.


Course design matters as well. Long video lectures may be convenient for teachers to upload, but they can be hard for students to follow. Some educators therefore suggest combining short explanations with quizzes, discussion, and opportunities to ask questions. Others suggest that schools provide both live and asynchronous options so that students can choose a format that fits their circumstances. Feedback is another important condition. A student who watches recorded material may still need a clear way to ask for help and learn whether an answer is correct. Without timely feedback, flexibility can turn into isolation. For that reason, an accessible course must consider not only how content is delivered but also how learners remain connected to teachers and classmates.


This does not mean that every course should move online. Laboratory work, practical training, and some forms of discussion may depend on physical presence. A better goal is to identify which barriers online tools can remove and which barriers require other forms of support. Expanding access is not only a matter of offering more content; it also requires making participation realistically possible.

解説補強｜考え方を再利用する

### この問題で考えること
この問題では「online educationを増やす」こと自体ではなく、**どの参加障壁を、誰が、どんな具体策で下げるか**を考えます。access拡大とlearning qualityを同じものとして扱わないことも重要です。

### 考え方
提案は、
barrier
→ action
→ actor
→ participationがどう変わるか
の順で具体化すると強くなります。

例：
fixed-time participation is difficult  
→ recorded lessonsを用意する  
→ school / teacherが提供する  
→ students with work or family responsibilities can study at another time

suggest doing と suggest that S V の違いは、単なる文法差ではありません。**行為者を明示する必要があるか**という論理上の違いにもつながっています。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× Schools should use more technology.  
○ I suggest that schools lend devices to students who cannot afford them. This may reduce a concrete barrier to participation.

後者は「何をするか」「誰がするか」「どのbarrierに効くか」が追えます。

### 模範解答の読み方
モデル答案1文目はproposalです。  
2文目は、そのproposalが有効な理由を **cannot join at a fixed time → recorded lessons help** というMechanismで補っています。  
提案文だけで終わらず、「その策がなぜaccessにつながるか」を一文足すことでargumentになります。

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
提案問題では、**抽象的な理想ではなく、barrierを一つ特定し、それを下げるactionとactorを書く**と再利用しやすくなります。
