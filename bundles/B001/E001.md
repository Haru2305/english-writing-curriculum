Reading
When people discuss regional gaps in health care, they often begin by comparing the number of doctors in different places. Staffing matters, but it is only one part of the problem. Rural communities can differ from large cities in several ways that affect access to care.


First, distance changes what “available” care means. A clinic may exist within a region, yet patients may still need to travel a long way to reach it. This matters especially when public transport is limited or when a patient must make repeated visits. Second, the range of services can differ. A small local clinic may provide primary care but lack equipment or specialists for more complex treatment. In that case, patients still depend on larger hospitals elsewhere.


Infrastructure also shapes access. Reliable roads, communication systems, and internet connections can make it easier to arrange appointments, share information, or use remote services. However, improving infrastructure does not automatically solve every staffing problem. A hospital may attract new professionals for a short period but struggle to retain them if working conditions, training opportunities, or support systems are weak.


For this reason, regional access should not be treated as a simple question of whether one area has more doctors than another. Two regions may differ in population density, transport, equipment, and the types of care that residents need. Policies that focus on only one of these factors may improve part of the system while leaving other barriers unchanged. A better approach begins by asking how the regions differ from each other and which differences actually prevent people from receiving appropriate care.

G13｜前置詞の見方
前置詞は、日本語の「〜から」「〜で」だけを当てはめて選ぶのではなく、動詞が後ろの名詞とどんな関係を作るかで考えます。
differ from は「比較対象との距離」
differ in は「違いが現れる領域」
という区別です。
前置詞まで含めて differ from / differ in の塊で取り出せるようにしてください。
Why this word?
本文の
Two regions may differ in population density, transport, equipment, and the types of care that residents need.
では、regions 同士をもう一度比べることより、「どの点で違うか」を列挙したいため in が自然です。

### この問題で考えること
この問題の核心は「地方は医師が少ないか」ではなく、**人が必要な医療へ実際に到達できるかを、複数の障壁に分けて考えること**です。医師数は重要でも、accessを決める一要因にすぎません。

### 考え方
accessを「サービスが存在する」だけで終わらせず、
**staffing → reachability → available services/equipment → continuity**
のように分けます。

医師を増やす
→ staffingの制約は弱くなる
→ しかし交通、専門診療、設備、人材定着の制約はそのまま残り得る
→ したがって「医師を増やせば地域格差が解消する」とは言えない

ここで重要なのは、一つの対策が**どの障壁を動かし、どの障壁を動かさないか**を追うことです。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× Rural areas have fewer doctors, so they only need more doctors.  
○ More doctors may improve staffing, but patients may still face long travel times or limited specialist services.

改善点は、単一原因で終わらず、Reasonの後に「なぜそれだけでは足りないか」というMechanismを置いていることです。

### 答案の組み立て
- **Answer**: increasing the number of doctors alone may not remove regional gaps
- **Reason**: access depends on several barriers
- **Mechanism**: staffing改善だけではtransportやservice rangeは変わらない
- **Support**: long travel / limited specialist services
- **Return**: one policy can improve part of access without solving the whole problem

### 模範解答の読み方
1文目は rural areas と large cities の違いを具体化して、**どこに障壁があるか**を示しています。  
2文目は「医師数増加でstaffingは改善しても、他のbarrierは残る」とつなぎ、Reason → Mechanism → Conclusionを完成させています。  
Therefore は、単なる結論マーカーではなく、前文で示した複数障壁から「医師数だけでは不十分」という判断へ戻す役割です。

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
サービスへのaccessを考えるときは、**存在するか／到達できるか／必要な内容が使えるか／継続して提供できるか**を分けます。  
一つの対策を評価するときは、「何を改善するか」と同時に「何が残るか」を確認します。
