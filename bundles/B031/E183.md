E183｜一度の大規模調査か、小規模な反復観察か：二つの調査設計を比較する

P3 / LT03 / RQ12 / D06 / RF05 / WF02 / WT03 / SS02 / TF05

今日の狙い
二つの調査設計を同じ評価軸で比較し、要約からトレードオフ作文へつなげる。

時間配分
Silent Plan 1分 / Passage A 11分 / Passage B 10分 / Questions 10分 / English Summary 10分 / Trade-off Writing 11分 / Self-check 2分 ＝ 演習 55分 / Post-solve 3分（時間外）

Passage A
A research group wants to understand how students use a newly renovated library. One proposal is to conduct a single large survey near the end of the semester. Thousands of students could answer the same questions about visit frequency, preferred spaces, noise, and opening hours.

The main advantage is breadth. A large sample can include students from many departments and years, allowing subgroup comparisons. The survey is also relatively easy to analyze because everyone receives the same questions at the same time. However, it depends heavily on memory. Students must summarize weeks of behavior in one response, and the final week may influence what they remember about the rest of the semester.

Passage B
A second group proposes repeated small observations. A few hundred students would receive a one-minute questionnaire every two weeks, and researchers would also count use of selected spaces at fixed times.

This design produces fewer responses at each moment, but it captures change over time. Researchers can see whether quiet rooms become more popular before examinations or whether reported crowding varies by week. The cost is greater complexity: repeated contact may reduce participation, and the same limited observation times may miss students who use the library at other hours.

Questions
Q1｜RQ12
What research goal is shared by both designs?

Q2｜D06
Compare the two designs on breadth and time sensitivity.

Q3｜RQ09
Why can a large sample still produce a distorted picture of the semester?

Q4｜RQ12
Name a limitation that is specific to repeated observation rather than the large one-time survey.

English Summary
Summarize both passages in 70–90 English words. Use at least two common comparison axes.

Trade-off Writing
In 90–110 English words, choose a design for a university that wants to revise next year’s library schedule. Explain two advantages of your choice and one limitation. You may also recommend combining the designs.

Self-check
□ I compared the same axes across both passages.
□ I did not treat sample size as the only criterion.
□ My recommendation includes a trade-off.

Q1｜解答例
Both designs aim to understand how students use the renovated library so the university can make better decisions about spaces and schedules.

Q2｜解答例
The large survey has greater breadth because it can reach thousands of students across many groups, but it is weak at tracking changes during the semester. Repeated observation has less breadth at each time point but greater sensitivity to change over time.

Q3｜解答例
Because students report weeks of behavior from memory at the end of the semester, recent experiences may dominate their answers.

Q4｜解答例
Either one is acceptable: repeated contact may reduce participation, or the fixed observation times may miss students who use the library at other hours.

Model summary
Both proposals investigate library use but emphasize different strengths. A single large survey offers broad coverage and easy subgroup comparison, yet it relies on students’ memory of the semester. Repeated small surveys and observations capture changes over time and can reveal period-specific crowding, but they require more coordination and may miss users outside fixed observation times. The main trade-off is broad one-time coverage versus more detailed measurement of change that can guide scheduling decisions.

Model writing
I would combine repeated observation with a smaller end-of-semester survey. Repeated measurements would show when demand changes, which is important for revising opening hours, while direct counts could check whether reported crowding matches actual use at selected times. A final survey could then add broader coverage across departments and identify groups missed by repeated samples. The main limitation is complexity: collecting several rounds of data requires more staff effort and may lower participation over time. The university should therefore keep each repeated questionnaire very short. This mixed design would balance timing information with broader coverage.

## 解説の考え方

### この問題で考えること
調査設計は「大規模＝良い」で選ばない。**次年度のscheduleを直すために、どんな情報が必要か**から評価軸を選ぶ。

### 考え方
一回の大規模調査は breadth / subgroup comparison に強いが、semester 内の変化を記憶に頼る。反復観察は timing / dynamics に強いが、各時点の coverage と運用負担に限界がある。ここでは schedule revision には「いつ需要が変わるか」が重要なので repeated observation の価値が高い。ただし広い coverage も必要なら final survey を組み合わせる余地がある。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× 「回答者が多いから一回調査」  
○ 「schedule変更には時間変化が必要なので反復観察を重視し、breadth不足は別の調査で補う」  
改善点は、**評価基準を調査目的から逆算している**こと。

### 答案の組み立て
- **Position**: repeated observation + smaller final survey
- **Reason**: timing information と broad coverage を分担できる
- **Mechanism**: 反復で需要変化を捉え、最終調査で未捕捉集団を補う
- **Support**: exam period / fixed-time counts / department coverage
- **Qualification**: repeated contact と staff effort
- **Return**: repeated questionnaire を短くして負担を抑える

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
調査設計では sample size だけでなく、**breadth / timing / recall bias / coverage / burden**を目的に応じて比較する。
