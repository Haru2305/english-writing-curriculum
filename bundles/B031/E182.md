E182｜測定値の桁数と信頼性：精密そうに見える数字をどう評価するか

P3 / LT03 / RQ06 / RQ10 / D05 / A29 / RF03 / SS01 / TF08

今日の狙い
測定値の細かさと測定の信頼性を分け、譲歩表現と限定条件を読み落とさずに結論の強さを調整する。

時間配分
Silent Plan 1分 / Reading 19分 / Dense Sentence 10分 / Questions 13分 / Japanese Explanation 10分 / Self-check 2分 / Post-solve 5分 ＝ Core 60分

Reading
[1] A student laboratory compares two digital scales. Scale A displays mass to the nearest 0.001 gram, while Scale B displays mass to the nearest 0.01 gram. At first glance, Scale A appears to provide more trustworthy measurements because it shows more digits.

[2] More digits can be useful. If a device is well calibrated and produces stable readings, finer display resolution may help users detect small differences. However, display resolution and measurement reliability are not the same thing.

[3] Students place the same reference object on each scale five times.

Table
Trial | Scale A (g) | Scale B (g)
1 | 50.014 | 50.02
2 | 49.982 | 50.01
3 | 50.027 | 50.02
4 | 49.991 | 50.01
5 | 50.019 | 50.02

[4] Scale A reports smaller units, yet its repeated readings spread across a wider range. Scale B gives fewer digits but repeats within a narrower interval. The table does not prove that Scale B is accurate in an absolute sense, because the true mass of the object has not been independently established. It does show that the number of displayed decimal places alone is insufficient to judge measurement quality.

Dense Sentence
Although a finer display can increase the amount of information reported by an instrument, treating that extra numerical detail as evidence of greater reliability is justified only when calibration, repeatability, and the measurement conditions are themselves sufficiently controlled to make the additional digits meaningful.

[5] This distinction matters beyond laboratory scales. A report may present a result as 37.428%, but the apparent precision of the percentage can exceed the precision of the data or model that produced it. Extra digits may communicate computational detail without adding real certainty.

Questions
Q1｜RQ06
What concession does paragraph 2 make before presenting the main limitation?

Q2｜D05
State the structure of the Dense Sentence as “concession → condition → main claim.”

Q3｜RQ10
How confident is the author that Scale B is more accurate than Scale A? Explain.

Q4｜RQ09
What does the table support about repeatability?

Q5｜A29
Rewrite the Dense Sentence as three simpler English sentences.

Japanese Explanation

解答・解説・自己修正

Q1｜解答例
It concedes that more digits can be useful when a device is well calibrated and stable, because finer resolution may reveal small differences.

Q2｜解答例
Concession: a finer display can report more information.
Condition: calibration, repeatability, and measurement conditions must be controlled.
Main claim: extra digits alone do not justify claiming greater reliability.

Q3｜解答例
The author is not confident that Scale B is absolutely more accurate. The true mass is unknown, so the table supports better repeatability for B, not proven accuracy.

Q4｜解答例
Scale B is more repeatable in this small test because its five readings vary within a narrower range.

Q5｜解答例
A finer display can report more numerical detail. That detail is useful only when the instrument and conditions are sufficiently controlled. More digits by themselves do not prove greater reliability.

Model Japanese explanation
Scale Aは小数第3位まで表示するが、同じ物体の反復測定では値のばらつきがScale Bより大きい。桁数は分解能を示しても、反復性や正確さそのものではない。また真の質量は別に確定していないため、表から言えるのは主に再現性の差であり、絶対的な正確さではない。

Self-correction
数字が細かい→信頼できる、ではない。concessionを残したまま結論を限定する。
