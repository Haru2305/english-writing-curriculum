E185｜復習間隔と記憶成績：相関から因果説明を作りすぎない

P3 / LT03 / D04 / RQ05 / RQ09 / WF03 / WT03 / SS03 / TF10

今日の狙い
練習間隔と成績の関係を読み、もっともらしい機序を説明しつつ、観察データから因果を断定しない。

時間配分
Silent Plan 1分 / Reading 16分 / Table 8分 / Questions 11分 / Causal Map 7分 / Cause-effect Writing 12分 / Self-check 2分 / Post-solve 3分 ＝ Core 60分

Reading
[1] A fictional language course examines how students review new vocabulary. The course records the average number of days between review sessions and a delayed test score given three weeks later.

[2] Students are not assigned to a schedule. They choose when to review, so the data are observational.

Table
Group | Average gap between reviews | Average review sessions | Delayed test score
A | less than 1 day | 7.1 | 72
B | 1–3 days | 6.8 | 81
C | 4–7 days | 5.2 | 78
D | more than 7 days | 3.9 | 66

[3] The pattern is consistent with a familiar hypothesis: some spacing between reviews may require students to retrieve a word after partial forgetting, strengthening later access to it. Very long gaps, however, may allow too much forgetting before the next successful retrieval.

[4] Yet the table does not isolate review spacing. Group B also completes more sessions than Groups C and D, and students who plan reviews regularly may differ in motivation, prior vocabulary knowledge, or study time. The observed pattern therefore supports a question worth testing, not a complete causal explanation.

[5] A stronger study could assign students to schedules while keeping the number of review sessions similar. If the moderately spaced group still performed better, the spacing explanation would become more credible.

Questions
Q1｜RQ05
What causal mechanism does paragraph 3 propose?

Q2｜RQ09
Why can we not conclude from the table that a 1–3 day gap causes the highest score?

Q3｜D04
Construct a causal chain that is consistent with the hypothesis but mark one uncertain link.

Q4｜RQ09
What does paragraph 5 add to the interpretation of the table?

Causal Map
Complete:
moderate spacing → ______ → stronger later access
but possible alternative:
regular planning / prior knowledge → ______ → higher score

Cause-effect Writing
In 100–120 English words, explain what the data suggest about review spacing and what additional evidence would be needed before making a causal recommendation.

Self-check
□ I explained a mechanism.
□ I did not confuse an observational pattern with proof.
□ I named at least one alternative explanation.
□ I stated what stronger evidence would look like.

解答・解説・自己修正

Q1｜解答例
Moderate spacing may allow partial forgetting, so the next review requires active retrieval; successful retrieval may then strengthen later access to the word.

Q2｜解答例
Because students selected their own schedules, the groups differ in more than spacing. Review frequency, motivation, prior knowledge, and total study time may also influence scores.

Q3｜解答例
Moderate spacing → more effortful retrieval → stronger memory on the delayed test. The uncertain link is whether spacing itself caused the more effective retrieval in these students.

Q4｜解答例
It proposes an experimental design that would control the schedule and make a causal interpretation more credible.

Causal Map｜例
moderate spacing → effortful successful retrieval → stronger later access
regular planning / prior knowledge → more effective study behavior → higher score

Model writing
The data suggest that extremely short or very long review gaps may be less effective than moderate spacing, since Group B achieved the highest delayed score. One possible mechanism is that partial forgetting makes later retrieval more effortful, and successful retrieval may strengthen memory. However, the data are observational: students chose their own schedules, and the groups also differed in review frequency and possibly motivation or prior knowledge. Before recommending a specific interval, the course should compare assigned schedules while keeping the number of reviews and other study conditions as similar as possible.

Self-correction
plausible mechanism ≠ demonstrated cause。観察→仮説→代替説明→必要な追加証拠の順で書く。

到達目安

B：限界は書けるが、機序または追加証拠が弱い。
C：最高平均の群をそのまま因果的に推奨する。
