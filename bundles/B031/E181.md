E181｜古い記録の語をどう読むか：言い換え・指示語・具体例から意味の射程を追う

P3 / LT03 / RQ02 / RQ03 / D02 / RF03 / RF04 / SS01 / TF03

今日の狙い
歴史資料の語を現代語へ置き換えるとき、単語だけを機械的に対応させず、前後の説明・具体例・指示語から意味の射程を限定する。

時間配分
Silent Plan 1分 / Reading 20分 / Reference & Paraphrase 12分 / Questions 11分 / Translation 8分 / Japanese Output 5分 / Self-check 1分 / Post-solve 2分 ＝ Core 60分

Reading
[1] A fictional town archive contains letters written by merchants about “public convenience” in the early nineteenth century. A modern reader might assume that the phrase refers to comfort or ease in daily life. In the letters, however, it often appears beside discussions of roads, storage yards, river landings, and the regular arrival of goods.

[2] Archivists therefore avoid replacing the phrase with a single modern expression too quickly. One letter states that a new landing place would “increase public convenience” because carts could unload even when the river was low. Another praises a covered market for the same reason: grain could be stored and exchanged more reliably during bad weather. These examples suggest that the phrase sometimes included the practical functioning of shared commercial facilities.

[3] This does not mean that every use of “public convenience” had exactly that meaning. In another letter, the writer uses the phrase when requesting a wider footpath near a school. Here, the concern appears to be easier movement rather than commercial exchange.

[4] The archive team uses a simple rule. First, preserve the original wording. Second, identify what problem the surrounding passage describes. Third, compare several examples before choosing a modern paraphrase. This method is slower than replacing old words with familiar modern equivalents, but it reduces the risk of importing a meaning that became common only later.

[5] One catalog note originally paraphrased the phrase as “public comfort.” The team later revised it to “practical public access” in a passage about river transport. The revision was not intended to claim that “public access” was the phrase’s permanent meaning. Rather, it was a local explanation of what the phrase was doing in that particular passage.

解答・解説・自己修正

Q1｜解答例
“Practical public access” or “the reliable functioning of shared commercial facilities” is appropriate because the examples concern unloading goods and storing grain under difficult conditions.

Q2｜解答
It refers to the idea that “public convenience” in the archive always meant the practical functioning of commercial facilities.

Q3｜解答例
The general claim is that the phrase often appeared in discussions of infrastructure and movement of goods. The landing-place and covered-market examples show specifically how the phrase was used in those contexts.

Q4｜解答例
The revised wording explained the phrase only in that passage and was not meant as a definition that applied everywhere.

Q5｜解答例
Because the meaning changes with context, and preserving the original wording lets later readers distinguish the source’s language from the archivists’ interpretation.

Translation｜和訳例
この方法は、古い語をなじみのある現代語に置き換えるより時間がかかるが、後になって一般化した意味を過去の文章に持ち込んでしまう危険を減らせる。
