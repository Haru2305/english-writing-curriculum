Text A｜AI screening review
A company has used an automated hiring tool for two years. An internal audit finds that the system processes applications quickly but performs less accurately for one category of jobs than for others. The review team acknowledges this limitation and suggests reducing the tool's role while more data are collected. Managers propose a temporary policy in which the system can organize applications but cannot automatically reject candidates. The company also creates an appeal process and recommends a new validation study before wider use resumes. Reviewers will compare error rates across job types and examine whether rejected applicants were later successful when assessed by people. The temporary policy has a fixed review date, so the company must decide again whether the evidence supports expansion, continued restriction, or withdrawal. During the review period, human recruiters must record why they overturn or follow the system's ranking. This allows the next audit to examine both automated errors and possible human overreliance on the score. It does not treat the tool as either completely reliable or completely useless.


Text B｜School timetable consultation
A school proposes moving the first lesson thirty minutes later. Parents and students submit mixed feedback. Some favor the change because students may sleep longer, while others worry about buses and after-school activities. The school rejects a request to move the start two hours later because transport and staffing make the idea impractical. However, it does not dismiss the concern about sleep. Instead, it suggests a one-month pilot and promises to publish attendance, punctuality, and transport data before making a permanent decision. Teachers will also record whether the later finish causes more absences from clubs or creates conflicts with shared bus routes. The school also compares results across students with different travel times, because an average improvement could hide new difficulties for families who depend on early buses or fixed work schedules. The school tells families that the pilot is a test, not a hidden permanent decision.


Text C｜Coastal protection plan
A coastal town considers a new flood barrier. Engineers recommend one design because it offers strong protection at a manageable cost, but residents object to the loss of access to part of the waterfront. Officials decline a request to cancel the entire project before the risk assessment is complete. They also refuse to withhold the engineering report from the public. After consultation, the town proposes a modified design with additional access points. It also promises to monitor beach erosion and emergency access after construction. If those effects are worse than predicted, the design can be adjusted later. Officials publish the monitoring criteria before construction so residents know what would count as an unexpected problem. This makes later revision part of the original plan rather than an admission of failure. The final plan acknowledges that no option removes every cost or risk.

Short Writing
モデル答案
A school can reject one proposed timetable without dismissing the concern that students are tired. It may suggest a smaller pilot and then propose a permanent change if the evidence supports it. Decision makers should acknowledge practical limits and explain why one solution was rejected rather than treating the underlying goal as unimportant.


別解
A company may acknowledge a limitation in an AI system without rejecting the technology entirely. It can propose a narrower use, recommend further validation, and refuse to allow automatic decisions until performance improves. This approach preserves possible benefits while responding to the identified risk.

解説補強｜Core recap

### 核
decision processは **suggest → propose → acknowledge limits → recommend / reject → revise** と進む

### 模範解答の読み方
特定のtimetableはrejectしても、student fatigueというconcernはdismissせず、小さなpilotへ修正している

### 次へ
controversial policyでは最初の案への賛否だけでなく、**修正可能なprocess**を考える
