E193｜チェックリストはなぜ効かなくなるのか：段落機能と文挿入で論理を追う

P3 / LT03 / D01 / RQ07 / RQ09 / RF07 / SS01 / TF10

今日の狙い
各段落が「主張・理由・例・限定」のどの役割を担うかを取り、文挿入では接続関係と指示語から位置を決める。

時間配分
Silent Plan 1分 / Reading 19分 / Paragraph Function 9分 / Insertion 10分 / Questions 11分 / Short Output 5分 / Self-check 2分 / Post-solve 3分 ＝ Core 60分

Reading
[1] Checklists are often introduced when people repeatedly forget important steps. Their value seems obvious: if a task contains several actions, writing them down should reduce omission. Yet a checklist can become ineffective even when every listed item is correct.

[2] One reason is that users may stop treating the list as a decision aid and begin treating completion itself as the goal. A worker may mark each box while paying little attention to whether the condition behind the item has actually been satisfied.

[3] This problem is especially likely when a checklist contains items that require judgment rather than simple confirmation. “Confirm the door is locked” can often be checked directly. “Confirm the situation is safe” is different: it may require interpreting several signs that cannot be reduced to one visible cue.

[4] A second problem is timing. If a checklist is completed too early, conditions may change afterward. If it is completed too late, the opportunity to prevent an error may already have passed.

Candidate Sentence
For this reason, a good checklist must support the reasoning process at the moment when the relevant decision is still open.

[5] Designers therefore need to ask not only what items belong on the list but also when and how the list will be used. The most useful version may be shorter than the most complete version if it focuses attention on the few decisions most likely to be missed.

[6] None of this means that checklists are unhelpful. It means that their effectiveness depends on whether the list fits the structure of the task rather than merely reproducing every possible instruction.

解答・解説・自己修正

Q1｜解答例
Paragraph 2 gives the first reason for checklist failure: users may focus on checking boxes rather than evaluating whether the underlying condition is satisfied.

Q2｜解答例
Paragraph 3 develops and specifies the first reason by showing why judgment-based checklist items are especially vulnerable.

Q3｜解答
After paragraph 4. “For this reason” refers to the timing problem just described, and the sentence turns that problem into a design principle before paragraph 5 broadens the discussion to checklist design.

Q4｜解答例
Because a very complete list may overload users or direct attention away from the few decisions that matter most at the relevant moment.

Q5｜解答例
Main claim: checklist effectiveness depends on how well the checklist fits the task. Reason: users may mechanically complete items without performing the judgment those items require.

Model short output
A checklist can fail when users treat marking boxes as the goal instead of checking the underlying condition. This is especially risky when an item requires judgment rather than simple observation. A person may technically complete every line while missing the reasoning the checklist was meant to support. The list must therefore fit both the decision and the time at which the decision is made.
