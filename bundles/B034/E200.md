E200｜「分かりやすさ」はどこで生まれるか：指示語・段落機能・文挿入を統合する

P3 / LT03 / RQ03 / RQ07 / RF07 / D07 / SS01 / TF16

今日の狙い
抽象説明文で指示語の参照先と段落役割を追い、候補文を論理的な位置に挿入する。

時間配分
Silent Plan 1分 / Reading 20分 / Reference Tracking 8分 / Paragraph Function 8分 / Insertion 9分 / Questions 9分 / Self-check 2分 / Post-solve 3分 ＝ Core 60分

Reading
[1] Why does a diagram sometimes clarify an idea that a paragraph does not? The obvious answer is that pictures are easier than words. But that contrast is too simple.

[2] A diagram can reduce the need to hold several relationships in memory at once. When arrows, groups, or spatial positions remain visible, the reader can inspect them repeatedly rather than reconstructing the full structure from sentences.

[3] This advantage depends on design. A crowded diagram can be harder to interpret than a short paragraph, and a visually attractive layout may still hide the relationship the learner needs to notice.

[4] The key issue is therefore not whether information is verbal or visual. It is whether the representation makes the relevant structure easy to inspect.

Candidate Sentence
That is why converting every paragraph into a picture does not automatically improve understanding.

[5] The same principle applies in the opposite direction. A diagram may show that two variables are connected but fail to explain why the connection matters. A short verbal explanation can then supply a causal or conceptual relation that the image leaves implicit.

[6] Effective explanation often combines representations. The question is not “Which medium is best?” but “Which relation is difficult to keep track of, and which representation makes that relation easiest to inspect?”

Questions

Q3｜RF07

Q4｜D07

Q5｜RQ08

解答・解説・自己修正

Q1｜解答例
It refers to the advantage of making several relationships continuously visible so the reader does not have to reconstruct them from memory.

Q2｜解答例
Paragraph 3 limits the previous claim by showing that the benefit of diagrams depends on how they are designed.

Q3｜解答
After paragraph 4. “That is why” refers to the principle that usefulness depends on making the relevant structure inspectable, not on visual form itself; the sentence then rejects an overgeneralized design rule before paragraph 5 gives the reverse case.

Q4｜解答例
It replaces the simple question of words versus pictures with a functional question: identify the relation that is difficult to track and choose the representation that best exposes it.

Q5｜解答例
Clear explanation depends less on whether information is verbal or visual than on whether the chosen representation makes the relevant relationship easy to inspect.

Self-correction
reference → function → insertion → main point の順で構造を固定する。
