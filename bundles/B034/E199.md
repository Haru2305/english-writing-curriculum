E199｜「示している」と「証明している」を分ける：証拠の強さに合わせて主張を調整する

P3 / LT03 / RQ09 / RQ10 / D05 / RF03 / SS03 / TF19

今日の狙い
観察結果・代替説明・追加検証を区別し、証拠の強さに合わせて結論の断定度を調整する。

時間配分
Silent Plan 1分 / Reading 17分 / Table 8分 / Questions 13分 / Claim Calibration 8分 / Japanese Explanation 8分 / Self-check 2分 / Post-solve 3分 ＝ Core 60分

Reading
[1] A fictional study examines whether background music affects proofreading accuracy. Students choose whether to work in silence, with instrumental music, or with music containing lyrics. They then correct errors in a short passage.

Table
Condition | Students | Mean errors missed | Mean task time
Silence | 48 | 4.1 | 12.8 min
Instrumental | 44 | 3.8 | 13.0 min
Lyrics | 46 | 5.3 | 13.1 min

[2] The lyric group missed more errors on average than the other two groups. This pattern is consistent with the idea that processing words in music competes with attention to written language.

[3] However, students selected their own condition. Those who prefer lyrical music may differ in reading habits, fatigue, or confidence. The groups also completed only one short proofreading task.

[4] The result therefore supports a hypothesis rather than proving a general causal effect. A stronger test would randomly assign participants to conditions and repeat the task with several kinds of text.

[5] It would also be too strong to conclude that all background music harms proofreading. The instrumental group performed similarly to the silence group in this sample. The evidence points to a possible problem with lyrical distraction, not to a universal rule about music.

Questions
Q1｜RQ09
What is the strongest conclusion directly supported by the table?

Q2｜RQ10
How confident is the author that lyrics cause lower proofreading accuracy?

Q3｜D05
What concession appears in paragraph 5 before the final claim is limited?

Q4｜RQ09
Name two alternative explanations for the difference between groups.

Q5｜RQ09
Why would random assignment strengthen the causal interpretation?

Claim Calibration
Rewrite each claim so that it matches the evidence:
A. “Lyrics reduce proofreading accuracy.”
B. “Music is bad for proofreading.”

Japanese Explanation
本文と表に基づき、「結果が仮説を支持すること」と「因果を証明すること」の違いを100〜130字で説明する。

Self-check
□ I matched certainty to evidence.
□ I kept alternative explanations.

解答・解説・自己修正

Q1｜解答例
Students who chose lyrical music missed more errors on average in this sample than students who chose silence or instrumental music.

Q2｜解答例
The author is cautious. The pattern is compatible with a causal explanation, but self-selection and the single-task design prevent a strong causal conclusion.

Q3｜解答例
It concedes that the instrumental group performed about as well as the silence group, which limits the broader claim that all music is harmful.

Q4｜解答例
Differences in reading habits, fatigue, confidence, or other characteristics associated with students’ self-selected conditions.

Q5｜解答例
Random assignment would reduce systematic pre-existing differences between groups, making the listening condition a more plausible explanation for later performance differences.

Claim Calibration｜例
A. In this sample, lyrical music was associated with more missed proofreading errors.
B. The results do not show that music in general harms proofreading; instrumental music produced similar performance to silence.

Model Japanese explanation
今回の結果は、歌詞付き音楽を選んだ群で見落としが多いという関連を示し、注意競合の仮説と整合する。一方、条件は自己選択で群間差を統制しておらず、課題も一種類だけなので、歌詞が成績低下を直接引き起こしたとまでは証明できない。

Self-correction
support / suggest / be consistent with と prove / determine を使い分ける。
