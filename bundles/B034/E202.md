E202｜速く決めるか、深く検討するか：二本文要約から条件付き評価を書く

P3 / LT03 / D06 / RQ12 / RF05 / WF05 / WF07 / WT03 / SS02 / TF18

今日の狙い
迅速な意思決定と熟慮型意思決定を共通軸で要約し、状況条件に応じた評価作文へつなげる。

時間配分
Silent Plan 1分 / Passage A 10分 / Passage B 10分 / Questions 9分 / English Summary 11分 / Evaluation Writing 13分 / Self-check 2分 / Post-solve 4分 ＝ Core 60分

Passage A｜Fast Decisions
In emergencies or highly familiar situations, rapid decisions can be effective. Experienced people may recognize patterns without consciously comparing every possible option. Delaying action to gather more information can itself create cost.

However, speed is not automatically expertise. A quick response may also reflect habit, overconfidence, or attention to one striking cue. Fast judgment is most defensible when the decision maker has relevant experience, receives reliable feedback, and faces a situation similar to previous cases.

Passage B｜Deliberate Decisions
Deliberate analysis is useful when consequences are large, options differ on several dimensions, or the situation is unfamiliar. Writing down criteria can expose trade-offs that an immediate reaction might miss.

Yet more analysis is not always better. Collecting additional information can delay action, and some uncertainty cannot be removed no matter how long a person thinks. Deliberation is therefore valuable when the extra analysis is likely to change the decision enough to justify its time and effort.

Questions
Q1｜RQ12
What common problem are both passages trying to solve?

Q2｜D06
Compare the two approaches on time cost and reliability conditions.

Q3｜RQ09
Why is fast judgment not automatically evidence of expertise?

Q4｜RQ09
When can additional analysis become inefficient?

English Summary
Summarize both passages in 75–90 English words using the same comparison axes.

Evaluation Writing
In 100–120 English words, advise a hospital committee deciding whether to adopt a new non-urgent administrative system. Use at least two ideas from your summary and explain when the committee should stop gathering more information.

Self-check

解答・解説・自己修正

Q1｜解答例
Both passages address how much time and analysis a decision should receive under different conditions.

Q2｜解答例
Fast decisions minimize delay but are most reliable when experience, feedback, and situation similarity are strong. Deliberate decisions cost more time but help when options are complex, unfamiliar, or high-stakes.

Q3｜解答例
Because quick responses can result from habit, overconfidence, or attention to a vivid cue rather than valid pattern recognition.

Q4｜解答例
When additional information is unlikely to change the choice enough to justify the delay and effort required to obtain it.

Model summary
Fast and deliberate decisions solve the same problem in different ways. Rapid judgment can reduce costly delay and may work well when a decision maker has relevant experience and reliable feedback, but speed can also reflect bias or habit. Deliberate analysis is more useful for unfamiliar, complex, or high-stakes choices, although continued information gathering can waste time. The key trade-off is therefore between the value of additional analysis and the cost of delaying action.

Model writing
The committee should use deliberate analysis because the system is new, non-urgent, and likely to affect several groups. It should compare costs, workflow effects, training needs, and failure risks rather than relying on a quick impression. However, the passages also show that more analysis is not automatically better. The committee should stop gathering information once the major criteria are covered and new evidence is unlikely to change the ranking of realistic options. At that point, continued delay would add cost without materially improving the decision.
