E091｜空き店舗の再利用：問題に対応する解決策を読む
P2 / LT02 / D03 problem→solution / TF11
今日の狙い
「良さそうな施策」ではなく、本文が定義したproblemのどの部分にsolutionが対応しているかを読む。解決策の副作用や未解決部分まで含めてproblem→solutionの対応を追う。
時間配分
Problem→Solution Guide 3分 / Reading 10分 / Relation Map 6分 / Questions 7分 / Short Output 2分 / Self-check 2分 ＝ 演習 30分 / Post-solve 6分（時間外）
Problem→Solution Guide
1. problemを症状ではなく原因まで分ける
2. solutionがどの原因へ作用するか確認する
3. solutionが新しいcostを生まないか見る
4. 「一部を改善」と「問題を解決」を区別する
Reading
[1] Some shopping streets have many empty storefronts even though people still live nearby. The visible problem is vacancy, but vacancy can result from several different causes. Rent may be too high for small businesses, buildings may need repairs, foot traffic may be concentrated at limited times, or owners may prefer to wait for a long-term tenant rather than accept a temporary use.
[2] Because the causes differ, a single “revitalization” program can miss the actual barrier. A city might offer small business grants, for example, but financial support to a new tenant does little if the building cannot legally be used without expensive renovation. Similarly, lowering rent for six months may attract a temporary shop without creating enough customer traffic for the business to remain later.
[3] One response is to use short pilot leases combined with basic building inspections. The inspection identifies which spaces can be opened with minor work, and a pilot lease reduces the risk for both owner and tenant. This approach addresses uncertainty about the space and the market at the same time. It does not, however, solve weak pedestrian demand by itself.
[4] A second response is to coordinate opening hours and events across several businesses. If the street is active only at unpredictable times, potential visitors may not know when it is worth going there. Shared evening hours or a regular market can create a clearer pattern of activity. Yet events can produce a temporary increase in visitors without improving the economics of ordinary weekdays.
[5] The useful question is therefore not “Which program sounds most attractive?” but “Which barrier is this program designed to reduce, and what remains after it works?” A proposal is stronger when the problem definition, the mechanism of the solution, and the remaining limitation line up.

Reading answers
Q1｜A
Paragraph 2は「solutionが魅力的でもproblemの原因とずれると効かない」ことを具体例で示す。
Q2｜B
Paragraph 3〜4の異なるsolutionと限界を、5のmatching principleが統合する。
Q3｜解答例
A pilot lease directly reduces uncertainty and commitment risk for owners and tenants, especially when combined with an inspection. Weak pedestrian demand can still remain.
Q4｜解答例
An event may increase visitors temporarily, but ordinary weekday demand and the business’s long-term economics may remain unchanged.
