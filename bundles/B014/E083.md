E083｜都市配送の共同拠点：高密度文を展開し要約する

Structure Guide
圧縮構造を展開してから、要約では再び必要な関係だけを圧縮する。
読むとき：compressed → explicit
書くとき：explicit → selective compression
Reading
[1] The growth of home delivery has increased the number of small vehicle trips in many city centers. A proposed response is the creation of shared delivery hubs, facilities where goods from several carriers can be transferred to fewer vehicles for the final part of a journey. The expected reduction in repeated trips is the main attraction of this model.
[2] The environmental benefit, however, depends on actual consolidation. The arrival of several carriers at a hub does not automatically reduce traffic if each carrier still sends separate vehicles into the surrounding area. Effective coordination requires the combining of loads, the scheduling of departures, and the matching of vehicle size to local demand.
[3] The location of the hub is equally important. A site far from major delivery routes may add extra travel before consolidation begins. A central site may reduce distance but face high land costs or limited loading space. The selection of a location, often presented as a technical decision, therefore involves trade-offs among distance, cost, capacity, and neighborhood impact.
[4] Another issue is participation. A hub can work only if enough carriers and receivers use it consistently. Voluntary participation may be easier to introduce, but its uncertain continuation can make investment risky. Mandatory participation can create a more stable volume while also imposing costs on firms whose existing routes already work efficiently.
[5] Shared hubs should therefore be evaluated as coordination systems rather than as buildings. Their success depends on whether multiple actors actually combine deliveries, whether the location reduces rather than shifts travel, and whether participation remains high enough to justify the infrastructure.
[6] The model can also create distributional effects. A hub located near one neighborhood may reduce delivery traffic on several streets while concentrating loading activity, noise, or curb use near the facility itself. A city that measures only total vehicle distance may therefore miss who receives the benefit and who experiences the local burden. The evaluation of a coordination system should include not only aggregate efficiency but also where costs are shifted.
Structure Check
1. “the creation of shared delivery hubs” を節へ展開しなさい。
2. “facilities where goods...” が何を説明する同格か。
3. “the combining of loads, the scheduling of departures, and the matching of vehicle size...” を動詞中心の節へ戻しなさい。

Reading answers
Q1｜解答例
Traffic falls only if deliveries are actually combined into fewer final vehicles; simply bringing carriers to the same location does not ensure consolidation.
Q2｜B
Paragraph 2〜4のconsolidation / location / participationを統合し、5のcoordination systemsへ接続する。
Structure Check｜例
1. the creation of shared delivery hubs = (a city or companies) create shared delivery hubs
2. “facilities where goods ...” は shared delivery hubs を言い換えて定義する同格

RQ11 Guided
要約はtopicだけでは不足する。shared hubsの目的に加えて「何が成功条件か」を圧縮する。
Summary Task｜解答例
Shared delivery hubs aim to reduce repeated urban delivery trips by consolidating goods from multiple carriers. Their success depends on actual load combination, a location that reduces rather than shifts travel, and stable participation by enough users. However, a central site may cut distance but raise land costs, so a hub should be judged as a coordination system, not simply as infrastructure.
