E079｜研究資金の配分：圧縮された学術文を展開して読む

Structure Guide
1. head nounを探す
2. 名詞化を動詞へ戻す
3. 同格は「つまり何か」を補う
4. 分詞句は主節との時間・原因・結果関係を確認する
5. 展開後に本文全体へ戻す
Reading
[1] Public research agencies often face the problem of allocating limited funds across projects with very different goals. The evaluation of proposals therefore involves more than ranking scientific quality. A technically excellent project may require expensive equipment, long preparation, or access to a rare population, while a smaller project may produce useful evidence quickly.
[2] One source of difficulty is the prediction of future value. The expected contribution of a project to knowledge, policy, or later innovation cannot be measured with certainty before the work begins. This uncertainty does not make evaluation meaningless, but it makes the use of a single score problematic. Reviewers must often compare different kinds of promise rather than identical outcomes.
[3] The preference for diversified funding, an approach intended to avoid placing all resources on a few highly rated projects, can be understood as a response to this uncertainty. Supporting several methods or research questions may reduce the risk that one mistaken prediction determines the entire program. At the same time, excessive spreading of funds can leave every project too weakly supported to succeed.
[4] The resulting tension between concentration and diversification cannot be solved by a fixed formula. A funding agency has to consider the cost of failure, the value of learning from unsuccessful work, and the degree to which projects depend on one another. The decision is therefore partly about portfolio design, not merely about selecting the individually highest-scoring proposal.
[5] Clear evaluation requires the unpacking of these compressed judgments. “High potential,” “strategic importance,” and “acceptable risk” are not self-explanatory categories. They summarize assumptions about future benefits, uncertainty, and trade-offs that should be made visible when funds are distributed.
[6] Another source of compression is the language of evaluation itself. A phrase such as “the prioritization of high-impact research” can hide several decisions: who defines impact, over what time period, and whether uncertain long-term benefits should count more than measurable short-term results. Expanding the phrase into explicit clauses does not remove disagreement, but it makes the assumptions easier to examine. This is especially important when different reviewers appear to agree on a label while using different criteria underneath it.
Structure Check
1. “the prediction of future value” を節へ展開しなさい。
2. “The preference for diversified funding, an approach intended to avoid...” の同格関係を説明しなさい。
3. “Supporting several methods or research questions may reduce...” でSupporting句が文中で果たす役割を説明しなさい。

Structure Check
1. the prediction of future value = predicting how valuable the project will be in the future
2. “an approach intended to avoid...” explains what diversified funding means and why it is used.
3. “Supporting several methods or research questions” functions as the subject of “may reduce.”
Reading answers
Q1｜解答例
Because projects differ in goals, costs, and types of possible value, and their future contribution cannot be known with certainty.
Q2｜D
全文は単純な順位付けではなく、圧縮された評価語の背後にあるvalue / uncertainty / riskを展開する必要性へ向かう。
Q3｜解答例
Diversification can reduce the risk of relying on one mistaken prediction, but spreading funds too widely can leave each project underfunded.
A29｜名詞化・圧縮構造
prediction → someone predicts
evaluation → someone evaluates
preference → someone prefers
allocation → someone allocates
名詞化を節へ戻すと、行為者・対象・不確実性が見えやすくなる。
