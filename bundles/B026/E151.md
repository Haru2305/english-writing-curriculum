Reading
[1] Decisions are often described as choices between acting now and delaying. This description can be misleading because delay is not always passive. In some situations, waiting preserves the ability to choose later after new information becomes available.
[2] Consider a city deciding whether to convert an unused riverside lot into permanent parking. Building the lot would create an immediate benefit by increasing parking capacity, but it would also make later restoration more expensive. Leaving the land unchanged for one year has a cost because the parking shortage remains, yet it preserves more future options.
[3] We can call this benefit the value of reversibility. A reversible decision is one that can be changed later without losing too much time, money, or opportunity. The concept does not mean that reversible choices are always better. It means that the ability to revise a decision can itself have value when important information is still uncertain.
[4] The value becomes larger when three conditions are present. First, future information is likely to change the decision. Second, acting now would be difficult or expensive to undo. Third, the cost of waiting is limited. If all three conditions are weak, delay offers little advantage.
[5] This explains why the same delay can be sensible in one case and wasteful in another. A hospital choosing between two software systems may benefit from a short pilot if long-term performance is uncertain and switching later would be costly. By contrast, waiting to replace a clearly dangerous broken machine may simply prolong a known risk.
[6] The concept also changes how “efficiency” is judged. A decision that produces the highest immediate return may still be less attractive if it closes off valuable future choices. Conversely, preserving flexibility at any cost is not efficient either. The relevant question is how much future adaptability is worth relative to the present cost of waiting.
[7] The broader lesson is not “wait whenever uncertain.” It is that uncertainty, reversibility, and delay cost should be considered together. When new information may matter and commitment is hard to reverse, keeping an option open can be a rational part of decision-making.

The value of reversibility is the benefit of keeping a decision changeable when later information may matter and reversing an early commitment would be costly.

Model output
The value of reversibility is the benefit of keeping a choice open when future information may affect what should be done. It becomes especially important when an early decision would be costly to undo. However, waiting is not automatically better, because delay also has costs. Reversibility matters most when new information is useful, commitment is difficult to reverse, and waiting remains reasonably affordable. It is therefore not a rule to wait whenever things are uncertain.
## 解説の考え方

### この問題で考えること
「不確実なら待つ」と覚えるのではなく、**future information / irreversibility / cost of waiting** の3条件を同時に評価する。

### 考え方
reversibility の価値は、待つことで情報を得られ、その情報がdecisionを変え得る一方、今のcommitmentを戻すのが高コストなときに大きくなる。逆に既知の危険を放置するような場合は waiting cost が大きく、可逆性を理由に延期できない。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× 「不確実なら延期する方が安全」  
○ 「情報が増える見込みがあり、今決めると戻しにくく、待つコストが小さいときにoption valueが高い」  
改善点は、**uncertainty 単独ではなく、行動可能性と待機コストまで mechanism に入れる**こと。

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
不可逆な投資・制度導入・技術採用では、便益とリスクに加えて **後から変えられるか / 待てば何が分かるか / 待つ間に何を失うか**を比較する。
