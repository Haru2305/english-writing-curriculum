Passage A
A public library is considering digitizing all local-history materials before users request them. Supporters argue that a complete digital collection would make searching easier, reduce handling of fragile originals, and allow remote access to materials that currently require an in-person visit.
The difficulty is cost. Some documents are rarely used, and scanning, checking image quality, adding metadata, and maintaining files require staff time. A complete project may also delay access to high-demand materials because resources are spread across the entire collection.
Passage B
A second proposal is “digitization on demand.” Materials are scanned when users request them, and frequently requested categories are then prioritized for broader conversion. This approach directs effort toward demonstrated demand and may reveal which parts of the collection matter most to users.
However, first-time access remains slower because an unscanned item cannot be delivered immediately. Rare materials may also remain invisible if users do not know they exist. Demand therefore reflects both genuine interest and the quality of the catalog that helps people discover materials.
Questions
Q1｜RQ12
What goal is shared by both proposals?
Q2｜D06
Compare the proposals on speed of future access and use of staff resources.
Q3｜RQ09
Why might low request numbers fail to prove that a document has little value?

Q1｜解答例
Both proposals aim to make the library’s local-history materials available digitally so that users can access them, while managing the staff effort that scanning requires.
別解：to improve access to local-history materials。
Q2｜解答例
Complete digitization gives faster future access once finished but requires large up-front staff effort. On-demand digitization uses staff time more selectively but can delay access to unscanned items.
Q3｜解答例
Because users may not request materials they cannot easily discover in the catalog, so low demand may partly reflect poor visibility.
Model English summary
Both proposals aim to expand digital access to local-history materials, but they differ in when and how much to scan. Complete digitization would make future searching and remote use faster, but it requires substantial up-front staff time and may delay high-priority work. On-demand digitization targets demonstrated use and conserves resources, yet first-time access is slower and rare materials may remain hidden if users cannot discover them easily. The trade-off is therefore breadth and speed versus selective use of limited staff capacity.
Model evaluation
The library should begin with on-demand digitization while improving the catalog so users can discover unscanned materials. This approach fits limited staffing because effort is directed toward items that users actually request, while remote access still expands over time. However, the library should also identify some fragile or historically important materials for proactive scanning, since request numbers alone may underestimate value. Staff time saved on rarely used items could then be redirected to improving remote access to the most requested collections. A mixed policy would therefore balance staff capacity, remote access, and the risk that low visibility hides meaningful demand.
## 解説の考え方

### この問題で考えること
limited staff という条件の下で、**remote access をどこまで速く広げるか / staff effort をどこへ集中するか / hidden demand をどう扱うか**を判断する。

### 考え方
complete digitization は将来accessの速さとbreadthに強いがup-front costが大きい。on-demand はresource allocationに強いが、discoverability が低い資料はrequest自体が起きない。したがって「requestが少ない＝価値が低い」という推論を避け、catalog improvement と selective proactive scanning を組み合わせる。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× 「利用実績の多いものだけデジタル化すれば効率的」  
○ 「demonstrated demand を優先しつつ、catalog visibility と historical importance で見えない需要を補正する」

### 答案の組み立て
- **Position**: on-demand を基盤に mixed policy
- **Reason**: limited staff を高需要へ集中できる
- **Mechanism**: request に応じて順次remote accessを増やす
- **Qualification**: low requests may reflect poor discoverability
- **Return**: catalog improvement + selected proactive scanning
