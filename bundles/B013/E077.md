Reading
[1] Some urban drainage systems use small planted areas or shallow basins to hold rainwater temporarily before it enters pipes. In this lesson, retention time means how long water remains in the system before leaving through drainage or infiltration. A longer retention time can reduce the speed at which water reaches downstream pipes during a short storm.
[2] Longer is not always better. If water remains too long after ordinary rain, a site may stay unusable or create maintenance problems. Designers therefore combine storage volume, drainage rate, available space, and maintenance access. The objective is not to maximize one number but to slow peak flow while returning the space to normal use within an acceptable period.
[3] The table compares four fictional designs for the same small site. All four fit the available area. The city’s pilot rule is: prefer designs with at least 18 cubic meters of storage, a retention time between 4 and 10 hours, and no more than 6 maintenance visits per year. If more than one design meets those conditions, the lower annual maintenance burden is preferred.
[4] This simplified rule does not prove which design would work in a real city. It shows how a textual decision rule can be applied consistently to a data table. The important reading skill is identifying which conditions are mandatory and which criterion is used only after the mandatory conditions are met.
[5] A real pilot would also have to test whether the assumed retention time remains stable under different rainfall conditions. A design that performs well in a moderate storm might fill too quickly in a more intense event, while a very slow-draining system could fail to empty before the next storm. This is why the table is best read as a screening tool. It identifies which options satisfy the stated starting conditions, but later monitoring would still be needed before a long-term design decision.

Integrated answers
Q1｜B
Bはstorage 22、retention 7h、maintenance 4で全mandatory conditionsを満たす。Dも条件を満たすがmaintenance 6なので、tie-breakでB。
Q2｜解答例
Design C has the largest storage volume, but its 12-hour retention time exceeds the acceptable 4–10 hour range, so it fails a mandatory condition.
Q3｜解答例
Retention time is how long water remains in the system before leaving. Maximizing it is not the goal because water that stays too long can create usability or maintenance problems.
Q4｜解答例
First, eliminate any design that fails the storage, retention-time, or maintenance limits. If several designs remain, choose the one with the lower maintenance burden.
Short Output｜例
Mandatory conditions: storage ≥ 18 m³ / retention time 4–10 hours / maintenance visits ≤ 6 per year
Tie-break criterion: the lower annual maintenance burden
