Reading
[1] A city is testing “flex stops” on a low-frequency bus route. A flex stop is a location that a bus serves only when a passenger has requested it in advance. The idea is to avoid making every bus enter a side street when nobody needs that stop, while still preserving access for passengers who do.
[2] The system has two competing goals. Skipping unused stops can reduce travel time for through-passengers, but a request system can create a new barrier if it is difficult to use. A pilot should therefore consider both operational benefit and practical accessibility. A stop with large time savings is not automatically a good candidate if many of its users cannot reliably make a request.
[3] The pilot team sets three conditions. A candidate stop should save at least 3 minutes per trip when skipped, should have no more than 25 average boardings per day, and should have at least 70% of surveyed users reporting that they could use one of the available request methods. If two stops meet all three conditions, the team prefers the stop with the lower daily boardings because fewer trips are likely to require the detour.
[4] These rules are deliberately simple. They do not prove that flex stops are a good transport policy in general. They provide a transparent way to test whether a particular location fits the pilot’s stated objective without confusing operational efficiency with accessibility.
[5] The request system itself can also change behavior. If passengers must request a stop too early, occasional users may forget; if they can request it only through a smartphone, the accessibility percentage may fall for groups with limited digital access. A pilot can therefore use the table to choose a starting location, but it should also observe failed requests, complaints, and changes in boarding behavior. A candidate that satisfies the initial thresholds may still need redesign if the request process creates barriers that the survey did not predict.

Integrated answers
Q1｜B

Q2｜解答例
Stop C saves the most time, but only 61% of surveyed users report that they can use a request method, below the required 70% accessibility threshold.
Q3｜解答例
A flex stop is served only when a passenger requests it in advance. The trade-off is between saving travel time by skipping unused stops and preserving practical access for people who need the stop.
Q4｜B
本文全体はefficiency一辺倒ではなく、explicit criteriaでoperational benefitとaccessibilityを両立させる。
Q5｜解答例
Through-passengers prioritize avoiding unnecessary detours and reducing travel time, while stop users prioritize reliable access and a request system they can actually use.
