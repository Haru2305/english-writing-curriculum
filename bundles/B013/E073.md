E073｜地下空間の案内設計：知らない題材でも本文から組み立てる
P2 / LT02 / A25 未知題材耐性 / TF11
今日の狙い
背景知識がほとんどない題材でも、本文内の定義・因果・具体例だけを使って理解を組み立てる。知らない単語や制度名を見ても、外部知識で穴埋めしない。
時間配分

Unknown-topic Strategy
1. 最初に「本文が定義している語」を拾う
2. 因果・対比・例示だけで骨格を作る
3. 知らない固有概念は仮ラベルのまま保持する
4. 選択肢を自分の常識ではなく本文根拠で切る
Reading
[1] Large underground stations can be difficult to navigate because travelers cannot always use distant buildings, the sun, or other outdoor landmarks to confirm direction. Designers therefore rely on internal cues. One such cue is a “decision point”: a location where a traveler must choose among two or more routes. A useful sign should appear before the decision is required, not after the traveler has already entered the wrong corridor.
[2] This sounds simple, but adding more signs can sometimes make navigation harder. If several signs compete for attention at the same point, a traveler may spend longer deciding which information matters. Repeating the same destination in different formats can also create uncertainty if the labels are not consistent. The problem is therefore not merely the amount of information but how information is ordered around a choice.
[3] Designers sometimes use what can be called progressive disclosure. A traveler is shown only the information needed for the next decision, while later details appear closer to later decision points. Someone entering a station may first need to know which zone contains a platform. Inside that zone, the traveler may then need a platform number, and only near the platform may carriage positions become useful.
[4] The approach has a trade-off. Too little information can make travelers feel uncertain, especially when they want to plan several steps ahead. Too much information can overload a single sign. Good design therefore depends on identifying which decisions must be made now and which can safely wait.
[5] The broader principle is that navigation support should follow the sequence of decisions made by the user. A sign is effective not because it contains every possible fact but because it provides the right information at the point where that information changes the next action.
[6] The same logic can be used when different travelers need different amounts of planning. A commuter who already knows the station may need only a platform number, while a first-time visitor may want confirmation that the chosen corridor still leads toward the correct zone. This does not require showing every later detail immediately. It means that repeated confirmation can be useful when it supports the next decision without competing with information needed at the current point.

Reading answers
Q1｜解答例
A decision point is a place where a traveler must choose between two or more routes.
Q2｜A
Paragraph 3 introduces progressive disclosure and then illustrates it with the sequence zone → platform number → carriage position.
Q3｜B
Paragraph 1〜4の具体論をまとめ、情報量そのものではなくdecision sequenceとの対応を中心にする。
Q4｜解答例
More signs can compete for attention or present inconsistent labels, making it harder for travelers to decide which information matters.
A25｜未知題材耐性
A25では「題材を知っていること」を能力にしない。未知語があっても、definition / cause / contrast / exampleを拾えば本文内部だけで理解を構築できる。
今回なら decision point と progressive disclosure は本文が定義しているため、事前知識は不要。
