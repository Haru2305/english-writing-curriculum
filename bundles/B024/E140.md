今日の狙い

名詞化・挿入・並列が重なる長い英文を節へ展開し、意味関係を保った自然な日本語へ変換する。



時間配分

Reading

[1] Scientific advice often becomes difficult to communicate when evidence is incomplete. Researchers may agree that an effect is possible while disagreeing about how large it is, how often it occurs, or whether the observed association reflects a direct causal mechanism.



[2] This uncertainty does not make evidence useless. It changes what responsible communication should claim. A recommendation can be reasonable even when every mechanism is not fully understood, provided that the expected benefit, the possible cost of action, and the cost of delaying action are considered together.



Dense Sentence A

The tendency, common in public discussions of uncertain evidence, to treat the absence of a single conclusive experiment as equivalent to the absence of any meaningful reason for action can obscure the cumulative value of multiple studies that, although individually limited, point in a similar direction.



Dense Sentence B

What makes such cases difficult is not merely that different studies produce different numerical estimates but that decisions must often be made before the sources of those differences—including variation in populations, measurement methods, and background conditions—have been fully separated.



[3] The opposite error is also possible. If several studies point in the same direction, communicators may speak as though uncertainty has disappeared. Yet agreement about direction is not the same as agreement about magnitude, mechanism, or applicability to every population.



[4] Good scientific communication therefore requires two forms of discipline: avoiding the claim that uncertainty means “nothing is known,” and avoiding the claim that repeated evidence means “everything important is settled.” The goal is to represent both what the evidence supports and where its limits remain.



Questions

Q1｜A29

Rewrite Dense Sentence A as 3–4 simpler English clauses.



Q2｜RF04

Translate Dense Sentence A into natural Japanese.



Q3｜A28

What is parallel in the phrase “how large it is, how often it occurs, or whether…” in paragraph 1?



Q4｜RF03

解答・解説・自己修正



Q1｜解答例

Public discussions often make a particular mistake. They treat the lack of one conclusive experiment as if there were no meaningful reason to act. This can hide the value of several limited studies. Those studies may still point in the same direction when considered together.



Q2｜和訳例

不確実な証拠をめぐる公的議論では、決定的な一つの実験がないことを、行動を支持する意味のある根拠がまったくないことと同一視しがちである。この傾向は、個々には限界があっても同じ方向を示す複数研究の累積的な価値を見えにくくすることがある。



Q3｜解答

Three unresolved dimensions are coordinated: size of the effect, frequency of the effect, and whether the association is causal.



Q4｜解答例

同じ方向の結果が複数研究で得られても、効果の大きさ、原因となる仕組み、どの集団にも当てはまるかまでは一致しているとは限らないため。



A29 × RF04

圧縮構造は

noun phrase → clause

nominalization → who/what does what

へ展開してから日本語に戻す。



Model Japanese explanation

研究ごとに数値が違うだけでなく、その差が対象集団、測定法、背景条件のどれによるか十分に分からない段階でも判断を求められるため、不確実な科学的証拠に基づく意思決定は難しい。



Self-correction
