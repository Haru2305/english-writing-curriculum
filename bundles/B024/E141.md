Passage A

A school district is considering replacing some printed homework with an online platform that gives immediate feedback. Supporters argue that students benefit when they can see errors soon after answering. The platform can also adjust question difficulty based on previous responses, allowing students to spend less time on skills they have already mastered.



However, immediate feedback can become too easy to rely on. If students repeatedly guess until the system accepts an answer, they may complete more questions without developing a clear explanation of why an answer is correct. Supporters therefore propose using the platform mainly for practice after new material has been taught, with occasional tasks that require students to explain their reasoning without hints.



Passage B

Another group favors shorter but more frequent written homework. They argue that writing by hand encourages students to organize steps and gives teachers visible evidence of how an answer was produced. A paper assignment can also be completed without a reliable device or internet connection.



The weakness is slower feedback. A student may repeat the same misunderstanding across several questions before the work is checked. Teachers also need time to mark routine practice. For this reason, supporters of written homework propose reducing the number of questions and focusing on tasks where showing reasoning is more valuable than receiving instant correction.

Q1｜解答例

Both proposals aim to make practice support learning efficiently while preserving meaningful understanding.



Q2｜解答例

The online platform gives faster feedback but can hide weak reasoning if students rely on hints, whereas written homework makes reasoning visible but delays correction.



Q3｜解答例

Feedback speed, visibility of reasoning, access requirements, teacher workload, and suitability for different task types.



Model English summary

Both proposals aim to make homework more useful, but they emphasize different strengths. An online platform gives immediate feedback and can adjust difficulty, though students may rely too heavily on hints and guess until an answer is accepted. Written homework makes reasoning visible and works without reliable technology, but feedback is slower and marking takes time. Each format therefore suits some learning goals better than others, so the best choice depends on the kind of practice intended.



Model judgment

The district should use both formats rather than choose only one. Online practice is useful for routine skills because students receive quick feedback and difficulty can be adjusted. Written homework is better when teachers need to inspect reasoning or when access to devices is unreliable. The school should therefore assign short online exercises after instruction and use fewer written tasks for explanation-heavy problems. Teachers could also review a few written explanations each week to check that online success reflects real understanding. This policy balances feedback speed, visible reasoning, and access without assuming that one format serves every learning goal.
