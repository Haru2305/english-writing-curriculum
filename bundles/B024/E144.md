Passage A

A town is testing neighborhood batteries that store electricity generated by rooftop solar panels. The goal is not to make each building independent from the grid. Instead, the batteries are intended to reduce sharp evening demand by storing some electricity during the day and releasing it when many households use power at once.



Early results are encouraging, but they require careful interpretation. On sunny days, evening demand from participating blocks fell noticeably. On cloudy days, the effect was smaller because less excess solar electricity was available to store. [Blank A]



The system also changed how residents thought about timing. Some households began running washing machines or charging devices earlier in the day when local solar production was high. These behavioral changes were not required by the program, but they increased the amount of evening demand that could be avoided.



Candidate Sentence

This variation means that storage can reduce peak demand without guaranteeing the same reduction every day.



Passage B

An energy analyst argues that neighborhood batteries should be evaluated as part of a larger system. Batteries can shift electricity across time, but they do not create new energy. If a region has several cloudy days and high demand, stored energy may be limited. Grid connections and other power sources therefore remain necessary.



At the same time, complete independence is not the only useful goal. A battery that reduces demand during the most expensive or stressful hours can still improve system operation even if it supplies only part of total consumption.

Cloudy weekday | 9%

Q1｜解答

After the sentence “On cloudy days, the effect was smaller because less excess solar electricity was available to store.” The Candidate Sentence summarizes the sunny/cloudy contrast and prepares the broader interpretation.



Q2｜解答例

Peak reduction is consistently larger on sunny days than cloudy days, suggesting that available solar generation strongly affects the battery’s short-term contribution.



Q3｜解答例

Both passages argue that batteries can be useful without providing complete or constant independence from the grid.



Q4｜和訳例

完全に自立できることだけが、有用性を判断する唯一の目標ではない。



Q5｜解答例

The table supports the claim that batteries reduce evening peaks but also qualifies it by showing that the size of the reduction varies substantially with conditions.



Model summary

The town is testing neighborhood batteries to reduce evening electricity peaks by storing daytime solar power. Participating areas showed meaningful reductions, especially on sunny days, and some residents also shifted electricity use earlier. However, performance varied with weather, and batteries did not remove the need for the grid. The sources therefore present storage as a tool for reducing peak pressure rather than a guarantee of energy independence.



Model writing

The town should continue the trial if the batteries consistently reduce evening peak demand enough to improve grid operation. Peak reduction should be the main performance measure because that is the program’s stated purpose, not complete energy independence. However, the town should compare results across sunny and cloudy periods, since the table shows large variation in performance. It should also track whether behavioral changes contribute to the result. A longer trial would show whether useful peak reduction remains reliable across different conditions without assuming that batteries can replace the grid.
