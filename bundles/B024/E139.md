E139｜地域イベントの成功例：一例からどこまで一般化できるか

P3 / LT01 / D09 Review / RQ09 / RQ10 / TF11



今日の狙い

成功例から一般化するとき、何が本文から言えて、何が言えないかを区別する。example→generalizationとhedgingを組み合わせる。



時間配分

Generalization Check 5分 / Reading 18分 / Questions 12分 / Claim Repair 10分 / Short Output 7分 / Self-check 3分 / Post-solve 5分 ＝ Core 60分



Reading

[1] A small town tested a monthly evening market in a public square that had previously been quiet after 6 p.m. Local restaurants, student groups, and craft sellers could apply for low-cost stalls. During the first three months, attendance was higher than organizers expected, nearby shops stayed open later, and survey responses were generally positive.



[2] It would be tempting to treat the event as proof that evening markets can revive underused public spaces. The trial does support a narrower conclusion: under some conditions, a recurring event can attract people to a place they previously had little reason to visit. However, several features of the town may have contributed to the result.



[3] The square was next to a railway station and already had lighting, toilets, and a sheltered area. Organizers also scheduled the market on evenings when there were no major competing events nearby. In addition, several popular food businesses joined from the start, giving residents a reason to attend even before they knew the smaller sellers.



[4] These conditions matter because another town could copy the visible feature—the market stalls—without copying the surrounding support. A square far from public transport might require a different strategy. A town with severe winter weather could face a much shorter operating season. A place with few participating businesses might need more promotion before the event becomes self-sustaining.



[5] The attendance data also require caution. Higher visitor numbers show that the event attracted people, but they do not by themselves show long-term economic benefit. Some visitors may simply have shifted purchases from other days or locations. Nor does a positive survey prove that all residents benefited equally; people disturbed by evening noise may have been less likely to attend and answer the survey.



[6] The useful generalization is therefore conditional. Events can help activate public space when the location is accessible, basic infrastructure is available, and the program gives people a clear reason to attend. The case is valuable not because it gives every town a formula, but because it identifies conditions that other communities can examine before copying the idea.



Questions

Q1｜RQ09

What can reasonably be inferred from the trial?



Q2｜D09

What broader point does the example support?



Q3｜RQ10

Why does the author use “can” and “under some conditions” instead of stronger language?



Q4｜RQ08

What is the main point of the passage?



Claim Repair

Revise each claim so that it matches the evidence:

1. Evening markets always revive public spaces.

2. The market increased the town’s total economic activity.

3. Positive survey results show that all residents supported the event.



Short Output

In 60–80 English words, state one useful generalization from the case and two conditions that limit it.



Self-check

□ I separated evidence from extrapolation.

□ I kept the author’s degree of certainty.

□ I did not turn one case into a universal rule.

□ I used conditions, not vague “it depends.”

解答・解説・自己修正



Q1｜解答例

A recurring event can increase use of an underused public space when supporting conditions such as access, infrastructure, and attractive participation are present.



Q2｜解答例

Successful local programs are most transferable when we identify the conditions that made them work rather than copy only their visible form.



Q3｜解答例

Because the evidence comes from one short trial in one town, and several local conditions may have contributed to the result.



Q4｜解答例

The market case is useful as conditional evidence, not as a universal formula for revitalizing public space.



Claim Repair

1. Evening markets can increase use of some public spaces when access, infrastructure, and participation conditions are favorable.

2. The market increased attendance and may have benefited nearby businesses, but the trial did not establish a net increase in total economic activity.

3. Positive survey results suggest broad support among respondents, but they do not prove that every resident supported the event.



D09｜example→generalization

例から一般化するときは、

case evidence → condition → broader principle

の順で上げる。条件を飛ばして universal claim にしない。



Model output

The trial suggests that recurring events can activate underused public spaces, but the result depends on supporting conditions. In this town, the square was easy to reach and already had useful infrastructure. Popular businesses also gave residents a clear reason to attend. Other towns should therefore examine access and local participation before assuming that the same visible format will produce the same result.



Self-correction

always / proves / all / will などが本文より強くなっていないか確認する。
