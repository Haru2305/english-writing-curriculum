Text A｜Rural transport
A rural region has fewer passengers than it did ten years ago. The number of people using each bus route is falling, but a number of older residents still depend on buses to reach clinics and shops. Closing every low-use route could reduce operating costs, yet it could also increase isolation for people who cannot drive. The region decides to invest in smaller vehicles and flexible booking rather than keep every route unchanged. Officials hope this will ease pressure on the transport budget while preserving basic access. They also keep several fixed departures because some passengers cannot use an app or make reservations in advance. A telephone booking option remains available, and staff check whether waiting times become longer for people living far from the main town. They also monitor missed trips and complaints. The new system is cheaper only if flexible services do not create excessive administrative costs or new barriers, so the region plans to compare both cost and actual access after the change.


Text B｜Food distribution
A supermarket chain has reduced inventory to lower storage costs. During normal weeks, the system works efficiently, but a disruption at one distribution center quickly creates shortages. Managers begin using a second supplier for several essential products and keep a small reserve of goods that are difficult to replace. These measures increase ordinary costs, but they may mitigate the impact of future delays. The company also improves communication so that temporary shortages do not encourage unnecessary panic buying. It does not increase reserves for every product, because large stocks of perishable goods can raise waste. Instead, managers identify items with few substitutes and longer delivery times. They also review which products depend on the same transport route, because two suppliers do not provide much protection if both can be disrupted by the same bottleneck. They accept some extra cost in normal weeks in exchange for more time to respond when the main supply route fails.


Text C｜Translation at a public service office
A city office receives documents in many languages. Automatic translation can process routine notices quickly, but some applications contain ambiguous wording or information with legal consequences. The office uses software to produce a first draft and asks trained staff to review high-stakes sections. A terminology guide supplements the software, while human review compensates for errors involving context. The office also avoids sending confidential documents through tools that have not been approved for sensitive information. Routine notices can be handled with lighter review, while decisions affecting rights or eligibility receive closer checking. Staff record recurring errors and update the terminology guide when the same problem appears repeatedly. The system does not replace staff; it allows them to focus attention where mistakes would matter most and reduces time spent translating predictable phrases.

## Writing解説

Short Writing
モデル答案
A rural transport system cannot be judged only by the number of passengers using each route. A region may invest in flexible services to reduce unnecessary costs while preserving access. These changes can ease pressure on the budget, but they do not eliminate every transport problem. Additional support may still be needed for residents who cannot use flexible services easily.


別解
A food supply chain can reduce storage costs by keeping low inventory, but this may make disruption more serious. A reserve can mitigate some risk, while a second supplier can complement the main source of supply. The system becomes less efficient in normal times but more resilient during a disruption.
