Reading
[1] Why do experts sometimes give explanations that are accurate yet difficult for nonexperts to use? One tempting answer is that experts simply know too much and therefore include unnecessary detail. Excess detail can certainly be a problem, but it does not fully explain the gap.

[2] Expertise changes what feels obvious. After years of practice, a specialist may no longer notice the intermediate steps that once required conscious attention. A statistician, for example, may move from a graph to a conclusion without explicitly stating the assumptions that connect them. To the specialist, those assumptions are background knowledge; to a learner, they may be the missing bridge.

[3] The difficulty is therefore not merely the amount of information. It is also the organization of information around knowledge that the speaker and listener do not share equally. This helps explain why deleting technical terms does not automatically make an explanation clear. A sentence can contain simple words while still depending on an unstated chain of reasoning.

[4] What, then, makes an explanation more usable? One useful strategy is to identify the decision or inference that the audience must make and work backward. The explainer can ask which steps are necessary for that inference and which assumptions are likely to be unfamiliar. This does not require presenting every detail. It requires making the hidden bridge visible.

[5] Even this strategy has limits. Different audiences begin with different knowledge, so no single explanation will be optimal for everyone. In addition, making every intermediate step explicit can overload a reader. Good explanation is therefore not maximal explanation. It is selective reconstruction of the reasoning that the intended audience needs.

解答・解説・自己修正

Q1｜解答例
The passage asks why accurate expert explanations can still be hard for nonexperts to use. It rejects “experts include too much detail” as only a partial explanation.

Q2｜解答例
Expert explanations often fail because expertise hides intermediate reasoning, so effective explanation requires selectively reconstructing the steps an intended audience needs.

Q3｜解答例
The claim is qualified rather than absolute. Working backward is presented as useful, but the author says audiences differ and that making too many steps explicit can overload readers.

Q4｜解答例
Because the real difficulty may be an unstated logical bridge rather than vocabulary itself; simple words can still depend on assumptions the reader does not share.

Q5｜解答例
It proposes identifying the inference the audience must make, then working backward to reveal the necessary but unfamiliar intermediate steps.

Model short answer
Expert explanations can fail because specialists often stop noticing reasoning steps that have become automatic through practice. As a result, an explanation may be factually correct and use simple words while still depending on assumptions that a learner does not share. One way to improve it is to identify the inference the audience must make and reconstruct only the missing steps needed to reach it. However, making every step explicit can also create overload.

## 解説の考え方

### この問題で考えること
説明の分かりにくさを「専門用語が多い」「情報量が多い」だけで説明せず、**話し手には自明になった中間推論が、聞き手には欠けている**という reasoning gap を捉える。

### 考え方
文章の流れは **question → tempting answer → deeper mechanism → repair strategy → limitation**。expertise により intermediate steps が自動化される → specialist はその steps を言語化しなくなる → learner は結論へつながる bridge を失う、という mechanism が中心である。改善策は「全部説明する」ではなく、audience が必要とする inference から逆算して欠けた bridge だけを再構成すること。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× 「難しい単語を簡単な単語に変えれば分かりやすい」  
○ 「語彙が簡単でも、前提や中間推論が共有されていなければ理解できない。必要な推論段階を選んで可視化する」  
改善点は、**surface wording と reasoning structure を分ける**こと。

### 模範解答の読み方
Model short answer は、原因→結果→改善策→限界の順で構成されている。最後の overload は「詳しくするほどよい」という別の単純化を防ぎ、selective reconstruction という主旨へ戻す役割を持つ。

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
説明を改善するときは「何を知らないか」だけでなく、**相手がどの inference を自力では飛べないか**を探す。
