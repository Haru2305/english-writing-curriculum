E189｜匿名フィードバックと公開フィードバック：二制度を評価する

P3 / LT03 / D06 / RQ12 / RF05 / WF05 / WT03 / SS02 / TF16

今日の狙い
匿名性と説明責任のトレードオフを、率直さ・偏り・文脈・責任の共通軸で比較し、条件付き評価を書く。

時間配分
Silent Plan 1分 / Passage A 10分 / Passage B 10分 / Questions 10分 / English Summary 10分 / Evaluation Writing 12分 / Self-check 3分 / Post-solve 5分 ＝ Core 60分

Passage A｜Anonymous Feedback
A university writing course allows students to comment anonymously on classmates’ draft essays. Supporters argue that anonymity can reduce social pressure. A student may be more willing to identify an unclear argument or weak paragraph when the author does not know who wrote the comment.

Anonymity may also reduce some status effects. Comments from a quiet student and a highly confident student appear in the same format, which can encourage authors to judge the content rather than the identity of the reviewer. However, anonymity can weaken accountability. Reviewers may provide vague criticism because they do not expect to explain it later.

Passage B｜Open Feedback
A second course attaches each reviewer’s name to comments and requires a short follow-up discussion. The purpose is not merely to identify the reviewer. Because students know they may need to clarify a comment, they have an incentive to give reasons and examples.

Open feedback can also provide useful context: an author can ask what a reviewer meant and can compare that explanation with the written comment. Yet visible identity may discourage criticism, especially when students differ in confidence or social status. Some reviewers may soften important comments to avoid tension.

Questions
Q1｜RQ12
What goal is shared by both systems?

Q2｜D06
Compare the two systems on candor and accountability.

Q3｜RQ09
Why can anonymity reduce one kind of bias while creating another problem?

Q4｜RQ10
Does Passage B claim that named feedback is always more informative? Explain.

English Summary
Summarize both passages in 70–90 English words using at least two shared comparison axes.

Evaluation Writing
In 90–110 English words, recommend a feedback system for a course that wants both honest criticism and useful justification. Include two criteria and one qualification.

Self-check

解答・解説・自己修正

Q1｜解答例
Both systems aim to help students improve drafts by giving useful peer criticism.

Q2｜解答例
Anonymous feedback may increase candor by reducing social pressure, but it can weaken accountability. Open feedback increases accountability and opportunities for clarification, but visible identity may reduce candor.

Q3｜解答例
It can reduce status effects by hiding reviewer identity, but reviewers may feel less responsible for explaining or defending vague criticism.

Q4｜解答例
No. The passage presents reasons why named feedback can improve explanation, but it also warns that social pressure may suppress important criticism.

Model summary
Anonymous and open peer feedback pursue the same goal but create different trade-offs. Anonymity can reduce social pressure and status effects, encouraging more candid criticism, yet reviewers may feel less accountable for vague comments. Open feedback supports follow-up discussion and stronger justification, but visible identity can discourage criticism when students differ in confidence or status. The key comparison is therefore not privacy versus openness alone, but candor, accountability, and the ability to clarify comments.

Model evaluation
I would use anonymous first-round comments followed by an optional structured clarification stage. Anonymity can make students more willing to identify weaknesses, while a requirement to give reasons and examples would preserve some accountability. Authors who need clarification could submit a question that the reviewer answers without immediately revealing identity. This system balances candor with justification better than either complete anonymity or fully open review. However, if repeated exchanges become necessary, the course may need named discussion so that misunderstandings can be resolved efficiently.
