E188｜野外観測の例外をどう読むか：平均傾向と局所条件を分ける

P3 / LT03 / D10 / RQ09 / RQ13 / RF03 / SS03 / TF08

今日の狙い
表の平均傾向と例外地点を同時に読み、例外を「傾向の否定」ではなく結論を限定する情報として扱う。

時間配分
Silent Plan 1分 / Reading 17分 / Table 9分 / Questions 13分 / Japanese Explanation 12分 / Self-check 3分 / Post-solve 5分 ＝ Core 60分

Reading
[1] A fictional ecology class studies whether tree cover is associated with lower afternoon ground temperature. Students measure four sites on five clear days. The goal is not to prove a universal law but to see whether a pattern appears under ordinary field conditions.

Table
Site | Tree cover | Mean afternoon ground temp. | Surface type | Wind exposure
A | 82% | 26.1°C | soil/leaf litter | low
B | 67% | 27.0°C | soil | medium
C | 31% | 31.8°C | paving | low
D | 18% | 29.4°C | grass | high

[2] Sites A, B, and C show the expected direction: lower tree cover is associated with higher mean ground temperature. Site D, however, is cooler than Site C despite having less tree cover.

[3] That exception does not make the entire comparison useless. Instead, it suggests that tree cover is not the only relevant condition. Site D has grass rather than paving and is more exposed to wind, both of which could alter surface heating.

[4] The class therefore revises its conclusion. Rather than saying that tree cover determines afternoon temperature, students state that greater tree cover is associated with lower temperature in most observed sites, while surface type and wind may modify the relationship.

[5] A stronger follow-up study would compare sites that differ in tree cover while matching other conditions more closely. It could also repeat measurements across different weather conditions. The present observations identify a pattern and a useful exception, but they do not isolate a single cause.

Questions
Q1｜RQ13
Which rows support the general association between tree cover and temperature?

Q2｜D10
What role does Site D play in the argument?

Q3｜RQ09
What can reasonably be inferred from Site D about paving and wind?

Q4｜RQ09
Why would it be too strong to say that tree cover “determines” temperature?

Q5｜D10
Rewrite the conclusion as a qualified claim that preserves both the trend and the exception.

Japanese Explanation
本文と表を使って、「例外データはなぜ傾向を無意味にするのではなく、結論を限定する材料になるのか」を130〜160字で説明する。

Self-check
□ I used the trend and the exception.
□ I did not infer a proven cause from Site D.
□ I turned the exception into a qualification.

---

解答・解説・自己修正

Q1｜解答例
Sites A, B, and C support the general association because tree cover decreases across the three sites while mean afternoon ground temperature increases.

Q2｜解答例
Site D is an exception that prevents a simple one-factor conclusion and points to other conditions that may modify the relationship.

Q3｜解答例
Grass or stronger wind exposure may contribute to lower temperature at Site D, but the data do not isolate which factor matters or prove causation.

Q4｜解答例
Because the sites differ in surface type and wind exposure as well as tree cover, and the observations are not a controlled experiment.

Q5｜解答例
Greater tree cover was associated with lower afternoon ground temperature in most observed sites, although surface type and wind exposure may modify the pattern.

Model Japanese explanation
A〜Cでは樹木被覆が少ないほど地表温度が高い傾向がある一方、Dは被覆が最少でもCより低温である。この例外は傾向全体を消すのではなく、舗装の有無や風など別要因が関係を修飾する可能性を示す。したがって結論は「樹木被覆だけで決まる」ではなく、他条件を含む限定的な関連として述べるべきである。

## 解説の考え方

### この問題で考えること
例外を見つけたときに「傾向は全部間違い」と捨てるのでも、「例外だから無視」とするのでもなく、**何が結論の適用範囲や mechanism を限定するか**を考える。

### 考え方
A–C は tree cover と temperature の association を支持する。D は方向が外れるため、surface type / wind など別条件が関係を修飾する可能性を示す。ここで重要なのは、D だけから grass や wind の因果効果を証明することではなく、**one-factor claim が強すぎる**と分かることである。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× 「Dが外れるのでtree coverは無関係」  
× 「Dは例外なので無視してよい」  
○ 「多くの地点では関連があるが、他条件で関係が変わり得るため、限定的なassociationとして述べる」  
改善点は、例外を **qualification の材料**として使うこと。

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
例外データは、主張を0か1で判定するためだけでなく、**どの条件で弱まるか・別要因が入りそうか・次に何を揃えて調べるか**を教える。


Self-correction
exception → reject everything ではない。exception → qualify mechanism / scope。
