Text A｜Automated public services
A city introduces an automated system to help residents find public services. The system can provide information quickly and offer users several possible routes to support. Officials argue for using it because staff spend less time answering routine questions. However, the system sometimes misunderstands unusual requests, and people with limited digital skills may find it difficult to use. The city therefore keeps a human contact option and reviews cases in which the automated answer may have caused confusion. Staff also examine whether errors occur more often for certain types of requests or for residents who use less common languages. If a repeated pattern appears, the city changes the system rather than assuming each mistake is an isolated case. Automation can therefore reduce routine workload while still requiring monitoring, correction, and an alternative route for people who need human help. The system is treated as an additional channel rather than the only way to obtain assistance.


Text B｜Health-data research
A research group asks patients for permission to use health records in a new study. The original records were collected for clinical care, so the researchers explain the secondary use separately. Some participants argue for broad data sharing because larger datasets can improve research. Others argue against giving unlimited access because sensitive information may be reused in ways that were not expected when the data were first collected. The hospital decides that researchers should use only the information needed for the study and that especially sensitive data require additional protection. It also limits how long the research copy can be retained and requires a new review before the data are used for a substantially different purpose. These rules may reduce some convenience for researchers, but they can make the conditions of use clearer to participants. The hospital does not claim that privacy risk can be eliminated; instead, it tries to keep the expected research benefit while limiting unnecessary access and reuse. The policy also makes future review part of responsible data use rather than a one-time decision.


Text C｜Flood resilience
A coastal town has experienced several years without a major flood. Some residents question whether the town should continue to invest in barriers, drainage, emergency communication, and backup power. Engineers argue that a long quiet period does not remove future risk. They also warn that building a barrier without maintaining pumps and drainage can leave important weaknesses. The town invests additional resources in inspection and training rather than spending the entire budget on new construction. Officials also run evacuation exercises and check whether warnings reach older residents, tourists, and people who do not regularly use smartphones. These activities do not guarantee that a future storm will cause little damage, because the size and location of an event still matter. They can, however, reduce avoidable failures in communication and response. These measures may contribute to lower losses if a severe storm occurs, although the exact benefit cannot be known in advance.

## Writing解説

Short Writing
モデル答案
The automated system can provide information quickly and offer users useful options, but it may misunderstand unusual cases. I would argue for keeping human support available when the cost of an error is high. The city should invest in staff training as well as automation so that efficiency does not reduce access for people who need help.


別解
Health-data sharing can support research, but unlimited access may create privacy risks. I would argue for rules that allow useful research while protecting sensitive information. Hospitals should invest resources in secure data systems and clear consent procedures.

## 解説補強｜Core recap

### 核
導入判断は **benefit → failure risk → where human support remains necessary** で考える

### 模範解答の読み方
automationの速さを認めつつ、unusual casesとhigh-cost errorsを条件にhuman supportを残している

### 次へ
便利さが増えるほど全面置換とは限らない。**どの場面を補完するか**を見る
