Passage
A fictional campus information service publishes status updates for shared facilities. The service uses the idea of a “freshness window.” A freshness window is the period during which an update is considered recent enough to guide a current choice without requiring another check.

The window is not the same for every type of information. A library’s normal opening hours may remain useful for many hours, while seat availability in a study room can change within minutes. [A] The more quickly the underlying situation can change, the shorter the period during which an old update remains dependable.

Dense Sentence
What determines whether an update remains practically useful is not simply the clock time printed beside it but the relationship among how rapidly the underlying condition can change, how inconvenient an outdated choice would be, how frequently new observations can be collected, and whether users have another easy way to confirm the situation before acting.

The service therefore assigns different freshness windows to different status types. A short window means the system should either refresh the information frequently or clearly mark it as needing confirmation. A longer window does not mean the information is permanently correct; it means that change is normally slower or the inconvenience caused by a small delay is lower.

Candidate Sentence
In other words, information ages at different speeds depending on what it describes.

FAQ
Q: Does an update become false as soon as its freshness window ends?
A: No. It becomes less suitable as the sole basis for a current choice and may need confirmation.

Q: Should every status be refreshed every minute?
A: No. Very frequent updates can use staff and system capacity without adding useful information.

Q: Can users rely only on the “last updated” time?
A: No. They also need to know how quickly that type of condition can change.

Table
Status type | Typical change speed | Current refresh interval | Inconvenience if stale | Alternative confirmation
Library hours | Slow | 6 h | Low | Website
Study-room seats | Fast | 10 min | Medium | Door display
Computer-room seats | Medium | 30 min | Medium | Room display
Printer availability | Fast | 45 min | Medium | Nearby sign

解答・解説・自己修正

Q1｜解答例
A freshness window is the period during which an update is recent enough to guide a current choice without another confirmation, given how quickly the underlying situation can change.

Q2｜解答
A. The previous sentences contrast slowly changing information with rapidly changing conditions. The Candidate Sentence states the general principle, and the next sentence explains the consequence: faster-changing conditions require shorter usable windows.

Q3｜解答例
An update’s usefulness depends on more than its timestamp. It depends on how fast the real situation changes and how inconvenient stale information would be. It also depends on how often new observations can be collected and whether users can confirm the situation another way.

Q4｜解答例
Printer availability, because it can change quickly while its current refresh interval is much longer than that of study-room seats.

Q5｜解答例
Library-hour information can probably use a longer refresh interval than rapidly changing availability information because the underlying condition changes slowly and users can also confirm it on the website.

Model summary
A freshness window is the period during which an update remains suitable for a current choice without further confirmation. Its length depends on how quickly conditions change, the inconvenience of stale information, refresh capacity, and alternative confirmation methods. Shorter windows can improve usefulness but require more updating effort. Printer availability may deserve review because it changes quickly while the current refresh interval is relatively long.

Model writing
The service should set refresh frequency by combining observed change speed with the inconvenience caused by stale information. Rapidly changing statuses, such as study-room seats or printer availability, should be refreshed more often than slow-changing information such as library hours. The service should also consider whether users can easily confirm the status another way before increasing update frequency. However, the table does not show how often each status actually changes during different times of day, so the service should collect those data before setting final intervals.
