E176｜河川の渇水対策：高密度文と表から蓄え・流入・需要を読む
P3 / LT03 / A29 / A28 / D04 / RQ09 / RQ13 / RF04 / SS03 / TF08

今日の狙い
高密度文をほどき、貯水量・河川流入・需要の関係から「何日分あるか」だけでは渇水耐性を判断できない理由を説明する。

時間配分
Silent Plan 1分 / Reading 17分 / Dense Sentence 13分 / Table Questions 11分 / Japanese Output 10分 / Self-check 3分 / Post-solve 5分 ＝ Core 60分

Reading
[1] A regional water system stores water in a reservoir and receives additional inflow from a river each day. During a dry period, officials often report the reservoir as a percentage of full capacity. That number is useful, but it is not a complete measure of how long supply can be maintained.

[2] A reservoir at 60% may be relatively secure if river inflow continues and demand is moderate. The same 60% can represent much greater risk if inflow drops sharply while demand remains high.

Dense Sentence
What determines how quickly stored water is depleted is not the starting storage percentage alone but the continuing balance among incoming river flow, household and industrial demand, unavoidable system losses, and any conservation measures that alter demand before the reservoir reaches a level at which operational restrictions become necessary.

[3] Officials compare four fictional weeks.

Table
Week | Starting storage | River inflow | Daily demand | End-of-week storage change
A | 62% | High | Medium | +1%
B | 62% | Low | Medium | -6%
C | 54% | Medium | Low | -1%
D | 70% | Very low | High | -10%

[4] Week A and Week B begin at the same storage level but move in opposite directions because inflow differs. Week D begins with the highest storage yet experiences the largest decline. This shows why a snapshot and a trend answer different questions.

[5] Conservation can slow depletion by reducing demand, but it does not create new water. Likewise, short-term rainfall can increase inflow without guaranteeing that the dry period has ended. Officials therefore monitor both the stock of stored water and the flows entering and leaving the system.

[6] The broader policy question is not simply whether the reservoir is “high” or “low.” It is whether current storage and expected flows provide enough time to respond if dry conditions continue.

解答・解説・自己修正

Q1｜解答例
Starting storage is only one factor. River inflow continues to add water. Demand and system losses remove water. Conservation can change the rate of demand before restrictions become necessary.

Q2｜和訳例
貯留水がどの速さで減るかを決めるのは開始時の貯水率だけではなく、その後も続く河川からの流入、家庭・産業の需要、避けられない系統損失、そして運用上の制限が必要となる水準に達する前に需要を変える節水策のバランスである。

Q3｜解答例
Weeks A and B, because both start at 62% but storage rises in A and falls sharply in B as river inflow differs.

Q4｜解答例
Week D combines very low inflow with high demand, causing rapid depletion despite its higher starting storage. Week C has lower storage but a much more balanced flow-demand situation.

Q5｜解答例
Conservation can reduce the speed of depletion by lowering demand, but it cannot replace lost inflow or guarantee long-term water security.

Model Japanese output
渇水リスクは開始時の貯水率だけでなく、その後の河川流入、需要、損失、節水による需要変化の収支で決まる。同じ貯水率でも流入が少なければ急減し、高い貯水率でも高需要が続けば短期間で悪化するため、量と変化の両方を見る必要がある。

## 解説の考え方

### この問題で考えること
判断すべきなのは「今どれだけ水があるか」だけではなく、**その量が今後どの方向・速度で変わるか**である。snapshot と trend を分ける。

### 考え方
渇水耐性は、**現在の stock + inflow − demand / loss** の収支で考える。開始貯水率が同じ A と B が逆方向へ動くことから、stock だけでは不十分だと分かる。さらに D は開始値が最も高いのに大きく減るため、「高い開始値＝安全」という判断も崩れる。節水は demand を下げて減少速度を遅くするが、inflow を作るわけではないので効果の範囲も限定する必要がある。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× 「70%あるDは54%のCより安全」  
○ 「Dは very low inflow と high demand が重なり、Cより速く減っているため、現時点の量だけでは安全性を比較できない」  
改善点は、**水準をそのまま結論にせず、変化を生む mechanism を読む**こと。

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
stock-flow 型の問題では、**現在量 / 入る量 / 出る量 / 変化させる介入**を分ける。病床、在庫、電力貯蔵、資金余力などでも同じ読み方が使える。
