Notice｜Crowding Management at the Civic Learning Center
During high-demand weekends, the center may use timed entry for the study hall and computer area. The purpose is to keep waiting predictable while maintaining access to essential services.

Possible measures:
• 90-minute study-hall sessions during peak periods
• online reservation of selected computer seats
• a same-day waiting list when reservations are full
• priority access for visitors with scheduled public-service appointments
• temporary suspension of new entry when safe indoor capacity is reached

FAQ
Q: Does a timed session mean everyone must leave after 90 minutes?
A: During peak periods, yes, unless unused capacity allows an extension.

Q: Can visitors enter without a reservation?
A: Yes. Some seats remain available for walk-in users, but availability is not guaranteed.

Q: Are all areas controlled by the same rule?
A: No. Reading rooms, public-service counters, and event spaces may use different procedures.

Q: If I leave briefly, can I automatically return to the same seat?
A: No. Re-entry depends on current capacity and the area’s rule.

解答・解説・自己修正

Q1｜解答例
Because capacity, waiting time, and walk-in availability change by time, and some areas switch to timed sessions or reservation rules during peaks.

Q2｜解答例
The 90-minute limit applies during peak periods, but a session may be extended when unused capacity remains.

Q3｜解答例
15:00 is the strongest peak in the table: occupancy is almost full, average waiting is longest, and few walk-in seats remain.

Q4｜解答例
The areas serve different purposes and have different capacity, appointment, equipment, and safety needs, so one access rule would not fit all of them.

Model Japanese summary
週末の混雑時には、安全な定員と待ち時間の予測可能性を保つため、学習席の90分制や一部端末の予約制を用いる。15時頃は特に混雑し、当日利用は保証されない。ただし空席があれば利用延長も可能で、窓口や閲覧室などは目的に応じて別の入場ルールを採用する。

Self-correction
「予約制」「90分制」を施設全体の固定ルールにしない。time × area × capacity の条件を残す。
