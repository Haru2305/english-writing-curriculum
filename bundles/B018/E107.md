Reading

[1] Educational interventions are often evaluated by comparing average outcomes before and after a program, but such comparisons can be difficult to interpret when participation itself is selective. Students who choose an optional support program may differ from nonparticipants in motivation, prior achievement, available time, or willingness to ask for help.



[2] The resulting uncertainty is not removed simply by collecting more outcome data. What matters is whether the additional evidence helps distinguish the effect of the program from the characteristics of the people who entered it. A larger sample can make an estimate more precise while leaving the underlying comparison biased.



[3] This is why researchers sometimes prefer a design in which access to a program is determined by a rule not controlled by the student—for example, random assignment, a capacity lottery, or a threshold that creates otherwise similar groups on opposite sides. Such designs are not automatically perfect: people may fail to use the program they were offered, and the estimated effect may apply only to students near the threshold or within the study conditions.



[4] The value of a stronger design, then, lies not in the elimination of every complication but in the clearer separation of alternative explanations. Evidence becomes more useful when it narrows the set of plausible reasons for an observed difference, even if some uncertainty remains about how broadly the result applies.



Questions

Q1｜A29

In paragraph 2, expand “the underlying comparison biased” into a fuller clause.



Q2｜RQ10

Why can a larger sample make an estimate more precise without making the causal interpretation stronger?



Q3｜RQ09

What can reasonably be inferred about stronger research designs from paragraph 4?



Japanese Output

Writing Task

In 70–90 English words, explain why “more data” and “better evidence” are not always the same thing in this passage.

解答・解説・自己修正



Reading answers

Q1｜解答例

while the comparison on which the estimate is based remains biased / while the groups being compared still differ systematically.



Q2｜解答例

Because precision concerns how narrowly an effect is estimated, whereas causal strength depends on whether alternative explanations and selection differences have been separated.



Q3｜解答例

Their value lies in ruling out or narrowing alternative explanations more clearly, not in removing every limitation.



A28/A29｜構造保持

Paragraph 3では colon後の

people may fail...

and

the estimated effect may apply only...

が並列のlimitations。

“the program they were offered”のtheyはpeople。



RF04｜英文和訳

Model translation

このような研究デザインも自動的に完全なものになるわけではない。参加者が提供されたプログラムを実際には利用しないこともあり、推定された効果が、基準値の近くにいる学生や、その研究条件の範囲内にいる学生にしか当てはまらない可能性もある。



Model answer

More data can make an estimate more precise without making the comparison more credible. If students who enter a program differ systematically from those who do not, a larger sample may measure that difference very accurately while still mixing the program’s effect with selection factors. Better evidence must therefore help separate competing explanations—for example, by creating more comparable groups—not merely increase the number of observations. That is the central methodological distinction.



Self-correction

和訳ではmay / only、日本語の主語、修飾先を確認。英語ではprecisionとcausal interpretationを別概念として書けているか確認する。
