Summary-linked Guide

1. audience

2. purpose

4. uncertainty

を保つ。

原文の順番をそのまま短くする必要はない。



Reading

[1] Research institutions often publish short summaries of new studies for readers who will never see the full academic paper. The challenge is not simply replacing technical terms with easier words. A summary must decide which details are necessary for the reader to understand the significance of the result and which details can be omitted without changing its meaning.



[2] Consider a study that finds an association between two behaviors. The original paper may spend many pages discussing measurement methods, statistical models, and alternative explanations. A public summary cannot reproduce all of that. Yet if it removes every limitation and writes only that one behavior “leads to” the other, the summary becomes easier to read by becoming less accurate.



[3] Good compression therefore preserves the relationship between the main finding and its uncertainty. It may replace several methodological details with a phrase such as “after accounting for several measured differences,” while still explaining that the study does not prove a causal effect. This is selective compression rather than simplification at any cost.



[4] Audience also changes what deserves space. A specialist may need information about sample construction or measurement error. A general reader may instead need a clear explanation of what was observed, what was not established, and why the finding might matter. Neither audience needs every fact in the same order.



[5] The writer must also distinguish importance from novelty. A surprising detail can attract attention without being central to the study’s conclusion. If the detail occupies too much of a short summary, the reader may remember what was unusual and miss what the research actually showed.



[6] A strong short summary is therefore a reconstruction, not a miniature copy. It preserves the main claim, the most important limitation, and the reason the result matters, while reorganizing the information for the intended reader.

Model summary

A good public research summary does more than replace difficult words with simple ones. It selects the information a general reader needs to understand the main finding, why it matters, and what remains uncertain. Important limitations should not disappear merely to make the message shorter; turning an association into “leads to” would read more easily but misstate the evidence. Because different audiences need different details, effective compression reorganizes the original information instead of copying the paper in miniature.

## 解説の深掘り

### この問題で考えること
要約は「各文を短くする作業」ではない。**読み手と目的に合わせて、main finding・limitation・significanceの関係を壊さず再構成する作業**。

### 考え方
1. audience を決める：今回はgeneral reader。
2. central relation を一文で言う：good compression preserves meaning while selecting detail。
3. 消してはいけないlimitationを一つ残す：associationをcausationへ強めない。
4. significanceを残す：なぜその結果を知る価値があるか。
5. 原文順を捨てて、上の機能が自然につながる順へ並べ直す。

「驚くdetail」は目立っても、central relationを支えないなら削れる。逆にlimitationは地味でも、消すとclaimの意味が変わるので残す。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× 各段落から一文ずつ抜いて短くする  
○ main claim＋重要なlimitation＋significanceを、audience向けに新しい順序で書き直す

また、association を leads to に変えるのは単なる言い換えではなく、**証拠が支えるclaimを強くしすぎる変更**になる。

### 模範summaryの読み方
- 第1文：compressionの核心を提示
- 第2文：残す情報を main finding / significance / uncertainty に整理
- 第3文：短さのためにlimitationを捨てないという境界
- 第4文：audienceによってdetail選択と再配置が変わることを示す

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
長文要約では、**「何を削るか」より先に「何を失うと意味が変わるか」**を決める。研究広報だけでなく、記事要約、政策説明、英語abstract、資料付き英作文にも使える。
