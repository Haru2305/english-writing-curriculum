Reading

[1] A museum introduced timed-entry reservations after weekend crowding made some galleries difficult to use. Visitors can choose a two-hour arrival window online, and a small number of same-day tickets are kept for people who arrive without a reservation.



[2] The system reduced the largest entrance queues, but staff noticed a new problem. Some visitors reserve several possible times and use only one, while other people see “sold out” online even though unused capacity remains later. The museum does not want to remove flexibility entirely because travel delays and family schedules can make exact arrival times uncertain.



[3] A useful reform would therefore target unused reservations rather than simply make the rules stricter for everyone. One idea is a confirmation request sent on the morning of the visit. Reservations not confirmed by a certain time could return to the same-day pool. Another idea is to shorten every arrival window, which might reduce uncertainty for the museum but make the system harder for visitors traveling from far away.



FAQ

Q: If I arrive late, is my reservation automatically cancelled?

A: No. Staff may admit late visitors if capacity allows, but entry at the original time is not guaranteed.



Q: Can I hold two reservations for the same day?

A: The current system allows it, although the museum asks visitors to cancel any reservation they will not use.



Q: Who mainly uses same-day tickets?

A: Staff observations suggest they are especially important for visitors who did not know about the online system or who could not plan their arrival far in advance.



Notice

Next-month test:

For four Saturdays, reservation holders will receive a confirmation message at 08:00. Unconfirmed slots will be released at 11:00 to the same-day ticket pool. Arrival-window length will not change during the test.



Questions

Q1｜RQ09

What problem is the next-month test mainly designed to measure?



Q2｜RQ12

How do the FAQ and Notice together show a trade-off between flexibility and access?

Reading answers

Q1｜解答例

It is mainly designed to test whether unused reservations can be identified early enough to release capacity for other visitors without changing the arrival-window system.



Q2｜解答例

The FAQ shows that the current rules are flexible (two holds are allowed and late visitors may still be admitted) and that same-day tickets matter for people who cannot plan ahead. The Notice widens same-day access by releasing unconfirmed slots at 11:00, but at the cost of flexibility for reservation holders, who may lose a slot if they miss the 08:00 message or arrive late.

Model answer

The museum should continue the confirmation test because it targets unused reservations without immediately making arrival windows stricter for everyone. Same-day tickets matter for visitors who cannot plan far in advance or did not know about the online system, so releasing unconfirmed slots could improve access. However, some visitors may miss the morning message even though they still intend to come. The museum should monitor how many slots are released, how many are reused, and how many visitors who did not confirm arrive and find their slot already released. Those results would show whether the system improves access without creating a new barrier. That balance matters.

## 解説の深掘り

### この問題で考えること
論点は「予約を厳しくするか」ではない。**柔軟性をできるだけ残しながら、使われない予約枠を必要な人へ戻せるか**を考える。

### 考え方
1. problemを分ける：crowding自体は改善したが、unused reservations が見かけ上のsold outを作る。
2. accessの意味を確認する：same-day ticketsは、事前計画が難しい人やonline systemを知らなかった人に重要。
3. mechanismを作る：morning confirmation → 来ない可能性が高い枠を早く特定 → 11:00にsame-day poolへ戻す → unused capacityを再配分。
4. safeguardを見る：arrival window自体は短くしないので、全員のflexibilityを削る案より限定的。
5. 新しいbarrierも考える：来る予定でもmessageを見逃す人がいる。
6. measureを置く：released slots / reused slots / unexpected loss を追えば、benefitとharmを同じtestで評価できる。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× 「multiple reservationsを禁止すればよい」  
○ 「unused slotsをconfirmationで回収し、same-day accessへ再配分する。見逃しによる誤取消しもmonitorする」

前者はproblemを解く可能性はあるが、遠方移動や家族予定の不確実性というflexibilityの価値を消してしまう。

### 答案の組み立て
- **Position**：testを継続
- **Reason**：unused capacityをaccessへ戻せる
- **Mechanism**：confirmation → release → reuse
- **Support**：FAQが示すsame-day利用者の事情
- **Qualification**：message missという新しいbarrier
- **Return**：reuseとunexpected lossを測って継続判断

### 模範解答の読み方
第1文がPosition＋設計上の利点、第2文がsame-day accessのReason、第3文がQualification、第4文が測定すべきSupport、第5〜6文がevaluation criterionとReturn。単に「便利だから続ける」ではなく、**benefitと新しいbarrierを同じpilotで測る**構造になっている。

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
予約・申請制度では、**ルールを一律に厳しくする前に、無駄になっているcapacityをどう再配分できるか**を考える。病院予約、大学施設、公共サービスにも転用できる。
