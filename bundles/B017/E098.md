Source A

[1] A suburban town is testing a demand-responsive minibus that can deviate from a fixed route when a passenger makes a reservation. Supporters expect the service to improve access for residents who live far from regular bus stops, especially older people and households without cars.



[2] The pilot has two possible measures of success. One is operational efficiency: whether the bus can serve additional requests without creating large delays for other passengers. The other is access: whether people who previously traveled infrequently are actually able to make more trips. A high passenger count alone cannot distinguish these goals because frequent existing riders may account for much of the increase.



[3] Reservations also create a participation barrier. Some residents are comfortable using a smartphone form, while others prefer telephone booking. Requiring only one method could reduce administrative complexity but also exclude people the service is intended to reach.



[4] The town therefore plans to evaluate not only total ridership but also the number of first-time or previously infrequent users, average deviation time, failed reservation attempts, and the method used to book. A useful pilot should reveal which constraint limits access, not simply whether the buses are busy.

Notice

Pilot booking update:

From next month, telephone reservations will be accepted until 20:00 on weekdays. Smartphone reservations remain available at all times. The town will compare booking completion rates before and after the change. The route area and number of vehicles will not change during this period.

解答・解説・自己修正



Reading answers

Q1

An increase in first-time or previously infrequent users most directly supports improved access.



Q2｜解答例

The Notice turns telephone booking hours into a testable variable. If failed telephone reservations fall after the hours are extended while route area and vehicle count stay constant, the earlier booking window becomes a plausible barrier.



Q3｜解答例

The longer telephone window may have reduced a booking barrier even if the service did not attract many additional riders overall.



Q4｜解答例

Because a high passenger count can come from frequent existing riders and does not show whether previously excluded or infrequent residents gained access.

## 解説の深掘り

### この問題で考えること
中心は「利用者が増えたか」ではなく、**これまで使いにくかった人のaccessが改善したか、その障壁をどう特定するか**。total ridership と access を同じものとして扱わない。

### 考え方
Source Aでまず「成功」の定義を決める。Tableはその定義に照らした観測結果、Noticeは一つの運用条件を変える次のtestとして読む。

- Source A：accessは first-time / infrequent users や failed requests で見る
- Table：telephone requests の失敗率が smartphone より高い
- Notice：telephone booking hours だけを延長し、route area と vehicles は変えない
- 推論：延長後にtelephone completionが改善すれば、以前の受付時間がbarrierだったという説明が強くなる

ここで大切なのは、**変えたものと変えていないものを分けること**。条件を一つ動かすことで、単なる同時変化よりmechanismを検討しやすくなる。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× 「ridershipが19%増えたのでaccessは改善した」  
○ 「first-time / infrequent users が増えたことはaccess改善により直接的で、telephone failureの高さは残るbarrierを示す」

total ridershipは既存の頻繁な利用者だけでも増えるため、目的に対するproxyとして粗い。

### 混合資料の処理手順
**問いを先に固定 → 各sourceの役割を決める → 同じ問いに必要な証拠だけ接続 → claimの強さを調整**、の順で読む。資料ごとの要約を三つ作ってから統合しようとすると遅くなる。

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
制度変更の資料では、**outcomeを定義し、どの変数を変え、何を固定したかを見る**。予約制度、オンライン申請、健診参加、公共交通などで再利用できる。
