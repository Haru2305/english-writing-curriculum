P3最初のCheckpointとして、連続文・短い立場資料・表を統合し、推論・比較・要約・優先順位付き英作文を60分で処理する。新規Focusは置かない。



時間配分

Checkpoint Plan 3分 / Source A 12分 / Source B 7分 / Data Table 6分 / Questions 9分 / Summary Task 8分 / Writing Task 12分 / Self-check 2分 / Post-solve 1分 ＝ Core 60分



Checkpoint Plan

出力ごとに情報を分ける。

Source A

[1] A university is considering how to improve access to its shared study-support program. The current program offers individual appointments with trained advisers, but demand is concentrated in the two weeks before major examinations. During ordinary weeks, many appointment slots remain unused.



[2] One proposed change is to add large drop-in workshops before exams. These could serve more students at once, but they would provide less individualized help. Another proposal is to spread shorter group sessions across the term. This might reduce last-minute demand if students use the sessions early, but it requires staff time even when attendance is uncertain.



[3] The university’s goal is not simply to maximize attendance. It wants to reach students who currently seek help too late to make substantial changes to their study habits. A successful pilot should therefore be judged partly by timing: whether participants begin using support earlier in the term.



[4] Evaluation should also distinguish reach from intensity. A large workshop may reach many new students but provide limited interaction, while individual appointments provide intensive support to fewer people. Neither measure is sufficient alone. The university needs evidence about which format changes behavior at the point where additional support can still influence later study.



[5] The next pilot should therefore make the timing and type of participation visible. Counting total attendance without recording when students first engage would answer the wrong question.



Source B

Student representative:

“Exam-period workshops would make support visible to students who have never booked an appointment. Even brief contact may encourage some of them to seek more help later.”



Adviser:

“Earlier small-group sessions may have lower attendance, but they give students time to change their routines before pressure becomes severe. We should not confuse a crowded event with earlier engagement.”



Data Table

Fictional pilot forecasts



Option | Expected participants | First contact before final 2 weeks | Staff hours/week

A: exam workshops | 180 | 28% | 12

B: term group sessions | 95 | 64% | 16

C: more appointments | 70 | 41% | 18



Questions

Q2｜RQ13

Q3｜RQ09

Q1｜解答例

The student representative emphasizes broad visibility and reaching new students near exams, while the adviser emphasizes earlier engagement that leaves time for behavior change.



Q2

Option B, because it is predicted to produce the highest share of participants whose first contact occurs before the final two weeks, which directly matches the stated goal.



Q3｜解答例

Because the university defines success partly by earlier engagement, not total attendance, so a smaller program can outperform a crowded one if it changes when students first seek help.

Model summary

The university wants study support to reach students earlier, not simply attract the largest possible audience. Different formats trade broad reach against more intensive help, so total attendance alone cannot measure success. A useful pilot should record when students first engage and what type of support they receive, allowing the university to judge whether the program changes behavior while there is still time to improve study habits.



Model answer

The university should pilot Option B because earlier engagement is the strongest criterion. Source A argues that total attendance can hide whether support reaches students soon enough to change their study habits, and the adviser makes the same point. Although Option A is expected to attract more students, the table shows that Option B produces a much higher share of early first contact. The trade-off is lower overall reach. The university should therefore track both the number of new participants and the proportion who first use support before the final two weeks. That comparison would show whether earlier contact improves actual support use.

## 解説の深掘り

### この問題で考えること
Checkpointの核心は、資料を全部要約することではなく、**出力ごとに必要な情報を切り替えながら、同じdecision criterionを失わないこと**。このsetでは「最大参加者数」ではなく「十分早い時期のengagement」が中心基準になる。

### 考え方
Source Aからまずcriterionを抜く：supportは「いつ使い始めたか」が重要。  
Source Bは二つの価値を対比する：visibility / reach と earlier behavior change。  
Tableではそのcriterionに直接対応する First contact before final 2 weeks を優先して比較する。

Option Bは参加者数95でAの180より少ないが、early first contactは64%でAの28%より高い。  
**早く接触する → 試験直前になる前に学習習慣を変える時間が残る**、というmechanismがあるため、大学の目的にはBが合う。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× 「Option Aは180人で最大だから成功しそう」  
○ 「大学のgoalはattendance最大化ではなくearlier engagementなので、64%が早期接触するOption Bを優先する」

表の最大値を探すだけでは、**Taskが指定した成功基準と違う列を最適化する**危険がある。

### SummaryとWritingを混ぜない
- **Summary**：Source Aが何を主張しているかを圧縮する。自分のchoiceは入れない。
- **Writing**：Aのcriterionを使い、BとTableを統合してdecisionを出す。

同じ資料でも、output functionが違えば残す情報が変わる。

### 模範解答の読み方
第1文はPosition、第2文はSource Aから取ったReason、第3文はadviserによる補強、第4文はA/B比較＋table evidence、第5文はtrade-off、第6〜7文は何を測るかとReturn。特に「earlier engagement → behavior changeの時間が残る」というmechanismが、人数の少なさを上回る判断理由になっている。

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
混合資料のdecisionでは、**Taskから成功基準を一つ固定し、全sourceと全optionをその基準へ戻す**。reach、cost、qualityなど別の数値が目立っていても、goalと一致する指標を優先する。
