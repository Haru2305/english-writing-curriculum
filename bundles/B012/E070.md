Passage A
Requiring reviewers to verify their identity can improve accountability. A person who knows that a platform can confirm who submitted a review may be less willing to create multiple false accounts or post claims with no connection to an actual experience. Verification can also help a platform investigate coordinated manipulation. For these reasons, some form of identity check is likely to reduce certain kinds of abuse.
But verification does not make a review automatically reliable. A verified person can still misunderstand an experience, exaggerate, or judge a service according to unusual expectations. A platform should therefore treat identity as one signal among several. Purchase records, patterns across many reviews, and clear rules for evidence may also matter.
Passage B
Strong identity requirements can create a different problem: people may avoid reviewing sensitive services if they believe their real identity will be exposed or stored unnecessarily. Someone describing a difficult medical, financial, or workplace experience may have a legitimate reason to separate a public comment from a legal name. A platform that demands extensive identification could therefore reduce participation precisely where firsthand accounts are valuable.
This does not mean that anonymous posting should have no safeguards. Platforms can limit repeated accounts, detect unusual patterns, or privately verify that a transaction occurred without publicly displaying the reviewer’s identity. The better question is not whether identity matters, but which level of verification is proportionate to the risk.
Passage A continued
Verification can also vary in strength. Confirming that a reviewer completed a transaction is different from requiring a government-issued identity document, and the two checks address different risks. A platform can therefore increase accountability without treating verification as a single all-or-nothing choice.
Passage B continued
The same distinction matters for privacy. A reviewer may be privately verified by the platform while remaining publicly anonymous to other users. Separating private verification from public identification can preserve some accountability while reducing the social cost of speaking about sensitive experiences.
