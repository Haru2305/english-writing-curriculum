E068｜録画授業は学習機会を広げるか：2つの立場を比較する
P2 / LT01 / RQ12 複数本文比較 / SS02 / A08・RQ10 Transfer / TF05
今日の狙い
2本の独立した英文を片方ずつ要約して終わらず、共通の評価軸を作って一致点・相違点・確信度を比較する。
時間配分
Compare Guide 6分 / Passage A 10分 / Passage B 10分 / Comparison Map 10分 / Questions 10分 / Output 6分 / Self-check 4分 / Post-solve 4分 ＝ Core 60分
Compare Guide
1. AとBのtopicをそろえる
2. 共通の評価軸を1〜2個作る
3. each author’s claim + evidence + limitationを並べる
4. agree / disagree だけでなく「どこまで強く言うか」も比較する
Passage A
Recorded lectures can widen access to learning because students are no longer limited to one time and place. A student who is ill, commuting, caring for a family member, or reviewing a difficult concept can return to the material later. Recordings may also help learners pause, replay, and check unfamiliar terms. For these reasons, making recordings available is likely to improve flexibility.
However, flexibility should not be confused with complete substitution. A recording cannot reproduce every question, discussion, or spontaneous explanation that occurs in a live class. Nor does access guarantee that students will use the material effectively. Recorded lectures should therefore be treated as an additional route into a course rather than as automatic evidence that attendance no longer matters.
Passage B
The strongest case for recorded lectures is not convenience alone but control over study pace. In a live class, everyone receives information at the same speed. A recording allows students to slow down when the material is dense and move quickly through what they already understand. This can make review more efficient and may reduce the disadvantage faced by students who need more processing time.
Yet recordings can also encourage postponement. When material is always available “later,” some students may delay review until the amount becomes difficult to manage. The value of recording therefore depends partly on course design: clear deadlines, short review tasks, and links between recorded material and later discussion may help students use flexibility productively.
Passage A continued
The practical value of recording also depends on what happens around the recording. If students know that difficult material will be discussed again, they may use the video to prepare questions rather than merely replay explanations. In that case, recorded and live learning can reinforce each other instead of competing.
Passage B continued
Course design can also make the timing of review visible. Short prompts or small tasks linked to each recording can create reasons to return to the material before a final examination. The recording remains flexible, but the surrounding structure reduces the chance that flexibility becomes indefinite delay.

D. There is no meaningful difference.

Reading answers
Q1｜B
両者ともrecordingsに便益を認める一方、availabilityだけで成果が決まるとは言わない。
Q2｜A
Aはaccess＋substitutionの限界、Bはpace control＋procrastination riskが中心。
Q3｜解答例
Passage A makes the somewhat stronger claim about flexibility because it says recordings are “likely to improve flexibility.” Passage B uses “can” and “may,” which keep its claims more conditional.
Q4｜解答例
Both authors see recorded lectures as useful additions to learning, but they emphasize different benefits and risks. Passage A focuses on access and the limits of replacing live classes, whereas Passage B focuses on study pace and the need to prevent postponement.
