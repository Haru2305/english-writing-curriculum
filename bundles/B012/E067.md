Reading
[1] Public health messages often fail for a simple reason: giving people more information does not necessarily make the desired action easier. A reminder to attend a health check may be perfectly accurate, yet a person can still postpone the visit because the booking process is confusing, the available hours are inconvenient, or the message does not explain what will happen during the appointment. Communication therefore works best when information and action are designed together.
[2] One useful approach is to reduce the number of decisions a reader must make after receiving a message. Instead of saying only that a check is important, a notice can explain who should consider attending, how long the visit usually takes, what preparation is needed, and where an appointment can be made. These details do not force anyone to act. They reduce uncertainty between understanding a recommendation and carrying it out.
[3] The wording of the message also matters. A warning that sounds too certain can be misleading when the actual risk varies among individuals. A message that is too vague, however, may leave readers unsure about whether the advice applies to them. Effective communication has to balance accuracy with usability. It should distinguish what is known, what depends on personal circumstances, and what action is reasonable under uncertainty.
[4] This means that success should not be measured only by whether people remember the message. A useful notice should support an appropriate next step while preserving the reader’s ability to decide. Better communication is therefore not simply more persuasive communication. It connects reliable information to a clear, manageable choice.
[5] The same principle applies when messages are personalized. A general reminder can be brief, while a message aimed at a particular group may need to explain why the recommendation is relevant without implying that every member of the group faces the same risk. Personalization can make information easier to act on, but only if the basis for the advice is transparent enough for readers to understand. Otherwise, a message may feel precise while hiding the uncertainty behind it.

解答・解説・自己修正
Reading answers
Q1｜解答例
An accurate reminder may still fail if practical barriers or uncertainty make the recommended action difficult to carry out. Clear information does not automatically remove those barriers.
Q2｜解答例
個人差のあるリスクを過度に断定せず、かといって曖昧すぎて対象や行動が分からなくならないよう、正確さと使いやすさを両立させること。
Q3｜解答例
したがって、よりよいコミュニケーションとは、単に相手をより強く説得することではない。信頼できる情報を、分かりやすく実行可能な選択へと結び付けることである。
Q4｜B
Paragraph 1のproblem、2のaction design、3のaccuracy/usability balance、4の結論を最も広くまとめる。
A23｜形式適応
同じ理解から出力形式を切り替える。
RF02では英語で短く再構成。
RF03では採点可能なcontent unitsと関係を日本語で明示。
RF04では指定文の構造・限定・対比を落とさず自然な日本語へ。
設問形式が変わるたびに本文理解をゼロから作り直さない。
