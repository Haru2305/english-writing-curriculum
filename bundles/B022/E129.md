E129｜夜間学習スペース：問いと答えを保って日本語に圧縮する

P3 / LT03 / D07 / RF06 / TF05



今日の狙い

question→answer構造を読み取り、その関係を壊さず日本語要約へ変換する。途中の条件や限定を落として単純な賛否にしない。



時間配分

Guided Integration 5分 / Reading 18分 / Questions 10分 / Question-Answer Map 7分 / Summary Task 12分 / Self-check 3分 / Post-solve 5分 ＝ Core 60分



Reading

[1] A university opened several classrooms until midnight during the final month of the semester. The change followed a simple question: would longer opening hours improve access to quiet study space? Before the trial, students often reported that the main library became crowded in the evening, especially before examinations.



[2] The first answer seemed obvious. The late rooms were used regularly, and many students said they appreciated having another place to work. Usage was highest between 8 p.m. and 10 p.m. Students who lived in shared housing were especially likely to use the rooms because they had fewer quiet options at home.



[3] However, the trial raised a second question: who could actually benefit from the extra hours? Students who depended on the last bus often left campus before the rooms became most useful. Some students with part-time jobs also said that they arrived too late to use the service for long. Longer opening hours increased the supply of study space, but they did not automatically create equal access to it.



[4] Safety and staffing also affected the answer. The university had initially planned to keep every participating building open. Instead, it concentrated late access in two buildings near the main gate, where lighting and security staff were already available. This reduced operating costs and made it easier for students to find help if a problem occurred.



[5] Survey responses suggested that the best policy was not simply “open more rooms for longer.” Students valued predictable availability, clear information about which rooms were open, and locations that connected reasonably well with transport. In other words, the original question about opening hours became a broader question about usable access.



[6] The trial therefore supports a qualified answer. Longer hours can improve study access for students who need quiet space in the evening, but the effect depends on transport, location, information, and safety. A useful policy should be judged not only by the number of extra hours provided but also by whether the intended users can realistically take advantage of them.



Questions

Q1｜D07

What is the passage’s initial question, and how does it become more specific?



Q2｜RQ07

What function does paragraph 3 serve?



Q3｜RQ11

Which information should remain in a Japanese summary, and which examples can be omitted?



Summary Task

本文を130〜160字の日本語で要約する。

「最初の問い」「試行の結果」「利用可能性を左右した条件」「最終回答」を含める。



Self-check

□ 問いと答えの対応が残っている。

□ 長時間開室＝全員に公平、と単純化していない。

□ 具体例を圧縮し、条件をまとめた。

□ 日本語として一つの説明になっている。

解答・解説・自己修正



Reading answers

Q1｜解答例

The initial question is whether longer hours improve access to study space. It becomes a question about which students can realistically benefit from those hours.



Q2｜解答例

It qualifies the initial positive result by showing that transport and work schedules limit who can use the service.



Q3｜解答例

Keep the purpose, evidence that some students benefited, the main access constraints, and the qualified conclusion. Individual examples can be compressed into broader conditions.



D07 × RF06

問いを要約から消すと、本文がなぜその情報を並べたかが見えにくくなる。

question → evidence → limitation → qualified answer

を日本語で一つの因果・判断の流れへ変換する。



Model summary

大学は、長時間開室が静かな学習場所の確保につながるかを確かめるため、期末に教室を深夜まで開放した。夜間の利用は多く共同住宅の学生に役立ったが、最終バスや仕事の都合で使えない学生もおり、時間を延ばすだけでは公平な利用にならなかった。開室時間の長さより、交通や場所、安全面を含めて実際に使えるかで評価すべきだと分かった。



Self-correction

「利用された」という結果だけで終わらず、誰が利用できたかという第二の問いを残す。



到達目安

B：主要情報は残るが、問いか限定のどちらかが弱い。

C：段落ごとの訳を短く並べるだけになる。
