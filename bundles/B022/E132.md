Source A

A community center started a low-cost repair program for small household items. Before the launch, a survey found strong interest: many residents said they would prefer repairing a lamp, toaster, or small appliance to throwing it away. Three months later, however, actual use of the service was much lower than expected. The main question became clear: why did stated interest fail to become participation?



One explanation was convenience. The program operated only on two weekday afternoons each month. Residents who worked during those hours could not easily attend, and the center did not accept items in advance. Another explanation was uncertainty. Some people did not know which items were eligible, how long repairs might take, or whether they would be charged if an item could not be fixed.



The center first responded by advertising the environmental value of repair more strongly. This increased awareness but produced only a small change in use. Staff then simplified the eligibility list, added examples of common repairs, and allowed residents to reserve a time slot online or by phone. They also introduced one Saturday session each month.



These changes did not prove that every interested resident would use the service. Some items were too damaged to repair economically, and some residents still preferred replacement. Yet participation rose most among people who had previously reported uncertainty about the process. The result suggested that positive attitudes toward repair were not enough when the practical path from interest to action remained unclear.

Reading answers

Q1｜解答例

Why did strong stated interest in repair fail to become actual participation?



Q2｜解答例

A vs B isolates knowledge of eligibility rules among people who could attend; A vs C isolates ability to attend among people who knew the rules.



Q3｜解答例

The technician emphasizes clear limits on what can be repaired, while the resident adviser emphasizes making the process understandable and usable.



Q4｜解答例

Positive attitudes do not automatically produce participation; practical access requires clear information, usable schedules, and realistic service boundaries.



Model Japanese summary

修理への関心は高かったが、実際の利用は低かった。対象品や費用、手順が分かりにくいことに加え、実施が平日の昼間だけで、働く人が来られなかったためである。表でも、規則を理解し時間も合う群の利用率は61%だが、どちらかが欠ける群は2割前後にとどまる。制度は情報と時間の障壁を下げつつ、修理できる範囲の限界も明確に示す必要がある。



Model English output

The program was underused because interest alone did not remove practical barriers. Residents who knew the eligibility rules used the service far more often than those who did not (61% versus 24%). The center should first make the process predictable by clearly listing eligible items, likely costs, and booking steps. A weekend session could then help people who knew the rules but could not attend on weekdays (Group C, 19%). Clear access matters more than simply repeating the environmental message.
別解：時間の障壁を優先し、A vs C（61% vs 19%）を引いて週末枠の拡大を提案する答案も可。
