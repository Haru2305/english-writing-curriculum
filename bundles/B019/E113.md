Reading

[1] A city sends residents invitations to a free preventive health check. Staff initially assumed that people who did not attend had simply decided that the check was unnecessary. Interviews suggested a more complicated picture. Some residents had not understood whether the invitation applied to them, while others wanted to attend but found the available times difficult.



[2] This distinction matters because different barriers require different responses. A clearer letter may help people who are uncertain about eligibility, but it does not create a usable appointment time. Evening appointments may improve schedule access, but they will not help residents who never understood the purpose of the invitation.



[3] Participation should therefore be interpreted cautiously. A higher attendance rate after one change is consistent with the idea that the change reduced a barrier, but it does not prove that the barrier was the only reason for nonattendance. Several barriers can exist at the same time, and the people affected by each one may differ.



FAQ

Q: Who can use the check?

A: Residents aged 40–69 who received an invitation can book one free appointment during the program period.



Q: Are evening appointments available?

A: At present, appointments end at 17:00. The city is considering two trial evenings next month.



Q: If I do not attend, does that mean I refused the service?

A: No. The city records nonattendance but does not assume a reason unless additional information is collected.

Reading answers

Q1

It refers to the difference between not understanding eligibility/purpose and wanting to attend but being unable to use the available times.



Q2｜解答例

Because an attendance increase after a change could have other explanations and several barriers may operate at the same time.



Q3

Difficulty with appointment times (38%), the largest share in the interview sample.



Q4｜解答例

Because evening hours address schedule access but do not solve uncertainty about eligibility or understanding of the invitation.



Japanese Output｜解答例

SS05｜混合資料

本文のcausal caution、FAQの制度条件、表のbarrier patternを統合する。

表の最大値だけで「原因」と断定しない。



A25/A12/RQ03

未知の公衆衛生制度でも本文根拠で処理する。

is consistent with は causal proof より弱い。

This distinction は直前名詞ではなく前段の二種類のbarrierを受ける。



Model answer

The city should first test two evening appointment sessions because schedule difficulty is the most common reported barrier and the FAQ says appointments currently end at 17:00. During the trial, staff should compare booking and attendance among residents who previously said that daytime hours were difficult. They should keep the invitation wording unchanged so that the main change is appointment time. Even if attendance rises, the result should be interpreted cautiously because other barriers and differences between participants may still affect who attends. This would help separate scheduling effects from broader access barriers.



別解：27%（対象か分からない人）を狙い、案内文の表現だけを変える小さなtestも、表とFAQで正当化できれば可。
