E112｜家族介護の支援：正しい英文を自然な情報の流れへ直す

P3 / LT03 / A18 Natural / D08 / TF03



今日の狙い

文法ミスを直すだけでなく、主語・動詞・情報順序を調整して、読み手が自然に追える英語へ直す。日本語の情報順をそのまま英語へ移した答案から、old→new informationの流れを作る。



時間配分

Natural Guide 5分 / Reading 15分 / Questions 7分 / Writing Task 21分 / Revision 6分 / Self-check 3分 / Post-solve 3分 ＝ Core 60分



Natural Guide

Naturalを「難しい表現」にしない。

1. 既知情報を主語や文頭に置く

2. 新情報を文末側へ送る

3. 抽象名詞より明確な動詞を使う

4. 同じ名詞を不自然に反復しない

5. 文法的に正しくても重い直訳は短くする



Reading

[1] Family members often provide care long before they describe themselves as “caregivers.” They may arrange appointments, prepare meals, manage medication, or change work schedules little by little. Because these tasks accumulate gradually, families may not recognize the point at which ordinary help has become a substantial care burden.



[2] This matters for support services. A program advertised only for “caregivers” may fail to reach people who are already doing large amounts of care but do not identify with that label. Services can become easier to find when information begins with recognizable situations—for example, “Are you regularly arranging medical visits for a family member?”—and only then introduces the name of the support program.



[3] Timing also affects usefulness. Advice offered after a family reaches exhaustion may still help, but earlier support can prevent some problems from becoming severe. Short-term respite, information about available services, and help coordinating appointments may reduce pressure before a crisis occurs.



[4] Support should not assume that every family wants the same kind of help. Some people want practical assistance; others mainly need reliable information or a temporary break. The aim is not to replace family involvement but to make the burden more manageable and to give families choices about which responsibilities they can continue safely.



[5] Communication therefore matters as much as the existence of the service itself. If support is described in language that families do not connect with their own situation, it may remain invisible even when technically available. Good information moves from familiar experience to the name of the service, and from the problem the family already recognizes to a practical next step.



Questions

Q1｜RQ08

What is the passage’s main point?



Q2｜D08

Why does the author recommend beginning with a familiar situation before introducing the label “caregiver”?



Writing Task

A student wrote the following paragraph. It is mostly grammatical but unnatural and repetitive. Rewrite it in 100–120 English words.



“There are many families which have care burden without recognition of caregiver. The support information has the problem that the word caregiver is first. Because of this, there are people who do not think the information is for them. It is important to make familiar situations first and after that explain the service. Also, before the family becomes very tired, support should be given. By doing this, the family can have choices and can continue care more safely.”



Your revision must:

- keep the original meaning

- improve subject/verb choices

- create old→new information flow

- reduce repetition

- remain clear rather than decorative



Self-check

□ I changed information structure, not only vocabulary.

□ I removed unnecessary “there is/are”.

□ My subjects connect to the previous sentence.

□ I chose simple verbs when possible.

解答・解説・自己修正



Reading answers

Q1｜解答例

Support is more useful when it is described in language families recognize, offered before burden becomes severe, and matched to different kinds of need.



Q2｜解答例

Because people may already be providing substantial care without identifying themselves as caregivers, so familiar situations help them recognize that the service applies to them.



A18｜Natural

Naturalは「ネイティブっぽい飾り」ではない。

There are many families which have... → Many families provide...

has the problem that... → can fail because...

make familiar situations first → begin with familiar situations

のように、主語と動詞を軽くする。



D08｜old→new information

families → these families / support information → such information

のように既知情報を次文の入口に置き、新情報を後ろへ送る。



Model answer

Many families provide substantial care without thinking of themselves as caregivers. As a result, support information can fail when it begins with a label that people do not connect with their own experience. Services should first describe familiar situations, such as arranging appointments or managing medication, and then explain what support is available. Help should also be offered before families become exhausted, not only after a crisis develops. Earlier, clearer support can give families more choices and make it easier for them to continue the responsibilities they can manage safely. In short, support works best when its wording, timing, and form match what families actually experience.



Self-correction
