Text A｜Library budget
A city must reduce spending while maintaining several neighborhood libraries. Officials first consider allocating the same amount of money to every branch. However, the branches differ in building costs, opening hours, local alternatives, and the number of residents who depend on them for internet access or study space. The city decides to allocate funds according to both basic operating needs and local access. It also assigns several specialist staff to work across multiple branches rather than placing one specialist at every site. During examination season, additional study materials are distributed to branches with unusually high demand. The policy does not remove every difference between neighborhoods, but it uses limited public resources more deliberately and transparently. Officials publish the criteria used for allocation so residents can see why some branches receive more maintenance funding while others receive longer opening hours or additional study materials. Equal spending would be simpler to explain, but it could ignore different building conditions and different barriers to access.


Text B｜Water restriction message
After several dry months, a region introduces temporary water restrictions. The first public notice lists the new rules but does not explain why some uses are restricted before others. Residents complain that the policy seems arbitrary. Officials revise the message to communicate current reservoir levels, convey the risk of continued drought, and explain which activities use the most water. They also express concern about households that may have difficulty reducing essential use. The new message does not make the shortage disappear, but it can improve understanding of the policy and reduce unnecessary conflict. Officials also use text messages, local radio, and printed notices because relying on one communication channel could exclude some residents. They update the wording when reservoir levels change so that earlier advice does not sound more certain than current evidence allows.


Text C｜Customer-service automation
A company introduces an automated chat system to answer routine customer questions. The system reduces the number of simple requests handled by staff, but it performs poorly when a problem involves several unusual details. The company therefore uses automation to complement human agents rather than replace them. A searchable guide supplements the system with updated policy information, while experienced staff compensate for cases that require judgment. Managers also assign agents to review repeated failures and communicate those patterns to the software team. The company hopes this practical arrangement will ease workload without making support less accessible overall. It measures not only how many questions the system answers automatically but also how often customers need to repeat information after being transferred to a human agent. If automation saves staff time but makes complex cases harder to resolve, the company adjusts which requests are handled automatically and which should move directly to a human agent.

## Writing解説

Short Writing
モデル答案
A city should not always allocate exactly the same amount of funding to every library because local needs can differ. It can assign specialist staff across several branches and distribute additional materials where demand is high. The policy should also communicate why the distribution differs so that residents can understand the criteria.


別解
Automation can complement human customer-service staff by handling routine questions. A searchable guide can supplement the system, while people compensate for cases that require judgment. Managers should assign staff to review repeated errors rather than apply the same automated response to every problem.

## 解説補強｜Core recap

### 核
公平は常に「同じ量」ではない。**different need → different allocation → explanation** で考える

### 模範解答の読み方

### 次へ
