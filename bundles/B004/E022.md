Reading
Self-checkout systems are often described as technology that replaces cashiers. They can certainly automate some routine tasks. Customers scan items, select a payment method, and complete a purchase without a worker handling every step. This can reduce the amount of repetitive payment processing required from staff.


However, a checkout job contains more than scanning products. Customers may need help with age-restricted items, coupons, payment errors, damaged barcodes, or unfamiliar machines. A worker may also answer questions, manage queues, or notice situations that require judgment. These tasks do not disappear simply because routine transactions are automated.


The result is often a change in task distribution rather than the complete removal of human work. One employee may supervise several machines instead of operating one register. The technology can complement staff by processing predictable transactions quickly, while workers focus on exceptions and customer support.


Human workers can also compensate for weaknesses in the system. A machine may repeatedly reject an item that has been entered incorrectly in the database, or it may give instructions that a customer does not understand. Staff can identify the underlying problem and decide what to do when the normal procedure fails.


The system can be supplemented in several ways. Clear signs, accessible screen design, and staff positioned nearby can reduce confusion. Stores may also offer both staffed and self-service lanes because customers differ in what they need. Someone buying a few familiar items may value speed, while another customer may need more assistance.


Employment effects therefore depend on how stores reorganize work. A store may need fewer workers processing routine transactions, but it may need more attention to customer support, security, maintenance, or online orders. The number and type of jobs can change without every cashier role simply disappearing. Whether workers benefit also depends on whether they receive training and whether new responsibilities come with reasonable pay and schedules.


For this reason, evaluating self-checkout requires looking at tasks rather than labels. Automation can reduce some forms of work and create demand for others. The useful question is not whether a machine replaces a person in the abstract, but which tasks are automated and which still benefit from human judgment.

解説補強｜automationの評価単位をjobからtaskへ下げる

### この問題で考えること
セルフレジ導入後も必要なhuman workを、job titleではなく**task function**で追います。

### 考え方
routine payment → automated
→ staff time shifts to exception handling / support / security
→ some tasks disappear, others remain or expand
→ employment effect depends on task reorganization and training

### 弱い考え方との比較
× Self-checkout replaces cashiers.
○ Self-checkout can replace routine payment processing while increasing the relative importance of exception handling and customer support.

### 模範解答の読み方
complement / supplement / compensate for は、technologyとhuman workの役割関係をそのまま表しています。語彙選択とsystem designを切り離さないことがポイントです。

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
automationでは **automated task / residual task / new task / transition support** を列挙します。
