Passage A
Public institutions often collect feedback after introducing a new service. A common mistake is to treat the most visible response as the whole evaluation. High satisfaction among current users may show that the service works well for people who already use it, but it may reveal little about those who never gained access.
A stronger evaluation therefore separates use, experience, and reach. Use asks how many people participate. Experience asks what users think after participating. Reach asks whether the service reaches the groups it was intended to serve. These measures can move in different directions. A program may have high satisfaction but low reach, or broad reach but uneven user experience.
The choice of evidence should follow the decision. If an institution is considering expansion, it may need to know whether demand exists beyond current users. If it is trying to improve quality, detailed feedback from existing users may matter more. Evaluation is useful when it reduces the uncertainty attached to the next decision.
Passage B
A university library recently extended weekend opening hours for one month. Current users reported high satisfaction, and total weekend visits increased by 18 percent. However, a short survey of students who still did not use the library found that transport availability and part-time work were more common barriers than opening hours.
The library is now considering whether to add even later weekend hours.
Questions
Q1｜RQ09
What can reasonably be inferred about the proposed later hours from the two passages?
Q2｜RQ10
How confident should the library be that later hours will substantially increase reach among current non-users?
Q3｜RQ12
How does Passage B illustrate the distinction among use, experience, and reach from Passage A?
Summary Task
Summarize Passage A in 55–70 English words.
Writing Task
Write 90–110 English words advising the library on its next step. Your answer must:
- use evidence from Passage B
- apply at least one evaluation principle from Passage A
- avoid merely summarizing the passages
- include one limitation or uncertainty
- propose a next action

Reading answers
Q1｜解答例
Later hours may benefit some existing or potential users, but the evidence suggests that opening time is not the main barrier for many current non-users.
Q2｜解答例
The library should be cautious because the non-user survey identifies transport and part-time work as more common barriers, so later hours alone may not substantially expand reach.
Q3｜解答例
The higher visit count shows use, high satisfaction reflects experience, and the survey of non-users reveals a reach problem that those two measures do not capture.

Model summary
Public-service evaluation should distinguish among use, user experience, and reach because these measures answer different questions and may produce different results. The evidence selected should match the next decision: expansion requires information about demand beyond current users, while quality improvement may rely more on existing-user feedback. Evaluation is most useful when it reduces uncertainty about what to do next.
Model answer
The library should not immediately assume that even later hours will solve its access problem. The pilot increased weekend use and satisfied current users, but non-users more often reported transport and part-time work as barriers. Before extending hours again, the library should test which change would improve reach, perhaps by surveying demand for transport support or different weekend time slots. The current survey is limited, so a short follow-up test would provide stronger evidence before the library commits additional staff and operating costs. This would test access barriers more directly than longer hours alone.
