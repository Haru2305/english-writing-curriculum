Reading
[1] Research participants often contribute time, personal information, or biological samples to studies, yet they may never hear what the study eventually found. Some researchers argue that providing a short results summary recognizes participants’ contribution and can strengthen trust in research.
[2] Returning results, however, is not as simple as sending the final academic paper. A paper may use technical language, and some findings may still be uncertain or difficult to interpret at an individual level. A participant-friendly summary should distinguish between what the study found about a group and what can be concluded about one person.
[3] Timing also matters. Results released too early may change as analysis continues, while results released years later may no longer feel connected to participation. Researchers therefore need a point at which the findings are stable enough to explain but still relevant enough to return.
[4] There are also practical constraints. Contact information may be outdated, translation may be needed, and some participants may prefer not to receive certain kinds of information. A good return-of-results plan should therefore be designed before the study ends, not added as an afterthought.
[5] The broader principle is that communication should match both the evidence and the recipient’s needs. Respecting participants does not require overstating what the data mean. It requires making reliable information understandable, appropriately limited, and available through a process that participants were told about in advance.

Model summary
Returning study results can recognize participants’ contribution and support trust, but communication must be designed carefully. Researchers should explain findings in accessible language, distinguish group-level evidence from individual conclusions, and choose an appropriate time for release. A good plan should also account for practical needs, such as outdated contact details or translation, and for participants’ preferences before the study ends.
Model answer
The city should publish a short, accessible summary after the pilot data have been checked and the main findings are stable. The report should explain what changed during the six months, what remains uncertain, and which conclusions apply only to the tested routes or users. Residents should be able to read the summary online and receive a brief notice about it. The city should also explain how the findings will affect the next transport decision. That would make the report useful for future public decisions.

## 解説補強｜source principleを新しい相手・目的へ写像する

### この問題で考えること
SummaryとTransferは似て見えるが、処理は逆である。

- **Summary**：本文固有の内容を、中心原則へ圧縮する
- **Transfer**：その中心原則を、新しい相手・目的・制約に合わせて再展開する

この問題で移すべき原則は「研究参加者へ結果を返すこと」そのものではなく、**communicationは evidence の強さと recipient の必要に合わせる**という設計原理である。

### 考え方
市のpilotへ移すときは、次の対応を作る。

**Evidence**  
何が観察されたか／何がまだ不確実か

**Recipient**  
住民が次の意思決定を理解するために何を知る必要があるか

**Format / timing**  
専門的な生データではなく、結果が安定した時点で短く理解可能な形にする

**Action link**  
報告して終わりではなく、その結果が次の交通判断にどう使われるかを示す

つまり、

**reliable findings**
→ **scopeとuncertaintyを保つ**
→ **recipientが理解できる形へ変換する**
→ **次のdecisionにつなげる**

というmechanismである。

### 弱い考え方との比較
× *The city should be transparent and share the results with residents.*  
→ 「透明性」は方向として正しいが、何を・どの範囲で・何のために伝えるかがない。

× 研究本文の *participants / academic paper / study* を *residents / report / pilot* に置き換えるだけ  
→ 表面語のtransferであって、論理のtransferになっていない。

○ 「tested routes/usersに限る結果」と「まだ分からないこと」を分け、住民が次の政策判断を理解できる形で示す  
→ evidence limitとrecipient needが新題材で再構成されている。

### 模範解答の読み方
1文目は **Timing + Proposal**：結果が安定した後に短いsummaryを出す。  
2文目は **Content + Qualification**：変化・不確実性・適用範囲を分ける。  
3文目は **Access**：住民が実際に受け取れる経路を置く。  
4文目は **Action link**：結果が次の交通判断にどう影響するかを示す。  
5文目は **Return**：単なる報告ではなく、将来の公共判断に使えるcommunicationだと結論へ戻す。

### 次の問題に持っていくもの
情報提供の問題では、単に「分かりやすく伝える」で止めず、

**evidenceの範囲 × 相手が必要な情報 × 伝える時点 × 次の行動**

の4点を合わせる。研究結果、災害情報、学校からの通知、政策評価、医療説明にも使える。
