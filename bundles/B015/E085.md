Precision Guide
Correctだけでは十分でない。
“X causes Y.” が文法的に正しくても、本文が “X may contribute to Y.” としか言っていないなら意味が強すぎる。
確認するのは、
1. 対象範囲
2. 確信度
3. 条件
4. 例外

Reading
[1] Forecasts about scientific innovation often sound more certain than the evidence allows. A laboratory result can show that a method works under controlled conditions, but it cannot by itself establish how widely the method will be adopted or how strongly it will affect society. Adoption depends on cost, regulation, training, compatibility with existing systems, and whether users see enough benefit to change their behavior.
[2] This does not make forecasting useless. Early evidence can identify plausible directions. If a method repeatedly reduces the time needed for a task, it may be reasonable to expect pressure for some form of adoption. Yet the size and speed of that change remain uncertain. A forecast should therefore separate what has been directly observed from what is inferred about future use.
[3] Precision also requires attention to scope. A study involving one type of hospital, school, or workplace may support a conclusion about that setting without supporting a claim about all institutions. Larger samples can reduce some uncertainty, but a large sample is not automatically representative if the same important group is missing from it.
[4] Good forecasting language makes these limits visible. Phrases such as “the findings suggest,” “under the tested conditions,” and “may be more likely” can sound weaker than dramatic headlines, but they often communicate the evidence more accurately. Hedging is not a way to avoid making a claim. It is a way to make the claim fit the evidence.

Writing Task
A student writes:
“Because the study showed that the new method saves time, organizations will adopt it quickly and it will transform the whole industry.”
Rewrite this in 60–80 English words so that the claim is precise and consistent with the passage. Include:
- the observed finding
- at least one condition or limitation
- an appropriately hedged future claim
Revision Task

“Does the passage really support this level of certainty?”

Reading answers
Q1｜解答例
Because a measured improvement under controlled conditions does not determine whether organizations will adopt the method; adoption also depends on cost, regulation, training, compatibility, and user behavior.
Q2｜解答例
A sample can be large but still fail to represent the wider population if important groups or settings are systematically missing.
Q3｜解答例
The author sees hedging as a way to match the strength and scope of a claim to the evidence, not as a way to avoid making a claim.
WQ03｜Precise
Precise = 難語を使うことではない。
範囲・限定・確信度が意図通りかを見る。
“will transform” → “may encourage adoption”
“the whole industry” → “some organizations or settings”
のように、本文が支える範囲へ戻す。

Model answer
The study shows that the method can save time under the tested conditions, but this result does not prove that organizations will adopt it quickly. Adoption may become more likely if the method remains useful in real settings and if costs, regulation, training, and compatibility do not create major barriers. Even then, broader industry effects would remain uncertain and would require evidence from more organizations and settings.
Self-correction
