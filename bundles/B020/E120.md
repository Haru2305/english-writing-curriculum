Reading
[1] A regional health service is considering a mobile clinic for communities far from major medical centers. Supporters argue that bringing basic services closer to residents can reduce travel barriers, especially for routine checks and follow-up visits.
[2] This advantage is important, but distance is not the only reason people delay care. Some residents may be unable to attend because of work schedules, uncertainty about cost, language barriers, or difficulty arranging continued treatment. A mobile clinic can reduce one barrier while leaving others unchanged.
[3] A pilot in one district reported more first-time consultations after the clinic began visiting twice a month. That result is encouraging, but it should not be generalized too quickly. The district had limited public transport and a large number of residents who had previously reported travel difficulty. A different community may have different barriers.
[4] The pilot also revealed a second issue. Some patients received an initial consultation locally but still needed to travel to a hospital for tests or specialist treatment. If the mobile service increases diagnosis without improving referral coordination, the first barrier may fall while a later barrier becomes more visible.
[5] The relevant evaluation is therefore a chain rather than a single count: who uses the service, whether they receive the needed next step, and whether the overall delay to appropriate care changes. A rise in first visits matters, but it is not by itself evidence that access to complete care has improved.
[6] The strongest case for expansion would combine two findings: the mobile clinic reaches people who previously faced a travel barrier, and the referral system allows those people to complete necessary follow-up. If only the first condition is met, the service may still be useful, but its effect should be described more narrowly.

Reading answers
Q1｜解答例
The passage concedes that bringing basic services closer can reduce travel barriers and increase first consultations, while limiting the claim that this alone improves access to complete care.
Q2｜解答例
Mobile services can improve initial access when travel is an important barrier, but their broader value depends on the barriers in the local population and on what happens after the first visit.
Q3｜解答例
Reduced travel barrier → more appropriate first consultations → successful referral / follow-up → shorter delay to appropriate care.
Q4｜解答例
The doctor treats earlier identification as meaningful even if later barriers remain, while the planner requires evidence that patients can complete subsequent steps without a new bottleneck.

Model summary
A mobile clinic can reduce travel barriers and increase first consultations, but improved initial access does not guarantee access to complete care. The pilot’s result is most relevant where travel is a major barrier, and evaluation should also track referral and follow-up. Expansion is strongest when new users receive both earlier contact and the later services they need.
Model answer
The health service should expand the pilot cautiously to communities where travel is a documented barrier. The existing pilot shows that bringing care closer can increase first consultations, and that benefit is important. However, the result should not be generalized to every community, because other barriers may dominate elsewhere. Expansion should also depend on referral performance: if more first visits lead to completed tests and specialist follow-up with shorter delays, the service is improving access to complete care. The next phase should therefore combine mobile visits with referral coordination and measure both entry and completion.
