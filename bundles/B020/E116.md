E116｜野生動物保護の事例：一つの成功例から何を一般化できるか

P3 / LT01 / D09 example→generalization / RQ08 / TF08



今日の狙い

具体例を読んで終わらず、その例がどの一般命題を支え、どこまでは支えないかを抽象化する。印象的な成功例から過剰一般化しない。



時間配分

Generalization Guide 6分 / Reading 20分 / Questions 12分 / Generalization Map 8分 / Short Output 6分 / Self-check 3分 / Post-solve 5分 ＝ Core 60分



Generalization Guide

例を読んだら3段階で戻す。

1. What happened?

2. What principle does it illustrate?

3. What would be too broad a conclusion?

成功例は「常に有効」の証明ではない。



Reading

[1] Conservation programs often become easier to discuss when they are attached to a visible success story. A wetland is restored, a bird population increases, or fishing restrictions are followed by the return of a local species. Such cases can show that intervention matters, but the lesson drawn from them depends on which parts of the case are treated as essential.



[2] Consider a coastal area in which nesting birds had declined after visitors began using a narrow section of beach throughout the breeding season. Local managers did not close the entire coast. Instead, they marked a small nesting zone, moved a walking route several meters inland, and explained the change to visitors. Over the next few seasons, nesting success improved while most recreational access remained open.



[3] One possible lesson is that strict exclusion is unnecessary in conservation. That conclusion would be too broad. The intervention worked partly because the disturbance was concentrated in a small area, alternative walking space existed, and the protected period was limited. In a different habitat, human activity might be spread widely or alternative access might be impossible.



[4] A stronger generalization is that conservation can sometimes reduce conflict when the specific mechanism of harm is identified precisely. If the main problem is disturbance at a particular place and time, a targeted restriction may protect the ecological function while preserving other uses. The principle is not “small restrictions always work,” but “interventions should match the mechanism and spatial scale of the harm.”



[5] The same reasoning applies when a program fails. A failed targeted restriction does not automatically show that targeted conservation is useless. It may show that the assumed mechanism was wrong, that the protected area was too small, or that an important source of disturbance was ignored. Cases become informative when they are used to refine a general model rather than treated as slogans.



[6] Examples are therefore most valuable when readers move in both directions: from principle to case and from case back to a qualified principle. A conservation story can make an abstract idea concrete, but its broader value depends on identifying which conditions travel to other settings and which remain local.



Questions

Q1｜D09

What broader principle does the coastal bird example support?



Q2｜RQ09

Why would “small restrictions always work” be an overgeneralization?



Q3｜RQ08

Q4｜D09

Short Output

In 55–75 English words, state one justified generalization from the example and one conclusion that would be too broad.



Self-check

解答・解説・自己修正



Reading answers

Q1｜解答例

Targeted conservation can reduce conflict when an intervention is matched closely to the mechanism, place, and timing of the harm.



Q2｜解答例

Because the example depended on local conditions such as concentrated disturbance, available alternative space, and a limited protected period.



Q3｜解答例

Examples support useful general understanding only when readers identify which conditions can be generalized and avoid turning one success or failure into a universal rule.



Q4｜解答例

A failed case can reveal that the assumed mechanism, scale, or relevant conditions were wrong or incomplete, helping refine the general model.



Short Output｜例
A justified generalization is that conservation can reduce conflict when an intervention is matched to the specific mechanism, place, and timing of the harm. It would be too broad to conclude that small restrictions always work or that strict exclusion is never needed, because the bird example depended on concentrated disturbance, alternative walking space, and a limited protected period.
