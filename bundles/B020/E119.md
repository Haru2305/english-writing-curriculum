Source A

[1] Online platforms sometimes attach context labels to posts that contain disputed or incomplete information. The label may link to an explanation, identify missing context, or warn that a claim is under review. Supporters argue that such labels can slow impulsive sharing by introducing a moment of doubt.



[2] The effect, however, is likely to depend on how the label is interpreted. Some users may treat it as useful guidance, while others may see it as evidence that the platform is taking sides. A label can also attract additional attention to a post that would otherwise have been ignored.



[3] For this reason, the success of a label should not be measured only by whether users notice it. More relevant outcomes include whether readers open the linked information, whether confidence in an unsupported claim changes, and whether sharing behavior differs. Even these measures need caution because users who interact with labels may differ from those who do not.



[4] Labels can still be worthwhile. They are relatively easy to apply and can provide immediate context while slower review processes continue. The stronger claim is not that labels “solve misinformation,” but that they may improve some decisions when their wording, placement, and supporting information are designed carefully.



Source B

Platform designer:

“A short label is one of the few interventions that can reach users at the moment they see a questionable claim. Even a small reduction in immediate sharing could matter at scale.”



Media researcher:

“Attention is not the same as correction. If a label makes a post more memorable or is trusted only by people who already doubt the claim, the apparent intervention may have little effect on the audience of greatest concern.”

Reading answers

Q1｜解答例

Both accept that labels can affect what happens at the moment users encounter a claim. The designer emphasizes possible reductions in immediate sharing, while the researcher questions whether attention produces correction or reaches the users most likely to believe the claim.



Q2｜解答例

Source A concedes that labels are easy to apply, can provide immediate context, and may improve some decisions, while rejecting the stronger claim that they solve misinformation.

Model answer

The platform should continue the label pilot, but only as a limited intervention. Labels can provide context at the moment users see a questionable claim, and the table suggests that sharing decreased after labeled exposure. However, Source B warns that attention does not necessarily mean correction and that labels may mainly influence users who already doubt the post. The platform should therefore track whether confidence changes among initially trusting users, not only whether people notice the label. The pilot supports continued testing, not the conclusion that labels solve misinformation. That distinction should guide expansion.
