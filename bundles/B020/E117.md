Causal Chain Guide

Reading

[1] Electricity systems must balance supply and demand continuously. Discussions of energy policy often focus on how much electricity a technology can generate, but the timing of demand can be equally important. A system may have enough total capacity over a day and still face short periods when demand rises faster than available supply.



[2] One reason is that people respond to the same conditions at similar times. On a very hot evening, many households may return home and begin using air conditioning, cooking equipment, and other appliances within a narrow period. The difficulty is not simply that total daily demand is high; it is that a large share is concentrated.



[3] Utilities sometimes respond with time-based prices or voluntary demand programs. If some users shift flexible activities—such as charging a device or running certain equipment—to a later hour, the peak can become lower even when total energy use changes little. This can reduce the need to activate expensive backup generation for a short period.



[4] The effect is not automatic. A price signal only matters if users notice it, have something they can shift, and consider the change worthwhile. Households with little flexibility may be unable to respond. In that case, a program that appears efficient on average may distribute inconvenience unevenly.



[5] Lowering the peak can also affect investment decisions. If extreme demand occurs less often, the system may require less capacity that sits unused during most hours. However, planners cannot assume that a short pilot will permanently change behavior, and a reduction in one location may not appear elsewhere.



[6] Demand management is therefore not a substitute for generation or grid investment. It is one tool that can change when some electricity is used, which may reduce pressure at critical times. Its value depends on the behavioral response and on whether the program shifts rather than merely hides costs.

Reading answers

Q1｜解答例

Because reducing demand during the short peak can prevent the system from needing expensive backup generation even if the same energy is used later.



Q2｜解答例

Users must notice the signal, have flexible demand they can shift, and see enough benefit in changing when they use electricity.



Q3｜解答例

Behavior during a short pilot may not persist, and a reduction observed in one place may not generalize to other locations or conditions.



D04/RQ05｜因果連鎖

peak concentration → shiftable use moves → peak falls → backup use falls

という途中段階を飛ばさない。

price signal → lower peak と直接つなげるとmechanismが抜ける。



WF03｜原因・結果

Model answer

Demand management can reduce backup generation when users move flexible electricity use away from a short period of peak demand. If enough households shift activities to later hours, total daily consumption may remain similar while the maximum load falls. The system then has less need to activate expensive backup capacity for only a brief period. However, this chain weakens when users do not notice the price signal or cannot change their schedule. It may also place more inconvenience on households with less flexibility, so efficiency and distribution should be evaluated separately.



Self-correction

because / therefore を増やすだけで因果説明にしない。各文が前文の何を原因として受けているか確認する。
