E115｜学習支援の公平性：反対要素を認めても主張は変わるのか

P3 / LT02 / D05 concession→main claim / RQ06 / TF04



今日の狙い

譲歩部分を筆者の最終主張と取り違えず、「何を認めた上で、結局何を言っているか」を読む。although / while / admittedly / however が複数回出ても、中心主張の向きを保持する。



時間配分

Concession Guide 6分 / Reading 19分 / Questions 13分 / Claim Map 8分 / Short Output 6分 / Self-check 3分 / Post-solve 5分 ＝ Core 60分



Concession Guide

1. 筆者が相手側に認めている点を取る

2. その譲歩の後に何を残すかを見る

3. “both sides have a point”で止めない

4. however / still / yet / nevertheless の後ろを機械的に主張とせず、段落全体で確認する



Reading

[1] Schools that provide additional support to some students are sometimes criticized for treating students unequally. If one student receives extra tutoring time, a quieter testing room, or access to a device that others do not receive, the visible treatment is different. From that observation, it can seem natural to conclude that fairness requires identical provision.



[2] This concern should not be dismissed. Rules that allocate extra support can be poorly designed, inconsistently applied, or influenced by assumptions about who is “deserving.” A support system that cannot explain its criteria may create distrust even when its intention is reasonable.



[3] Yet identical treatment does not always create equal opportunity. A student who cannot access a digital assignment at home is not placed in the same practical position as a student with reliable equipment simply because both are given the same deadline. Likewise, a student who understands the material but cannot use a standard testing format may be assessed partly on an unrelated barrier.



[4] The relevant question is therefore not whether every student receives the same resource, but whether differences in support are tied to barriers that affect access to the intended learning task. This does not mean that every disadvantage must be compensated or that all requests should be accepted. It means that the justification for different treatment should be connected to the educational goal rather than to convenience, status, or preference.



[5] Targeted support can also produce secondary effects. If a program is visible only to students who already know how to ask for help, it may widen rather than narrow a gap. For this reason, fair allocation requires attention not only to who receives support but also to who can discover, request, and use it.



[6] A defensible system therefore combines two ideas that can appear to conflict: common standards and differentiated access support. The standard identifies the learning goal; the support reduces barriers that are not themselves part of that goal. Fairness lies not in making every path identical, but in being able to explain why a difference in treatment is relevant to the opportunity being protected.

解答・解説・自己修正



Reading answers

Q1｜解答例

The author concedes that targeted support can be unfair or distrusted when allocation rules are poorly designed, inconsistently applied, or difficult to justify.



Q2｜解答例

The author still defends differentiated support when it addresses barriers that interfere with the intended learning opportunity and when the criteria can be explained.



Q3｜解答例

Because access to the support itself may depend on knowing that it exists or knowing how to request it, so students already better connected to resources may benefit more.



Q4｜C



Short Output｜例
Same treatment gives every student identical resources, even when students face different barriers. Fair access asks whether differences in support are tied to barriers that affect the intended learning goal, and whether students can discover and use that support. Common standards remain, but the reason for different treatment must be transparent and relevant to the opportunity being protected.


D05｜concession→main claim

譲歩は主張の放棄ではない。

admittedly X / concern X / risk X

を認めた後に、

still Y / relevant question is Y / therefore Y

として中心命題を限定し直す。
