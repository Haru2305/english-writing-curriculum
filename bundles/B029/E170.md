E170｜都市の熱対策：高密度文と表から平均値・ピーク・公平性を読む
P3 / LT03 / A29 / A28 / D06 / RQ09 / RQ13 / RF04 / SS03 / TF09
今日の狙い
都市の暑さ対策を平均気温だけで評価せず、ピーク時の曝露と地域差を高密度文・表から統合する。
時間配分
Silent Plan 1分 / Reading 17分 / Dense Sentence 13分 / Table Questions 11分 / Japanese Output 10分 / Self-check 3分 / Post-solve 5分 ＝ Core 60分
Reading
[1] A city is comparing several heat-reduction measures: street trees, reflective roofs, shaded bus stops, and cooling centers. Each measure can lower heat exposure, but not necessarily in the same place, at the same time, or for the same people.
[2] Average temperature is one useful indicator. Yet a small reduction spread across an entire day may matter differently from a larger reduction during the hottest two hours. Similarly, a citywide average can hide neighborhoods where residents have little shade or limited access to cooled indoor space.
Dense Sentence
What makes the evaluation of urban heat policy difficult is that a measure capable of producing a modest average temperature reduction across a wide area may provide less protection to people facing short periods of extreme exposure than a more localized intervention whose citywide average effect appears smaller but whose benefits are concentrated where heat risk and outdoor dependence are greatest.
[3] The city evaluates four neighborhoods after different interventions.
Table
Neighborhood | Average daytime reduction | Peak-hour reduction | Residents without home cooling | Main intervention
A | 1.2°C | 1.5°C | 8% | Reflective roofs
B | 0.8°C | 2.4°C | 27% | Street shade
C | 1.0°C | 1.2°C | 15% | Mixed measures
D | 0.5°C | 2.7°C | 31% | Shaded transit + cooling center
[4] The table does not establish that Neighborhood D has the “best” policy. Its intervention may be responding to greater underlying vulnerability, and the measures are not directly interchangeable. However, the table shows why average temperature alone cannot answer every policy question.
[5] If the policy goal is to lower citywide heat broadly, average reduction matters. If the goal is to reduce dangerous peak exposure among residents with fewer private cooling options, peak-hour effects and who receives them become more important.
[6] The broader principle is that evaluation metrics should match the purpose of the policy. A single summary number can be useful without being sufficient.

解答・解説・自己修正
Q1｜解答例
Some policies reduce average temperature across a wide area. That does not necessarily give the greatest protection during extreme heat. A localized measure may have a smaller citywide average effect. Yet it may strongly benefit people who face the highest peak exposure and have fewer alternatives.
Q2｜和訳例
都市の暑さ対策を評価しにくいのは、広い範囲で平均気温をやや下げる施策が、短時間の極端な暑さにさらされる人々を十分に守るとは限らず、逆に市全体で見た平均効果は小さい局所的施策でも、暑熱リスクが高く屋外活動への依存が大きい場所に便益を集中できる場合があるためである。
Q3｜解答
Neighborhood D.
Q4｜解答例
One goal is broad citywide temperature reduction; the other is reducing dangerous peak exposure among more vulnerable residents.
Q5｜解答例
Its intervention appears to produce a strong peak-hour reduction in an area with many residents lacking home cooling, but the table alone cannot show that the intervention is superior overall.
