Notice｜Heavy Snow Service Plan
During severe snowfall, the regional transit operator may reduce service before tracks or roads become fully unusable. The goal is to maintain a smaller, more reliable network rather than publish a normal timetable that cannot be operated.
Possible measures:
• lower train or bus frequency
• temporary suspension on exposed sections
• earlier final departures
• cancellation of some through-services so vehicles can remain within shorter operating zones
FAQ
Q: If my line is running, will my usual connection operate?
A: Not necessarily. Through-services and connecting routes may be reduced separately.
Q: Why reduce service before conditions become impossible?
A: Lower frequency creates more time for snow clearing, vehicle recovery, and delay absorption.
Q: Does a “30-minute interval” mean exactly every 30 minutes?
A: It is a target under disruption; further delay may occur.
Q: When will normal service return?
A: Only after route condition, vehicle position, and staff availability are reassessed.

解答・解説・自己修正
Q1｜解答例
Because through-services and connecting routes can be reduced independently, so one segment may operate while the full journey cannot be completed as usual.
Q2｜解答例
The interval is only a disruption target; actual delays may increase if snow clearing, vehicle movement, or staff conditions worsen.
Q3｜解答例
At least part of Route D is expected to remain unavailable under the snow plan, and passengers may need alternative travel for the outer section.
Q4｜解答例
Lower frequency creates operational slack for snow clearing, recovery from delay, and repositioning vehicles, which can make the reduced service more dependable than an unrealistic normal timetable.
Model Japanese summary
